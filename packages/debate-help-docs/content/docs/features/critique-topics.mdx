# Critique Questions: Belief Resolutions

These topics frame your questions as affirmative-or-negative resolutions about what we ought to believe, value, or regard as legitimate—not as proposals requiring a specific government policy. Each preserves a philosophical clash while giving both sides a clear proposition to defend.

## Power, Freedom, and Emancipation

### 1. Power and Resistance
Resolved: Liberation requires transforming power relations rather than escaping them.

- Affirmative: Freedom consists in making power contestable, reversible, and less dominating.
- Negative: Transforming power cannot establish liberation without an independent standard for distinguishing freedom from domination.

### 2. The Foundations of Critique
Resolved: Critiques of oppression must defend an account of justice.

- Affirmative: Condemning oppression presupposes a defensible distinction between legitimate and illegitimate power.
- Negative: Contradictions, exclusions, and suffering can justify resistance without a comprehensive theory of justice.

### 3. Consent and Socially Produced Desires
Resolved: Consent cannot legitimate institutions that systematically manufacture the preferences through which people accept them.

- Affirmative: Institutions cannot independently justify themselves through desires they have produced.
- Negative: Socially shaped preferences can still express agency when people can reflect, dissent, and revise their commitments.

### 4. Emancipation and New Domination
Resolved: Emancipatory movements should prioritize preventing domination over realizing a shared vision of liberation.

- Affirmative: Protecting dissent and limiting arbitrary authority prevent liberation from becoming another compulsory ideal.
- Negative: Without a substantive vision of liberation, opposition to domination cannot adequately guide collective transformation.

## The State, Sovereignty, and Political Authority

### 5. Sovereignty and Liberation
Resolved: Sovereign authority is incompatible with emancipation.

- Affirmative: The ultimate authority to impose decisions preserves subjection, even when exercised in liberation’s name.
- Negative: Collective authority can restrain private domination and establish the conditions for meaningful self-government.

### 6. Authority and the Right of Refusal
Resolved: Political authority is legitimate only when those subject to it possess a meaningful right of refusal.

- Affirmative: Participation cannot substitute for consent when people cannot reject the authority governing them.
- Negative: Equal participation, accountability, and protection against domination can legitimate binding authority without individual opt-out rights.

### 7. Political Membership and Exclusion
Resolved: Political community does not require the exclusion of an outsider.

- Affirmative: Shared political life can rest on open, overlapping, and revisable forms of membership.
- Negative: Every political community must distinguish those entitled to make binding decisions from those who are not.

### 8. The Authority to Declare an Emergency
Resolved: No political authority may legitimately determine the exceptions to its own constraints.

- Affirmative: The authority to suspend limits makes those limits conditional on the will of the constrained institution.
- Negative: Legitimate government requires some discretion to recognize emergencies that ordinary rules cannot adequately address.

## Capitalism, Ideology, and Transformation

### 9. Reform and Structural Transformation
Resolved: Reforms within capitalism can constitute emancipation from capitalist domination.

- Affirmative: Changes to ownership, dependence, and collective power can transform the relations that sustain capitalist domination.
- Negative: Reforms preserve capitalism’s organizing relations while making those relations more durable and legitimate.

### 10. Judging Revolutionary Change
Resolved: Revolutionary transformation need not be justified by the standards of the existing social order.

- Affirmative: Existing standards may encode the very domination that revolutionary change seeks to overcome.
- Negative: Rejecting inherited standards does not remove the obligation to justify coercion, foreseeable harms, and claims to political authority.

### 11. Participation and Complicity
Resolved: Unavoidable participation in an unjust system does not eliminate responsibility for its reproduction.

- Affirmative: Inability to withdraw does not erase obligations to resist, mitigate, or redirect one’s contribution.
- Negative: Responsibility requires meaningful control; unavoidable participation alone cannot establish culpability or a duty to resist.

### 12. Resistance and Systemic Reproduction
Resolved: Resistance within a system’s authorized political channels can overcome its underlying relations of domination.

- Affirmative: Institutional opposition can accumulate collective power and create possibilities that exceed the system’s original limits.
- Negative: Authorized opposition channels conflict into forms that reproduce the order they appear to challenge.

## Security, Desecuritization, and Survival

### 13. Material Danger and Threat Construction
Resolved: Security threats exist independently of the political practices through which they are identified.

- Affirmative: Material dangers can exist regardless of how political actors describe them.
- Negative: A danger becomes a security threat only through judgments about whose survival matters, what must be protected, and which responses are permissible.

### 14. Security and Emancipation
Resolved: Security should be judged by the freedom it enables rather than the order it preserves.

- Affirmative: Stability cannot justify security practices that reproduce oppression.
- Negative: Security must preserve the conditions of political life rather than enforce a contested conception of emancipation.

### 15. Desecuritization and Political Freedom
Resolved: Desecuritization should be the default aim of emancipatory politics.

- Affirmative: Moving issues out of emergency frameworks makes disagreement possible without treating opponents as existential threats.
- Negative: Some forms of violence and domination require urgent security claims; ordinary politics may normalize rather than remedy them.

### 16. Catastrophe and Uncertainty
Resolved: The possibility of catastrophe is insufficient to justify coercion when its probability cannot be reliably assessed.

- Affirmative: Speculative catastrophe gives authorities an expansive justification for imposing concrete harms.
- Negative: An inability to calculate probability does not remove the obligation to prevent potentially irreversible destruction.

## Knowledge, Identity, and the Obligations of Critique

### 17. Objectivity and Power
Resolved: The social production of knowledge does not undermine the possibility of objectivity.

- Affirmative: Situated knowledge can support reliable judgments through evidence, criticism, and revision.
- Negative: Standards of evidence and reliability can reproduce the exclusions that claims of objectivity conceal.

### 18. Identity Politics and Collective Liberation
Resolved: Emancipatory politics should organize primarily around shared structures of domination rather than shared identities.

- Affirmative: Structural organizing can build solidarity without treating identity groups as internally uniform or politically fixed.
- Negative: Identity-based organizing makes distinctive forms of oppression visible and enables collective agency that broader frameworks may suppress.

### 19. Racism and Universalism
Resolved: Overcoming racism requires a race-conscious conception of justice.

- Affirmative: Racial domination cannot be dismantled through principles that abstract away from its histories, institutions, and unequal effects.
- Negative: Making race constitutive of justice risks reproducing racial categories and compromising equal moral standing.

### 20. Critique and Practical Responsibility
Resolved: Emancipatory critique must identify a meaningful possibility for action.

- Affirmative: Critique owes those experiencing oppression more than an explanation of why their circumstances are unjust.
- Negative: Requiring an actionable alternative makes criticism answerable to the limits of the order it seeks to challenge.
