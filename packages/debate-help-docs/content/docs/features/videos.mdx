## Native Wrapper [#native-wrapper]

==A native desktop (Windows/macOS/Linux) and mobile (Android/iOS) app== that
==opens debate-ai.com in its own window instead of a browser tab==, with a
Google-OAuth-compatible sign-in flow ==despite Google blocking OAuth inside
embedded webviews==. Built with [Tauri](https://tauri.app).

### What it shows [#native-wrapper-what-it-shows]

==Functionally identical to the website== — the app's window loads
debate-ai.com directly. ==The one difference is sign-in==: inside
the wrapper, a "Continue in your browser" button replaces the normal
social/magic-link buttons, ==since OAuth providers refuse to run inside an
embedded webview==. ==Once you finish signing in in your browser, it hands
you back to the app, signed in==.

### Running in the background (desktop) [#native-wrapper-running-in-the-background-desktop]

On Windows, macOS and Linux ==the app starts with your computer and waits in
the background==, so ==opening it is instant: the page has already loaded==.

- ==At login it starts hidden==. There is no window, and on macOS no Dock
  icon. ==The only sign of it is the Debate AI icon== in the menu bar (macOS),
  the notification area (Windows) or the status area (Linux).
- ==Click that icon to open or hide Debate AI==. You can also press
  `Ctrl+`` (`Cmd+`` on a Mac) from any app. ==It brings Debate AI up with
  whatever text you had selected==.
- ==Closing the window hides it==. ==The app keeps running until you pick==
  **Quit Debate AI** from the icon's menu.
- ==Right-click the icon for its menu==: **Open Debate AI**, **Hide to
  Background**, **Run on Startup** (a check box: untick it to stop the app
  starting with your computer), **Settings…**, **Load From** and **Quit**.

### Updates (desktop) [#native-wrapper-updates-desktop]

==The desktop app keeps itself up to date==. It ==checks for a new version when it
starts and once a day after that==, and downloads it in the background.

- ==It installs only while the app is out of sight in the background==, so ==it
  never closes a window you are using==. If the window is open, the update
  waits until you close or hide it.
- After installing, ==Debate AI restarts in the background, as it was==. Open it
  from its icon as usual.
- ==Every update is signed by Debate AI, and the app refuses one that isn't==.

### Camera and microphone [#native-wrapper-camera-and-microphone]

==The webcam room and the speech recorder work in the app==. The first time a
page turns the camera or microphone on, ==the operating system asks once==
(macOS shows "Debate AI would like to access the camera"); the app itself
does not ask again for its own pages. If the camera still won't turn on,
==check the OS privacy settings for Debate AI==.

### Good to know [#native-wrapper-good-to-know]

- Auto-update covers versions released after it was turned on; ==an older
  desktop version has to be replaced by hand once==.
- ==The Android and iOS apps aren't ready yet==, and ==they aren't in any app store==.

## Video Library [#video-library]

The debate video library holds rounds, lectures and top picks, and ==loads one
page at a time as you scroll==, so the first videos show up right away.

Find it at **/videos**, with a page for each category such as **/videos/pf**.

### Season and tournament addresses [#video-library-season-and-tournament-addresses]

==A video's address starts with its season, format and tournament==
(`/videos/21-22/cpd/ndt/finals/…`). ==The format is the division's short name==:
`vcx` (high school policy), `cpd` (college policy), `pf`, `ld`, `wsd`, `acc`
and `misc`; lectures have none. Links from before the format was added
(`/videos/21-22/ndt/finals/…`) ==still work and forward to the new address==. ==The season is written as the two years it
spans==, because ==a season runs from summer to summer==: `21-22` is the season that
ends in 2022. ==A publish date from July onward counts toward the next season==.
Older links spelled the season as the year it ended (`/videos/2022/…`); they
still work and forward to the new address.

==An address can be trimmed==: `/videos/24-25` ==opens the library filtered to that
season==, `/videos/24-25/ld` ==filtered to that season and format==, and `/videos/24-25/ndt` ==filtered to that season with the tournament's
name as the search==. The bare-year forms
(`/videos/2025`) still work too. `/videos/archive/…` ==opens the undated videos==.
`/videos/24-25/ld/<tournament>` looks like a video's address, so ==it opens the
filtered library only when no video has that address==

### Lecture categories [#video-library-lecture-categories]

==The sidebar has a single== **Lectures** row under **Videos**. Opening it
(`/lectures`) ==shows the lecture categories as a bar of cards at the top of the
page, on every screen size==: **All Lectures** first, ==then each category with
its icon and video count==. A card's background highlights on hover, the open
category is drawn selected, and ==clicking a card switches the list to that
category== (`/lectures/<category>`). The categories used to be a second,
expandable section of the sidebar; ==they now live only on the page==.

### WSD, ACC and Speech [#video-library-wsd-acc-and-speech]

World Schools (**WSD**), **ACC** and **Speech** ==are not sidebar links==. They
==show only on the All Videos page== (`/videos`), ==as a row of cards directly above
the search box==, each opening its collection (`/videos/wsd`, `/videos/acc`,
`/videos/speech`). The sidebar, the phone's quick-link tiles and the dock's
menu ==leave them out==.

A lecture whose title says WSD or World Schools ==is filed under WSD==, and one
whose title says ACC or American Congressional ==under ACC==. ==The abbreviations
match as whole words==, so "accent" is not ACC, and ==rounds keep their own format==.

Rounds from the **ACC Debate Tournament** ==are the one exception==: any video
whose title says "ACC Debate Tournament" ==also shows under ACC, while keeping
its format==. ==Because those rounds are college rounds==,
**College Team Debate** (`/videos/college`) ==shows an ACC card above its
search box too==, and ==the ACC card's count
includes them==.

==ACC is listed as debate rounds, not lectures==: `/videos/acc` ==shows the round
rows==, with the **Aff** and **Neg** teams side by side, grouped by season and
tournament. ==ACC has no card in the lecture category bar==, and the old
`/lectures/acc` address ==redirects to `/videos/acc`==.

### Merged lecture categories [#video-library-merged-lecture-categories]

==Topic analysis and topic lectures share one shelf==, **Topic Lectures**. Videos
still stored under the old "Topic Analysis" label ==are folded into it when the
library is shown==, and old `/lectures/topic_analysis`
==links open the merged shelf==.

### Sidebar format badges and the Greatest of All-Time banner [#video-library-sidebar-format-badges-and-the-greatest-of-all-time-banner]

In the sidebar, College Policy, HS Policy, HS Policy 1v1 (LD) and Public Forum ==show lettered badges== (NDT,
VP, PF, LD) ==instead of generic icons==. The Greatest of All-Time collection
(`/videos/topPicks`) ==opens on an== **All Time Greatest Legends** banner — ==the gold trophy-goat icon
over the title==, which is ==drawn in dots that grow in and then shimmer== (still
dotted, without the animation, if you've asked your device to reduce motion) — with
==drifting sparkles above the search bar==. ==Hover over or click the trophy== and it
plays a random effect each time — a spin, bounce, wobble, flip, golden glow, rainbow
or a confetti burst, never the same one twice in a row (off when reduce motion is on).
Under the trophy, ==a ticker scrolls the 20 best college teams of all time==, each
card showing its rank, school and last names, with ==the team's last NDT year as a
short badge in the corner== (for example ’05).

### Category search suggestions [#video-library-category-search-suggestions]

==The search chips beneath the grid are specific to the category, debate format,
season, or top-picks view currently open==. Their counts come from the titles and
metadata of videos in that selection, so ==choosing a chip never sends you to
unrelated lectures or rounds from elsewhere in the library==.

### Live rounds [#video-library-live-rounds]

==Rounds being live streamed right now show in a **Live rounds** row under the
search chips==, with the speech on air; ==replays of earlier live rounds sit in
the same row once they end==. Pick a card to open its radio player under the
row, or the corner link for the round's own page. The row is hidden while
searching, on My Favorites and History, and when there are no streams. See
[Live Stream (Round Radio)](/docs/features/rounds#live-stream).

### Compact search bar on scroll [#video-library-compact-search-bar-on-scroll]

As you scroll down the grid or row view, the search row (search box, season
dropdown, sort and layout buttons, **+** and the account badge) stays pinned to
the top of the window as a compact bar with a blurred background, so you can
change filters without scrolling back up. The search suggestions underneath
scroll away with the page. Scroll back to the top and the bar settles back
into the page. With reduce motion on, it switches without the animation.

### Loading as you scroll [#video-library-loading-as-you-scroll]

==Loading is automatic and has no ceiling==: ==the grid asks for the next page as
you scroll==, and the row (list) view ==keeps paging on its own, one page per idle
moment, until every group is complete==. ==To keep a long feed from freezing the
page==, ==only the cards near the screen are drawn==; the rest are filled in as
you scroll to them.

### Favorites and hidden videos [#video-library-favorites-and-hidden-videos]

==Favorites and hidden videos are kept on your account and follow you to
another device==. ==A hidden video also drops out of the season and format
counts==.

My Favorites (`/videos/favorites`) ==lists starred rounds and lectures together==,
==in separate Rounds and Lectures tables==. Only `/videos/favoritelectures`
==narrows favorites to lectures==. ==No other library filter applies there==:
format, season, category, search and hidden-video filters are ignored, ==so
every starred video is listed==.

==Signed out, they save in that browser alone==, and ==favouriting a video says so
once== — ==with an offer to sign in and keep the collection==. ==Declining is fine==: ==the
favourite is still saved==, and signing in later ==pushes everything that browser
already holds up to the account rather than replacing it==.

### Stacked playlists [#video-library-stacked-playlists]

==Some videos are the same debate twice==: a round, and the round-analysis video
made from it. They used to sit pages apart in the grid — and in a rounds
view the analysis was filtered out altogether — so ==the pair was there and
invisible==. ==They now share one card== (or one row, in the list layout) with
`<` / `>` arrows and a `1/2` counter ==to flip between them==. ==The full round is
what the slot opens on==, ==unless you arrived at the companion==, in which case it
opens on that.

The same applies to ==a round split across uploads== (a decision and a rebuttals
cut) and to ==the parts of a lecture series==.

==Uploads of the same round stack too==: the same round posted by two channels,
a round cut into "Part 1" and "Part 2", or ==one video per speech== (1AC and
1NC, 2AC, 2NC, 1NR, 2NR, 2AR, then the decision). ==They play in speaking
order==, the whole round first, and ==each is labelled with the part it covers==
("Part 2", "2NR", "Decision"). ==The label is also a menu==: pick any speech
from it to jump straight there. ==Titles that glue the speech onto a team==
("Kansas KQ vs Northwestern FS 2AR", even "2Kansas KQ"), camera filenames
("Harvard JP v Emory IW-2NR.MP4") and ==re-uploads posted years later still
stack== with the round they came from. ==Team and school pages stack their
video lists the same way==.
==A round cut into parts always shows as one stack==, Part 1 on top, even
before the library has re-stacked new uploads, and ==a team written as the
debaters' names== ("Emory Gallagher & Song") ==stacks with the same team written
as its code== ("Emory GS").

==Nothing about this is hand-curated==. ==Two videos are the same round when
they list the same two teams, the same round and the same tournament in the
same season==. ==Other videos stack because one video's YouTube description
links to the other== ("Full Debate: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=…`"), so
==linking two videos is an edit to a description on YouTube, not a change to
the site==.

==A round's card never gives away who won until you ask==: ==both teams show in
their side colors==, and where the decision would be there is a **Reveal
decision** button. ==Clicking it shows the decision and rings the winning team
in gold==, here and on the video's watch page, ==which shares the reveal== (see
[Video Watch Page](/docs/features/videos#video-watch-page-no-spoilers)).

Every card in the grid and every row in the list ==shows the video's length==
(`1:05`, `42m`) ==on its own line under the action buttons== — not as a column —
and ==flipping a stack shows the length of the video now on top==. ==A video
whose length hasn't been looked up yet simply shows none== (see Video durations
below).

The **Stacked playlists** button in the search panel (the layers icon) turns
it off, ==giving one card per video again==.

On a video's own page ==the stack is spelled out as a== **Playlist** under the
player — the round, its analysis, each part — ==with the one you are watching
marked==, so ==the analysis of a round is one click away==.

==A video made from a round lives one level below the round's address==: the
2024-25 Shirley final is at `/videos/24-25/cpd/shirley/finals/emory-gs-kansas-ls`,
and a second part of it at `/videos/24-25/cpd/shirley/finals/emory-gs-kansas-ls/part-2`.
Links shared under the older addresses (`…/dartmouth-sv-vs-michigan-pr-round-analysis`)
still work and forward to the new one.

==A numbered prelim is written short==: Round 3 of Kentucky is
`/videos/26-27/cpd/kentucky/r3/michigan-gv-dartmouth-cg`. Elims keep their
names (`finals`, `octafinals`). Links that spelled it `round-3` still work and
forward to the `r3` address.

A video tagged with a round but no team would have an address that names no
one video (`/videos/26-27/vcx/finals`), so ==its title is added after the
round==: `/videos/26-27/vcx/finals-<title>`, or just the title when it already
says the round. ==The short address still works and forwards to the video it
always opened==.

### List layout [#video-library-list-layout]

==Clicking a row opens that video's== [watch page](/docs/features/videos#video-watch-page)
(Ctrl/⌘-click opens it in a new tab). ==To keep browsing while it plays==, use
the row's **Watch in popout player** icon instead — ==it starts the video in
the floating player in the corner==. On a watch page the
same icon ==lines the video up for the popout instead, and it starts when you
leave the page== — see [Up next in the popout player](/docs/features/videos#video-watch-page-up-next-in-the-popout-player).

The list view, the alternative to the card grid, ==shows
a cleaned-up Tournament column rather than the raw stored value==: years, round
labels ("Quarterfinals", "R3", "Round Robin"), and generic org words
("Tournament", "Nationals", "Invitational") ==are stripped==, and "Tournament of
Champions"/"National Debate Tournament" collapse to "TOC"/"NDT". A round whose tournament cleans
to nothing (or that has no team on either side) ==shows its title across the
row instead of blank Tournament/Aff/Neg columns==.

==Rounds are grouped season → tournament, newest first at every level==: ==the
newest season on top==, within a season the tournament with the most recent
video on top, and ==within a tournament the newest video on top== (rounds from
the same day read Finals down to the prelims). Rounds with no tournament go
into an **Unsorted** tournament group, ==which is placed by its own newest video
like any other tournament==.

==A tournament row shows its logo in place of its name==: the tournament's own
(TOC, NDT, CEDA, NSDA, the Barkley Forum's Emory) or ==its host school's, found
by any spelling of the school== ("University of KY" shows Kentucky's). A
round-robin ==keeps a small **RR** badge after the logo==. ==The Shirley is Wake
Forest's tournament==, so it ==shows the Wake Forest logo with a **Shirley**
badge after it==, styled like the RR one; the badge also shows on video cards,
round rows, top-pick tooltips and the watch page.

==Each season row names that season's topic== beside its `23-24` label, such as
**Legal Personhood** for college rounds in 23-24. It is the topic's short name
(or the start of the resolution when there is none),
and ==hovering it shows the full resolution==. ==LD and PF seasons list every topic
name in order==, and the tooltip gives each with its starting month. ==A season
that mixes formats, as Greatest of All Time does, lines up that season's topics
side by side== — high school Policy, College, PF and LD, each behind its format
badge and cut short — and ==hovering any one shows that format's full
resolutions==.

Top Picks mixes every format, so there ==each tournament row carries a format
badge== (PF, LD, Policy, College) beside its name — ==one per format among its
rounds== — and so does the tournament name on every round row under it
==Single-format
listings== such as `/videos/pf` ==leave it off==.

==A top pick wears its 🎖️ badge wherever it is listed==, not just under Top
Picks: in All results, a search, a profile's videos, the related list under the
player, and ==on the round's own video page== beside the tournament.

==Hovering the 🎖️ badge opens its Greatest of All Time card==, which names both
teams and, for a college team the NDT record knows, ==its all-time ranks== from
the **All seasons: NDT points** table on the rankings: "#2 team of all time"
for the pair and "#1 individual debater of all time" for each debater, with the
same weights as that table (seasons before 2000 count 0.6×, high school TOC and
NDCA results count). A pair that was neither debater's best partnership shows
its rank among every partnership, marked *strictly together*. High school teams
and teams with no NDT results show no ranks.

On a screen wide enough to hold the table twice over, ==the list splits into two
side-by-side tables==, each with its own header (the sort, collapse and column
widths are shared). ==Rows are dealt to whichever column is shorter==, so a flat
list reads left, right, left, right like the card grid, and ==a page loading more
only adds to the bottom==. A listing that is all one season splits that season's
tournaments and ==repeats the season row atop both columns==. ==Narrower screens keep
the single table==.

Because the groups are built from whatever has loaded, ==the row view keeps
paging on its own== (up to the usual 600-video ceiling) ==instead of waiting for
you to scroll==. Otherwise ==sorting by views would show only each season's
most-watched rounds== until the rest of the feed arrived.

Every column (Aff, Neg, Date, Views) ==shows at every screen width==. On a phone
the table is wider than the screen, so ==swipe it left and right to reach the
columns beside the round==; ==the first column is capped at three quarters of the
screen so the next one always peeks in==.

==View counts are colored by tier==: wherever a video's views show (cards,
table rows, tournament rows, the watch page, the admin tables), ==the count
gets larger and changes color as views rise==. Under 500 is gray and a little
smaller than the text around it; then sky (500+), teal (1k+), green (2.5k+),
lime (5k+), yellow (10k+) and amber (20k+). ==30,000 views is gold with a ⭐
star==, then orange with a 🔥 flame (60k+), rose with a 🏆 trophy (100k+) and
violet with a 👑 crown (250k+). Hover a count for the exact number.

==A round's title wraps to up to three lines==. When a round has no Aff or Neg
team recorded, ==its title cell spans the Aff and Neg columns instead of
leaving two empty dashes==.

#### Team names and ratings [#video-library-team-names-and-ratings]

Each team shows as a [team label](/docs/features/team-collaboration#team-rankings-team-labels):
==its school's logo (or name), then its debaters==, ==in a blue box for the Aff
and a red one for the Neg==. Video cards and the watch page header ==use the same
label inside their blue and red team badges==, as do the mini player's title
bar and the top-pick tooltip. A code like "Harker LL" ==is split into the
school== ("Harker", shown as its logo) and the two debaters' initials ("LL"),
==which become their last names whenever the current season's rankings know
the team==. ==A school's full name finds the same logo as its short one==:
"University of Kansas" shows the Kansas logo, "California State University -
Long Beach" the Long Beach one, and ==nicknames such as== "Cal" and "Wake" work
too, as do ==endings like "High School", "HS", "Academy" or "College Prep"==
("Bronx High School of Science", "Walter Payton College Prep") and
abbreviations such as "USC", "MBA", "GBN" and "UK". ==Every school logo the site has is matched this way==. ==Another campus of a system keeps its own name rather than borrowing the
flagship's logo==, so "University of Texas - Dallas" stays as text.

In the Aff and Neg columns, a team from the **current season** that the
[team rankings](/docs/features/team-collaboration#team-rankings) know ==shows its
debaters' last names in full== (long names wrap rather than being cut off) and
==its rank as a small== **#N** badge inside the label, and ==clicking it opens that
team's page==. ==Hover the badge for the team's rating, rounds and aff/neg win
rates==. ==The badge scales with the rank: #1 is the largest, in the side's full
blue or red with a crown, and lower ranks get smaller and fade toward gray==.
Video cards and the watch page show the same badge. Any other team
==shows the initials from its code==.
Any other team keeps the old behaviour: ==clicking its name searches the
library for that team's videos==. ==The rankings cover the current season only==,
so ==an older round's team code is never matched against them== (it would name
different debaters).

**College rounds from past seasons** ==are matched against that season's own
ratings instead==: [debatedata](https://debatedata.weebly.com)'s top-100 table
==for the season the video is from, 2009-10 onward==. A team found there ==shows
its debaters' last names and its== **#N** rank that season; hovering the badge
shows the season and debatedata's rating. ==Its name links to the team's
past-season page== (see [Team Rankings](/docs/features/team-collaboration#team-rankings-past-season-team-pages)). Shorthand like "KU", "OU",
"UTD" or "Cal" and speech names tacked onto the label ("Baylor CM 1AC") ==are
understood==, and a code two teams in that season's table share (same school,
same initials) ==is left unmatched rather than guessed==. Teams outside that
season's top 100 ==show their initials==.

On the watch page, **high school rounds from past seasons** (2015-16 on) are
matched the same way against that season's full rankings, so the header's
team badges read ==the debaters' last names and that season's== **#N** rank
instead of the code's initials.

A team is matched the same way the team pages match it: ==the school loosely,
the initials exactly==, ==in the division the round's format is ranked in==.

### Guest video limit [#video-library-guest-video-limit]

==Signed out, a visitor can open ten different videos in a browser==; ==opening an
eleventh new one pauses it behind a sign-in dialog==. ==Videos already opened stay
watchable==, and ==signing in returns to the same page with the video playing==.
Closing the dialog closes the popout player, or leaves the watch page for the
library. ==The limit covers both the watch page and the popout player==.

### Suggesting a fix to a video [#video-library-suggesting-a-fix-to-a-video]

==Every watch page has a pencil button next to the video==. Click it to open the
video's details — title, format, lecture category, tournament, teams, winner
and so on — ==change whatever is wrong==, and press **Submit for approval**.
==Nothing changes right away==: ==your edit goes to the moderators, who are notified
and approve or dismiss it from the admin panel==. **Request deletion** in the
same dialog ==asks them to take the video off the site instead==. ==You need to be
signed in to suggest a change==.

Admins and moderators get the same pencil button, but ==for them it saves
straight to the library==. A video's address is built from its season,
tournament, round and teams, so ==correcting one of those moves the video to a
new address, and the page takes you there as soon as you save==, with the same
speech still open.

### Submitting a video or channel [#video-library-submitting-a-video-or-channel]

The **+** button beside the library's search bar opens **Add to the library**.
On the **Video** tab, ==paste a YouTube link to a round or lecture== — to share
your own, ==upload it to YouTube first (unlisted works)== and paste that link. On
the **Channel** tab, paste a channel link or type its `@handle` ==to have the
library follow it==. Add a note for the moderators if it helps, then press
**Submit for review**. ==Nothing is published right away==: ==the submission goes to
the moderators==, who are notified and approve or dismiss it from the admin
panel. ==You need to be signed in to submit==.

### Fixing a video (admins) [#video-library-fixing-a-video-admins]

The admin panel has a **Video library** card ==listing every video already published to
the site==, searchable by title, channel, tournament or YouTube id and filterable
by format. **Edit** ==opens the whole record== — title, channel, publish date,
format, tournament, round level, teams, each side's arguments, winner, judge
decision, description, speech-docs link and the top-pick flag — and saving it
==updates the public
library right away, including how the video sorts and what searches find it==.
**Lecture category** is a dropdown prefilled with every lecture shelf the
library uses (Topic Lectures, Affirmative Strategy, Counterplans & Theory and
the rest), ==so a typo can't create a stray shelf==; pick **None** for a round.
**Aff arguments** ==holds the affirmative's case== — the plan or advocacy and its
advantages — and **Neg arguments** ==the positions the negative ran== (ideally
what went for the 2NR); ==they are the 1AC and 2NR lines shown on the video's
card==, and on the watch page they sit in small type under each team's name. **✨ Auto-fill with AI** ==reads YouTube's title and
description and fills the format==, category, tournament, round, teams, both
sides' arguments, winner, decision and speech-docs link; ==anything the model
can't read leaves your field as it is==. ==Titles in the usual shape
("2024 TOC LD Finals: Greeley SG vs Monta Vista EY") are read instantly by
rule==, and the model is only asked for what's left (and gets 20 seconds per
key). It runs on OpenRouter's free models,
==on your own OpenRouter or Anthropic key when you've saved one in **Settings →
General**==, otherwise on Debate AI's shared key. If it fails, the message under
the form ==says which AI provider refused and why==.
On a **College** round, the form also has **🏛️ Look up results**: ==it finds
the debate on [College Debate Results](https://collegedebateresults.vercel.app)==
from the tournament, season, round and both teams, and shows the teams with
their debaters, the judges and the decision. ==A winner is only shown when the
site records the decision== — never guessed from seeds or standings — and a
tournament name that could mean two tournaments is refused with both listed.

#### Speech AI — speeches, summaries and alternatives in bulk [#video-library-speech-ai--speeches-summaries-and-alternatives-in-bulk]

The **Speech AI** section (admins only) ==runs the AI over many rounds at once==,
==so viewers don't have to press== **Detect speeches** themselves. Pick
**Greatest of All-Time videos** (the top-pick collection) or **Every video**
and press **Process**. For each video it:

1. ==fetches the YouTube captions== (or uses the ones already cached);
2. ==has the AI find the format and where every speech starts and ends==, the
   same way **Detect speeches** does on the watch page;
3. ==runs the prompt in the box over the round, speech by speech==. The default
   prompt ==asks for a summary of each speech and what the speaker could have
   done instead==; edit it to ask for something else. ==The reply format is added
   after your prompt automatically==;
4. ==asks the AI once more which argument the round was about==: what the
   affirmative read in the 1AC, and ==what the negative's final rebuttal went
   for== (the 2NR; the NR in LD, the Con Final Focus in PF). ==Those become the
   video's== **Aff arguments** and **Neg arguments**, as short tags like
   "Setcol" or "Cap K, Framework", ==replacing what was there before==, except on
   a video an admin edited by hand, ==whose arguments are left alone==. If this
   step fails the documents are still saved.

==The result is saved as the video's== **Summary** and **Analysis** documents,
with one heading per speech, such as "1AC (0:42–8:51)". On the watch page those
==become the speech buttons under the player==, and each speech's tab in the
sidebar shows its summary and its alternative. ==Documents an editor wrote are
never replaced==, and ==videos already analyzed are skipped unless== **Redo videos
the AI already analyzed** is ticked. To try a prompt first, paste one video's
link into the box below the prompt and press **Preview**, ==which shows both
documents and the picked arguments without saving them==; **Process and save** does that one video for
real. ==Every video costs three AI calls on the site's shared key==, so ==the run goes
one video per request== and **Stop after this video** halts it.

#### Watch page AI — summaries and alternatives for every round, ahead of time [#video-library-watch-page-ai--summaries-and-alternatives-for-every-round-ahead-of-time]

A round ==whose speeches have no start times gets== **Detect speeches** ==first==,

- ==Rounds that already have their results are skipped==, and ==nothing anyone

## Video Watch Page [#video-watch-page]

==Every video in the library has its own page==: ==the player, the synced
transcript beside it, the round's metadata under it, and related videos
below that==. ==It replaces the transcript dialog that used to open on top of
the grid==, which had no address — ==nothing to send a teammate, nothing for
search to index==, and no room for anything but the player and a narrow
column of sentences.

Open it by clicking any row in the list layout, or the transcript (CC) button
on a video card.

### The URL [#video-watch-page-the-url]

```
/videos/watch/2022-ndt-finals-dartmouth-vs-michigan
                └──────────── title ────────────────┘
```

==The title half is for readers and for search engines==. ==A video that gets
retitled gets a new address== — ==old links stop working==, which is the
trade-off for clean URLs without appended ids.

### The title [#video-watch-page-the-title]

==A round's page is titled after the round, not after whatever the uploader
called it== — in the browser tab, in search results and in the heading over
the video:

```
2026 NDT College Octas: Kansas #14 OW (K) vs CSULB #7 MO (Policy)
```

That is ==the tournament's year and name, the level (College, HS Policy, LD,
PF) and the round, then the aff and the neg==. ==Each team's rank sits
between its school and its debaters== (`Kansas #33 Harris & Wilkus`), and ==its
argument== (the 1AC for the aff, the 2NR for the neg) ==follows in parentheses==,
each when known; the rank is the one the team's badge shows, from the current
season's rankings or, for a past college round, that season's table. The
year is the one in the tournament's name or the video's title, or else the
season's: its autumn for a video uploaded in the autumn, its spring
otherwise. ==A round with a computed title doesn't repeat the upload's own
title under the heading==.

==Every round stores this title as its computed title==, with ==that season's
rank and the debaters' full last names==:

```
2026 Harvard Quarters: Iowa #6 Eltyeb & Weimar vs Emory #1 Gallagher & Young
```

==The computed title is what the library shows and searches== — on cards, list
rows, the mini player and the command palette — ==in place of the YouTube
title==. Staff fill it from the admin YouTube sync page (**Fill computed
titles**); a round added since then is titled the same way without ranks.

A video with no teams recorded whose title still reads as a round
(`2022 NDT Finals - Dartmouth SV vs Michigan PR`) ==is titled from its title
the same way==, without ranks or arguments. ==A lecture keeps its own title==.

==works out the format== (Policy, LD, Public Forum, or another), and ==finds
when each speech begins and ends== — 1AC, 1AX (the cross-ex of the 1AC),
1NC, 1NX, 2AC, 2AX, 2NC, 2NX, 1NR, 1AR, 2NR, 2AR for Policy, and the
matching speeches for LD and PF.

How it reads the captions: ==auto-captions have no speaker names==, so ==the
quiet stretches between them== (prep time, the next speaker setting up)
==are the main clue==. Those pauses are marked in what the AI reads, along
with each speech's usual length and a first guess placed from the pauses
alone. The AI then ==moves each start to where the words show the speech
really begins== — "time starts now", a roadmap, the switch to cross-ex
questions — and ==skips anything it can't find rather than guessing==.
Times past the end of the video, overlapping speeches and the AI's own
low-confidence guesses ==are thrown out==.

If the AI can't be reached (you're signed out, or the daily AI limit is
used up), **Detect speeches** ==still places the speeches from the caption
pauses and the format's speech lengths, and says so== — ==check those starts
before relying on them==.

If you've added your own OpenRouter or Anthropic key in **Settings → General**,
==Detect speeches and LLM summaries use that key first, and fall back to Debate
AI's own AI if it fails==.

- ==Each speech's tab is timed, the timeline fills in==, and **Transcript**
  ==shows exactly the captions spoken during that speech== — cut off at its
  end, ==so prep time and the judge's comments stay out==.
- A round with a written summary ==keeps its own speeches and only gains
  the times it was missing==. A **Mark start** ==you set yourself always
  wins over a detected one==.
- The result is ==published for everyone who opens the video== (see
  [Shared with everyone](#video-watch-page-shared-with-everyone)). ==Anyone signed in can
  press== **Detect again**, and ==their new run replaces the published one
  for every viewer==. **Clear** ==forgets a copy that only this browser
  has==.

#### LLM summary — a speech's key points and warrants [#video-watch-page-llm-summary--a-speechs-key-points-and-warrants]

==Every speech with a transcript has an== **LLM summary** button. ==It outlines
the speech the way you'd flow it==: ==each key point as a short bold tag==, ==the
warrants behind it as nested bullet phrases==, in the order the speaker
made them, ==ending with the speech's bottom line==. The outline opens in its
own **LLM summary** view, ==is kept in this browser and shared with every
viewer==, and **Run again** asks for a fresh one. On a round nobody typed up, ==the button turns on once the
speech has a start== — marked or detected.

==Both buttons need you to be signed in==, and use the site's AI model — or
your own OpenRouter or Anthropic key if you've added one in settings
==(a summary run on your own key doesn't count toward your plan's daily AI
limit)==.
==They work from auto-generated captions, so check them against the video==.

#### Shared with everyone [#video-watch-page-shared-with-everyone]

Detected speeches, speech starts, LLM summaries and Alternatives runs ==depend
only on the video, not on who asked==, so ==they are saved for the video and
shown to everyone who opens it afterwards== — signed out included. ==A
round only has to be analyzed once==: open it and ==the speeches are already
timed, the summaries and simulations already there==.

- ==Speech start times are public and anyone can fix them==. The latest
  **Detect speeches** run and every **Mark start** set while signed in ==are
  what every viewer of that video sees==. A published detection says
  **shared** next to its result. A run that fell back to the caption pauses
  ==only fills a video nobody has detected yet==, so it never replaces an AI
  detection.
- ==LLM summaries and Alternatives runs keep the first good result==; a later
  run shows on your screen but doesn't replace the shared one.
- ==A shared Alternatives run says it was run by another viewer==.
- ==Results you ran before sharing existed are shared too==, the next time
  you open that video while signed in.
- If a shared result is wrong, ==an admin or moderator can== **Run again**
  ==to replace it for everyone==.

#### Rounds the site has already processed [#video-watch-page-rounds-the-site-has-already-processed]

Many rounds — starting with the Greatest of All-Time collection — ==have
been run through the site's speech AI ahead of time==. Those open with the
speech buttons under the player ==already timed==, and each speech's tab has a
**Summary** ==(what the speaker did)== and an **Analysis** ==(what they could
have done instead)== ==with no button to press==. The **Summary** tab at the top
==shows the whole round's write-up at once==. ==These are AI-written from the
captions, and are marked as such==.

==The last three speeches of most rounds== (in policy the 1AR, 2NR and 2AR;
in PF the second summary and both final focuses) ==also come with their==
**LLM summary** ==and an== **Alternatives** ==run already there==, simulated for a
flow judge. Pick a different judge panel and **Simulate** runs it fresh.

#### Alternatives — what else the speech could have done [#video-watch-page-alternatives--what-else-the-speech-could-have-done]

==Every speech tab also has an== **Alternatives** view (it was called
**Outcomes** until November 2026). Press **Simulate** and
==Claude drafts two to four alternative responses the speaker could have
given instead== — a different collapse, an impact turn, a framework
push, or for a cross-ex a different line of questioning — and ==predicts
the ballot after each one==, ==next to its prediction for the speech as it
was actually given==.

- Pick the **judges** first ==from the **Judges** dropdown== — tick one to five of: flow, lay, policymaker,
  critique-friendly, theory-first and traditional. ==Each judge on the panel
  votes separately with their own reason for decision== and the argument
  that decided it, and ==the panel's decision is the vote tally== (an odd
  panel avoids ties). ==Your panel is remembered==.
- Optionally **steer** ==the alternatives== — "go for the DA instead of
  theory" — before simulating.
- A bar chart shows ==the aff's chance of winning after each version==, with
  the 50% line marked. The number beside each alternative is ==how much it
  helps or hurts the side that gave the speech==.
- ==The alternative that helps most is marked== **Strongest**; ==one that would
  change who wins is marked== **Ballot flips**. Open a card for the outline
  in speaking order, ==what it gives up, and every judge's ballot==.
- **Copy** ==puts the whole run on your clipboard as Markdown==, ready for a
  doc or your team chat.
- ==The last run for each speech and panel is kept in this browser and
  shared with every viewer==, and its tab gets a small sparkle. **Run again**
  asks for a fresh set.
- ==The top three alternatives== — the three that help the speaker most —
  ==each have an **AI Write** button== (a wand with the label), **Do the full speech**. It ==writes the whole
  speech in that direction==, working from ==the speech before it and the
  speech as given==, ==kept clearly different from the other two
  alternatives' full speeches==, and opens it ==in the Debate Editor so you can rewrite
  it==. ==A speech timer above the editor is set to the speech's length==;
  start it to ==record yourself delivering the speech== (the mic button turns
  recording off), and the recording plays back under the timer. ==Your edits
  and recording are kept in this browser==, so reopening the same
  alternative shows your version, and ==each alternative's button opens its own
  speech and recording==, even after **Run again**; **Write again** asks for a fresh one.
  ==The browser keeps your 40 most recently saved full speeches==; saving a
  new one past that drops the one you saved longest ago.
  Evidence the new strategy needs but nobody read shows as a bracketed
  placeholder to cut a card for.

==Simulating needs you to be signed in==. The alternatives and ballots are
==Claude's predictions from a rough transcript and summary, not real
results== — ==use them to think through options, not as a verdict==. For a
round nobody has written up, Claude works from the video's captions: the
speech's own captions once you have marked its start, otherwise the
whole round's captions, so ==marking starts gives sharper results==.

### Team rankings [#video-watch-page-team-rankings]

Under the round's details, a round whose aff and neg teams are in the
[team rankings](/coaching/rankings) ==gets a== **Team rankings** section: ==one card
per team with its rank in the division==, rating and deviation, matches played,
and aff, neg and elim win rates, beside its radar chart. ==The opponent is drawn
on each radar as a dashed outline==, ==so you can see where one team is stronger==,
and ==the team's school average in that division as a dotted one==. The school
average is on by default; a **Show school average** checkbox under the radar
turns it off, and only appears when the school has another ranked team in the
division.
==Each team name links to that team's profile==.

==Whenever either team is ranked==, a **Simulated matchup** card above the team cards
==predicts who wins==. ==It uses the sides the teams actually debated== (the
division's aff/neg split and each team's own aff and neg record) and the
round's stage: a prelim is one judge, an elim a 3-judge panel and finals a
5-judge panel, so ==a favorite's edge grows in elims==. It shows each side's win
chance, the predicted winner and a 1,000-round simulation, and, when the
round's decision is recorded, ==whether the favorite won or it was an upset==.
The math is the [matchup simulator](/coaching/rankings)'s, with the rating
mix each team's profile recommends. ==It is a simulation, not a ballot==. When
the round label isn't recorded, ==the round is treated as a prelim==.

==When only one team is ranked, the other plays as a stand-in==: an average
team from its school when the rankings know the school, otherwise a typical
team in the field. The card says which team that is.

Each card starts with ==a small picture of the school's mascot== when there is
one (colleges only for now: Dartmouth, Emory, Georgetown, Iowa, Kansas,
Michigan, Michigan State, Northwestern, Oklahoma, Texas, Cal and Wake Forest).
To hide mascots, turn off **Show school mascots** in
[Settings → Preferences](/settings/preferences).
Below the stats, a **Season history** table lists ==the same team's results in
past seasons, going back up to 15 seasons==: for college policy, its rank,
rating and skill in each season's debatedata top 100 (2009-10 on); for high
school, its rank of the whole field and its TOC bids in every season the same
two debaters were ranked, up to three seasons either side of the round
(2015-16 on). ==A pair that changed schools keeps its earlier seasons==, with
the old school named beside the rank. The season the card's own numbers are
from is highlighted.

The rankings themselves cover this season only, and ==a team code from an
earlier year belongs to other debaters==, so ==a past season's round is matched
in that season's own results instead==: a college round in that year's
debatedata table, a high school round in that season's full rankings. Its card shows that season's rank and rating (and TOC bids for high
school) with the same mascot and season history, but no radar. ==Past rounds
get a simulated matchup too==, back to 2015-16 for high school and the last 15 seasons
for college. High school uses that season's ratings and side records. College
uses debatedata's skill and uncertainty for that season (it has no side
records, so sides don't shift the odds), and a team missing from its top 100
plays as a team just outside it. A team no source knows is left out of the
cards, and when neither team is found ==the section doesn't appear==.

### Controls [#video-watch-page-controls]

==The toolbar above the video is the popout player's toolbar==: play/pause,
slow-the-spread (drops the round to 75% speed), skip to the next queued video, captions, and
picture-in-picture all mean exactly what they mean there. ==A page can offer
three more== — fullscreen, a permalink to copy, and a favourite toggle.

==The arrow beside the copy button chooses what it copies==: **URL only**,
**URL + title**, or a **Citation (Chicago)** in notes-and-bibliography form,
such as `Debate AI Institute. “2026 Harvard Quarters: …” February 15, 2026. Video, 1:42:07. https://….`
It uses the full posting date (or "n.d." if there is none) and adds the
running time after "Video," once the length is known.
==Picking one copies it right away, and the button keeps using it== in this
browser until you pick another.

==Two of them are about leaving==:

- **Minimize** ==hands the video back to the floating popout player and
  returns you to the library==. ==Playback continues from the same second==.
- **Close** ==stops the video and returns to the library==.

==Only one video ever plays==: ==opening a watch page takes playback over from
the floating player, and leaving hands it back==.

#### Several tabs [#video-watch-page-several-tabs]

==Only one tab plays at a time either==. ==The tab that is already playing keeps
playing==: a watch page you open in a new tab ==loads paused==, and the floating
player a new tab restores ==comes back paused== instead of starting over the
other tab. ==Press play in any tab and every other tab pauses==. Once you have
clicked around in a tab, ==a video you pick there plays straight away== and
takes over the same way. The desktop app's mini player window follows the
same rule.

#### Mini player window [#video-watch-page-mini-player-window]

**Pop out mini player** ==opens the video in a small window of its own==: the
video, a title bar, and the round's speeches as a strip of buttons with
**‹** / **›** ==to jump to the previous or next speech== (the back button
restarts the current speech if you are a few seconds into it). The page's
own player pauses and ==the window picks up at the same second==. ==The speeches
are the same ones the page shows==, including start times you marked or the
AI detected in this browser. **Open the full watch page** (the expand icon)
==sends you back to the watch page and closes the window==.

In the **desktop app** ==the window opens in the bottom-right corner== and has a
**pin** button: pinned, ==it stays on top of every other window==, and ==follows
you across virtual desktops==, so ==a round keeps playing in the corner while
you flow it== or write in another app. ==It starts pinned==; the app remembers
whether you last left it pinned. On Linux, ==some Wayland desktops ignore
always-on-top requests==, so the pin may have no effect there.

==The browser hands it to the desktop app== when the app is installed and
running on the same computer: clicking **Pop out mini player** on
debate-ai.com in Chrome, Edge or Firefox opens the app's pinned window instead
of a popup. The first time, Chrome or Edge may ask whether the site may
==look for apps and devices on your network==; allow it, or the browser popup
opens instead. With the app closed, nothing changes.

Otherwise, in a **browser** ==it is an ordinary popup window== (allow popups for the site
if nothing opens). ==Browsers can't keep a popup above other apps, so there is
no pin==; Chrome's **picture-in-picture** button ==is the browser's always-on-top
option==.


### Search box [#video-watch-page-search-box]

Under the round's details, just above the related videos, ==is a search box
for the whole library==. Press Enter (or **Search**) and the list underneath
==swaps to the library's matches==, headed **Results for "…"** — ==without leaving
the page, so the video above keeps playing==. Clicking a team name in one of
those rows ==searches for that team the same way==. **Clear** ==brings the related
videos back==. ==It is on every watch page==, including one with no related videos.

==Use it to build a playlist==: add matches one at a time with each row's
**Add to queue** button, or **Queue all** above the list ==to queue every
match not already queued==, in the list's order. The queue panel beside the
list ==shows what's lined up, and the next video plays when this one ends==.

### Related videos [#video-watch-page-related-videos]

Underneath the player: ==the rest of the same tournament first, then the
same debate format or lecture category, then the most recent videos== in the
library — enough passes to fill the row for a video with neither. Clicking
one ==opens *its* watch page, so the URL always names what is playing==.

Each related row ==shows its video's length under its action buttons==, and the
**Related videos** heading (or **Results for …** during a search) ==shows the
combined length of every listed video that has one==, e.g. `6:40 total`. The
video's own length sits beside its uploader, date and view count at the top of
the page, right under the teams and tournament.
==Lengths come from the stored durations== the admin **Fetch video durations**
card fills; ==a video it hasn't reached shows none and adds nothing to the
total==, though the page's own video picks up the player's reading once it
loads.

#### Up next in the popout player [#video-watch-page-up-next-in-the-popout-player]

A related row's popout icon ==does not take over from the video you are
watching==. ==It lines that video up in the floating popout player instead==: the
page's video keeps playing, a small **Up next in popout** card in the corner
names the lined-up video, and ==the popout starts playing it as soon as you
leave the page==. Click the icon again, or the card's ×, ==to cancel==. Opening the
lined-up video's own watch page ==plays it there and clears it==.
