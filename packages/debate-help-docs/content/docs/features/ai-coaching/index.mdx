## Coaching Programs and Group Challenges [#coaching-programs]

Lets a coach ==create a named coaching space scoped to a squad roster==, and
==opens each program's live board==: its shared topic sprint, its group-challenge
standings, and each member's generated drill set.

Open it from the **Coaching Programs** tab of [Coaching](/docs/features/ai-coaching#coaching).

### What it shows

==A form to create a coaching space==, then every program you have made with
its roster listed as badges. Each program has a **View board** action showing
==topic-sprint status, group-challenge standings==, and a **Member flows**
roster for recording each member's practice round.

### Roster analytics dashboard

A **Roster Analytics** section ==shows every roster member's group-challenge
standing and daily-quest streak in one table==, with **Drills completed** and
**Practice round** columns per member, plus a **Recent challenge results**
digest scoped to that program's roster.

## LLM Card Scoring [#llm-card-scoring]

==Scores a debate-evidence card across five dimensions== — relevance, clarity,
uniqueness, evidence quality, usability — ==ranks every card, flags likely
duplicates==, and ==can ask an AI for a written assessment==.

Find it at **/research/cards/scoring**.

### What it shows

A topic switcher, a **Score card** form, a **Bulk import** box (paste several
cards separated by a line of `---`), ==a ranked list with per-dimension scores== and a
**Likely duplicate** badge, and a **Get AI assessment** button per card.
==Evidence Library results also carry a "Score card" action== with an inline
score badge.

### Good to know

- ==Bulk import takes pasted text, not a file upload==.
- ==Scores and AI assessments sync to your account==, but ==they are not shared
  with your team== — a squad member scoring the same card sees their own
  assessment, not a teammate's.

## AI Outcome Responses [#ai-outcome-responses]

During a live round, the speech timer's menu (the ⋮ next to the timer) has
an **AI Outcome Responses** item. ==It reads the whole round so far== and
==suggests three ways to give the speech you are on==, then plays each one
forward.

### What it shows [#ai-outcome-responses-what-it-shows]

==Three candidate approaches side by side==. Each one has:

- a **success rate**: ==the AI's estimate that this approach wins the round==
  for your side, ==with the best bet marked==;
- a speech-ready **outline** that ==extends cards already read and answers
  the opponent's newest arguments== (copy button included). ==A "Possible
  fallacies" hint appears under the outline== when its wording matches an ad
  hominem, straw man or false dichotomy pattern; it is a prompt to look
  closer, not a verdict;
- the **opponent's likely answers** ==in their next speech==;
- the **judge decision**: ==who the predicted judge votes for on that path==,
  and why;
- the **issues** ==that could lose it== (drops, missing evidence, judge fit);
- **cards to find**: ==searches that run against CARDS in place== and
  show the highlighted text of the top hits, with a copy button and a link
  to open the full search.

==You can override the judge paradigm and add an optional focus== ("go for the
DA") before regenerating.

### What it reads [#ai-outcome-responses-what-it-reads]

- **Prior speeches:** ==every speech before the current one==. A speech's
  linked document is used when it has one, otherwise the flow's own speech
  document. ==Highlighted card text is kept marked== so the AI can tell what
  was read aloud.
- **Flow:** ==every flow sheet in the round==, argument by argument, with
  ==unanswered arguments flagged==.
- **Judge:** ==the round judge with the most ballots in==
  [Judge Profiles](/docs/features/tournaments-judge#judge-profiles), voting under that profile's most-tagged
  paradigm. With no saved profile for the round's judges, the paradigm most
  common across your saved profiles is used, else a flow judge.

### Good to know [#ai-outcome-responses-good-to-know]

- ==Fallacy hints are a quick wording check==, and only look at the outline,
  not the opponent answers or issues.
- ==Results are not saved==; closing the dialog keeps them only until you
  switch speeches.
- ==Success rates are AI estimates, not calibrated against real ballots==.
- On the free plan ==the AI's reply is length-limited==, which can cut a
  long three-candidate reply short; the dialog then shows an error.
- ==Very long rounds are trimmed==, oldest speeches first.

## How Team Ratings Are Computed [#bradley-terry-debate-rankings]

The [Team Rankings](/docs/features/team-collaboration#team-rankings) come from a
**ballot-level Bradley-Terry model** fitted ==to every decided round of the
season at once==.

Fitting the whole season together means ==the order rounds were debated in does
not matter==: a round in September counts the same as one in February, and ==a
team's rating reflects everyone it has beaten and lost to, weighted by how
strong those opponents turned out to be==.

### The model [#bradley-terry-debate-rankings-the-model]

==Each team (or LD debater) gets a skill== θ on the logit scale. The
probability that the aff wins a ballot is

```latex
\[
P(\text{aff wins}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\theta_{\text{aff}} - \theta_{\text{neg}} + \beta)}}
\]
```

where β is ==one shared== **side term** ==that soaks up any field-wide
aff or neg advantage==, so a team isn't credited for drawing the favored side.

### Side balancing, prelims and elims apart [#bradley-terry-debate-rankings-side-balancing-prelims-and-elims-apart]

One side term isn't enough on its own, because ==side bias differs by round
type==: ==prelim sides are assigned==, while in elims a coin-flip winner or the
higher seed picks the side, so ==the aff can win well over half of elims== in a
field whose prelims are close to even. Before the fit, every ballot is
weighted so that **aff and neg each carry exactly half the ballot weight,
==separately in prelims and in elims==**:

```latex
\[
w_{\text{aff}} = \frac{A + N}{2A}, \qquad w_{\text{neg}} = \frac{A + N}{2N}
\]
```

where A and N are the aff and neg ballots of that round type. The
favored side's ballots weigh less than 1 and the other side's more, and ==the
round type's total weight is unchanged== — so ==a team that won a run of elims on
the favored side isn't over-credited==, and one that won on the disfavored side
gets its due. β then absorbs whatever side effect is left. If a round type
has no ballots on one side, its weights stay 1.

### What counts as data [#bradley-terry-debate-rankings-what-counts-as-data]

- **Ballots, not just rounds.** When a round lists panel votes, ==each ballot is
  its own comparison== — ==a 3–0 is stronger evidence than a 2–1==. Rounds without
  votes count as one ballot for the winner.
- **Majors count double.** ==Ballots at the tournaments marked as majors have a
  likelihood weight of 2==, on top of the side weight above.
- **Byes, forfeits and undecided rounds are skipped** for the rating, though
  ==they still count toward the raw win rates==.

### The prior [#bradley-terry-debate-rankings-the-prior]

==The fit adds a ridge penalty of 1.0 on every skill== — equivalent to a
standard normal prior. ==Teams with only a few ballots are pulled toward
the field average==, and ==an unbeaten team still gets a finite rating== instead of
running off to infinity. A team with no decided rounds sits exactly at the
prior: field average, standard error 1.

### From skill to rating [#bradley-terry-debate-rankings-from-skill-to-rating]

Skills are reported on the familiar Elo scale, ==where 1500 is the field average
and 400 points is 10:1 odds==:

| Figure | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Rating | 1500 + (400 / ln 10) × θ |
| Deviation | The skill's standard error, in rating points |
| Adjusted Rating | Rating − 2 × Deviation — what the rank is sorted on |
| Matches | Decided rounds debated (aff + neg) |
| Aff Rating Advantage | Rating points the aff side is still worth, field-wide, after side balancing |
| Aff / Neg Side Weight | The prelim side weights above |
| Aff / Neg Elim Side Weight | The elim side weights above |

==Adjusted Rating is a conservative estimate==: a lightly tested team with a high
point estimate ==can't outrank a similarly rated, well-established one==.

The win probability between two entries, ignoring side, is

```latex
\[
P(A \text{ beats } B) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_B - R_A)/400}}
\]
```

### On this site [#bradley-terry-debate-rankings-on-this-site]

==The site shows every rating 1000 points lower and divided by about 6.83
(8.2 ÷ 1.2, so every rating reads 20% higher), then clamped to 0–109==. That puts ==the top handful of teams in each event
at or near the 109 cap (10.9 as shown)== and the weakest teams at or near 0. ==The shift and scale keep ranks
and win probabilities unchanged==; the clamp only ties the few teams past either
end. The aff rating advantage, being a difference, is only divided by 6.83.

==Ratings are then shown divided by 10 with one decimal==, so a 96 reads
9.6 and the scale runs 0.0–10.9 (sorting and every calculation still use the
full values). The whole part, 0 through 10,
==is the team's tier== and is shaded from gold at the
top tier down to gray at the bottom, so ==the tier boundaries stand out== as you
scroll down the table.

## Coach Materials [#coach-materials]

==Lets a coach upload grounding materials== — lecture transcripts, camp
materials, instructional documents, and practice-round recordings — ==for a
private team coach AI==, preview which materials a question would draw on,
and ==ask the coach AI a real question grounded strictly in those materials==.

Open it from the **Coach Materials** tab of [Coaching](/docs/features/ai-coaching#coaching).

### What it shows [#coach-materials-what-it-shows]

An upload form (kind, title, topic, tags, material text), a shortcut for
uploading a document or recording from your microphone, a search and filter
bar, materials grouped by kind, and an =="Ask the coach" section that keeps
your conversation history==.

Above the question, a **Coaching style** picker chooses how the coach answers:
Standard (a direct answer), Socratic (leads with questions), Devil's Advocate
(argues the other side) or Fact-Checker (flags claims that need evidence).
==Every style still answers only from your materials==. Answers given in a
style other than Standard are labelled with it in the conversation (for
example "Socratic coach"). ==The picker remembers your last style==: it is
saved in this browser and, when you're signed in, to your account, so it
starts there next time on any device. It is remembered separately from AI
Coach Mode's picker.

==Materials are saved to your account with full version history==, and a
==reviewer approval step== (pending, approved or rejected) decides which
materials can ground an answer.

### Good to know [#coach-materials-good-to-know]

- ==Uploaded audio and video recordings are not transcribed==.
- Word (`.docx`) ==upload only works in the browser==.
- Your conversation history syncs to your account, but ==it is still
  yours alone, not shared with the team==.

## AI Coach Mode [#coaching-sessions]

==Shows every saved AI Coach Mode coaching session==, grouped by round and
side — ==extension, refutation, collapse, and weighing prompts drawn from
that round's flow== — with a **Get AI feedback** action for open-ended AI
coaching feedback.

Open it from the **AI Coach Mode** tab of [Coaching](/docs/features/ai-coaching#coaching).

### What it shows [#coaching-sessions-what-it-shows]

Each coaching session ==is its own card with badges for its kinds of prompt==
(Extension, Refutation, Collapse, Weighing). An **AI feedback** section
==shows the feedback you generated or a "Get AI feedback" button==. Each card has a
Download action, ==a History timeline (with Restore)==, and a view for comparing
two sessions.

### Coaching style [#coaching-sessions-coaching-style]

Above the session cards, ==**Coaching style for AI feedback** picks how the AI
coach talks to you== the next time you choose **Get AI feedback**:

- **Standard** — a direct debrief: what to prioritise next time and why.
- **Socratic** — ==leads with questions== so you work out the fix yourself.
- **Devil's Advocate** — ==argues the other side== to stress-test your strategy.
- **Fact-Checker** — ==flags claims that need evidence== and what kind of source
  would back them; it does not invent facts or statistics.

Every style critiques the arguments, never the debater. Feedback from a
styled coach shows a badge such as **Socratic coach**, and the downloaded
coaching notes name the style in the AI Feedback heading.

==The picker remembers your choice==: the next time you open AI Coach Mode it
starts on the style you last picked. When you're signed in, the choice is saved
to your account, so it follows you to every device (it syncs with your other
tool data, see **Settings → General → Account sync**). Signed out, it is
remembered in this browser only.

### Saving [#coaching-sessions-saving]

==Coaching sessions sync to your account when you are signed in==. Signed
out, ==they stay in this browser only==.

## Prediction Markets [#prediction-markets]

==Bet play-money points on who wins a debate, who wins a tournament, and whose
team rating goes up==. ==Every account starts with== **1,000 points**. Points
are play money: ==they can't be bought, sold or cashed out==, and they have ==no
value outside the site==.

Find it at **/practice/predictions**.

### What it shows [#prediction-markets-what-it-shows]

- **Your points.** Your balance. ==The 1,000 starting points land the first
  time you open the page while signed in==. ==Guest accounts can watch== but get no
  points.
- **Top 5 teams.** Pick a division (Public Forum, Lincoln-Douglas, Policy,
  College Policy) and ==bet on whether each of its five highest-rated teams
  gains team rating by the end of the month==. The site opens these itself,
  ==a fresh set each month==, and ==they settle themselves at month end==.
- **Major tournaments.** For the same division, ==a winner market for each of
  the season's fifteen biggest tournaments still ahead==, in date order:
  Heart of Texas, Florida Blue Key, Apple Valley, Glenbrooks, Princeton,
  Blake, Harvard-Westlake, Emory, Stanford, Harvard, Cal, the NDT (College
  Policy only), NDCA, the TOC and NSDA Nationals. Each lists the division's
  ==eight top-rated teams plus "Anyone else"==. ==Betting closes when the
  tournament starts==, and ==a moderator settles it from the published results==.
  Dates not yet published are the tournament's usual weekend, marked
  "expected dates".
- **More open markets**: ==the ones members opened, closing soonest first==, filterable by type. Each market
  lists its outcomes with ==the current price as a percentage (the market's
  implied odds)==, how many points are staked, how it settles, and your
  position. ==The bet form quotes how many shares a stake buys==, what it pays if
  that outcome wins, and where it moves the price.
- **Open a market.** Three kinds:
  - **Debate**: ==who wins one round==. Type the two sides in, or pick an
    undecided round from a tournament hosted on the site.
  - **Tournament winner**: ==type the field in (up to 32 entries)==, or pick an
    event hosted on the site and every entry becomes an outcome.
  - **Rating move**: pick a team from the team rankings. The market asks
    ==whether its rating is higher at close than it is now==.
- **Settled**: ==recent results, with what you got back==.
- **Top forecasters**: ==the ten biggest balances==, shown as ranks and points only (no names; your own row reads "You").

### How it works [#prediction-markets-how-it-works]

**Pricing.** ==Each market has an automated market maker==, so ==a bet never
waits for someone to take the other side==. You stake whole points and receive
shares; ==each share of the winning outcome pays 1 point==. ==Buying an outcome
raises its price==, and the price you see is the market's current estimate of
that outcome's chances. The market maker also caps how many points any one
market can pay out beyond what was staked.

**Settling.** ==Payouts are rounded down once per person, not per bet==.
==Voiding a market refunds every stake==. A market settles one of these ways:

- **A round hosted on the site**: ==every judge on the round casts one
  vote== for the entry their ballot marks as the winner. ==Once all judges have
  voted, the majority wins==. ==A bye, a forfeit or an even split voids it==.
  A result that comes in stops the betting, even before betting was due to close.
- **A tournament hosted on the site**: ==the first-ranked entry in the
  published final results== (the bracket, or results labelled final, champion,
  places or elims). ==Prelim seeds don't count==.
- **Rating move**: once betting closes, ==the team's current rating is
  compared to the rating recorded when the market opened==. Higher resolves
  "rises"; equal or lower resolves "doesn't rise". A team that has left the
  rankings voids it, and a rating market settles "doesn't rise" ==when the
  rankings aren't refreshed during its month==.
- **Typed-in markets** ==are settled by hand==: by a moderator or
  admin, or by the creator ==while they hold no position in that market==.
  Moderators can also settle or void any linked market whose results never
  arrive.

==The site's own markets never reopen once settled==, and since they have no
creator, ==only moderators settle the tournament ones==. ==You get a
notification when a market you bet on settles or is voided==.

**Fair betting.** ==If someone else bets first and moves the price while you
are placing a bet, your stake is refunded and you are asked to try again at the
new price==, and ==a market only ever pays out once==.

**Limits.** ==You need a real (non-guest) login to bet or open a market==.
==Each account can have ten markets open at once==. Betting closes between five
minutes and a year after a market opens.

### Good to know [#prediction-markets-good-to-know]

- ==You can't sell a position back before the market settles==.
- Markets on rounds and tournaments that aren't hosted on the site (live
  Tabroom, round videos) ==must be typed in and settled by hand==.
- ==Rankings only change when the site publishes refreshed rankings==, so a
  rating market can only move then.
- ==Markets settle when someone next opens the predictions page==, not at an
  exact time.
- ==A wallet never refills==. Someone who loses every point can't bet again.

### Markets the site opens [#prediction-markets-markets-the-site-opens]


==Every Monday the site opens a rating market== ("Will … gain rating?") on the top five teams in the Lincoln-Douglas and Public Forum rankings, ==each settling after 28 days==. It also opens "Who wins …?" markets for the season's biggest tournaments (Florida Blue Key, Glenbrooks, Barkley Forum, Harvard and Cal), ==listing the current top eight plus "Someone else"==. Betting closes when the tournament starts and a moderator settles it from the final results. ==A team or tournament that already has an open market isn't opened twice==.

## Coaching [#coaching]

==One page for a coach's tools==. ==Six tools that used to have their own pages==
and sidebar entries sit side by side, one tab each:

| Tab | What it does |
| --- | --- |

### Sample data [#coaching-sample-data]

**Load sample data**, at the top of the page, ==fills the Coach Workspace with
a demo built from real cards== in the card database: ==opponent team and judge
scouting records== with their profiles, and ==a sample round flowed from those
cards== (aff cards as 1AC arguments, neg cards answering them). The sample
round is selected while the page is open, so ==every "Generate … for current
round" action works on it==.

==The sample stays in this browser and is never saved to your account==. It is
added beside your own records and never replaces one, and **Clear sample
data** ==removes only the sample==. The sample round is never saved with your
flows: ==it leaves the round workspace when you leave the page==.

### Old addresses [#coaching-old-addresses]

Each tool used to have its own page. ==Those addresses still work and
open the matching tab==.

### How it works [#coaching-how-it-works]

==The page only groups the tools==: coaching sessions, programs, calendar items
and coach materials carry over unchanged and ==keep syncing to your account==.
==The open tab is part of the address==, so a tab can be shared or bookmarked,
and ==it is remembered in this browser for the next visit==.

The Coach Workspace tab ==keeps its own stage strip== (Flow, Coaching, Prep,
Scouting, Practice). Links from before the page had tabs ==still open the Coach
Workspace on the right stage==.
