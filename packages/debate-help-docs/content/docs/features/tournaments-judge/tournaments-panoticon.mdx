## Application Paragraph

A debate app in which students monitor one another can be understood through Foucault’s account of panopticism. His statement that “it does not matter who exercises power” suggests that observation need not remain exclusively in the hands of a teacher: students can become observers by reviewing classmates’ rounds, checking evidence, and assessing arguments. When students know their work may be inspected at any time, the possibility of observation could encourage them to regulate their own behavior, reflecting the Panopticon’s production of “conscious and permanent visibility.” The app could also make this scrutiny reciprocal by allowing students to evaluate their reviewers’ feedback and judgments, corresponding to Foucault’s description of a system that permits people to “observe any of the observers.” Yet this connection is also a caution: peer monitoring could turn collaborative debate education into continuous judgment, encouraging conformity or anxiety rather than intellectual experimentation; the app would redistribute observation without necessarily escaping the power relations observation creates.
