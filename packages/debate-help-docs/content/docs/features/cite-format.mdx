## Debate format [#debate-format]

==Every card is a tag, a cite, and the highlighted text== — in that order, so a judge can read the claim, the source, then the warrant.

| Part | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tag** | The claim the evidence proves, one sentence | "Civil disobedience deserves legal recognition as organized, public dissent." |
| **Cite** | Who, what, when — a short `Smith 24` in the round, the full entry in the doc | "Arendt, *The New Yorker*, September 12, 1970." |
| **Highlight** | The exact words read aloud | "THINGS of this world are in so constant a flux that nothing remains long in the same state." |
| **Underlining** | The part the author leans on hardest, a subset of the highlight | "change … is inherent in a world inhabited and established by human beings" |
| **Descriptor** | Who the author is, for the qualified-source check | "Political theorist; Professor, University of Chicago" |
| **Stats** | Highlighted / underlined / total words, and quote count | 13 / 13 / 56,407 / 144 |

==The short cite in a speech is author and year; the full cite lives in the document==. In the round you say "Smith 24"; on the page the reader sees the whole Chicago entry. [Read mode](/docs/features/research#cardmirror-read-mode) shows only the tag, the short cite and the text you would read aloud.

## Chicago style rules [#chicago-style]

The papers are authored in **Chicago notes-bibliography** style (the "chaicho" style): ==the year sits near the end, after the publisher or periodical==, and a bibliography entry is built from four blocks — byline, title, source, link.

- **Byline:** `Last, First`, then `and First Last`, then `et al.`; add `ed.` / `eds.` for an editor. `Carlin, Diana, and David Levasseur`.
- **Year:** near the end, not behind the name. `… <em>The New Yorker</em>, September 12, 1970.`
- **Title:** a part of something (article, chapter) takes quotes with the period inside — `“Civil Disobedience.”`; a whole work (book, journal) is italic — `<em>Rules for Radicals</em>`.
- **Source:** a journal is `<em>Journal</em> 132, no. 8 (2023): 243–55`; a chapter is `In <em>Book</em>, edited by X, 145–60. Publisher`; a site is `<em>Site</em>, July 29`.
- **Link:** the bare URL is Chicago's last element, ending with a period.
- **Original year:** a reprint adds `Originally published 1785.` at the end.

The References page keeps the Chicago HTML on each entry, so the [citation style switcher](/docs/papers) can re-render the same entry as APA 7, MLA 9 or Harvard without rewriting the source.

## Research Paper refDoc standard [#refdoc-standard]

==The refDoc standard is the shape of every References page== in the [Synergy Slayer papers](/docs/papers) and the Collective Consciousness paper: a numbered list of Chicago entries, each one a collapsible card.

- **`<details><summary>`** holds the full Chicago entry; the reader expands it for the card.
- **The card** opens with a one-paragraph summary of the claim and warrant, then the author descriptor in italics, then the stats line.
- **The quote** keeps the debate highlighting: `<u>` for underlined words, `<mark>` for the highlighted words, `[…]` for elisions.
- **The count table** at the top totals highlighted words, underlined words, total words and quotes across the page.
- **In-text citations** link to the matching entry, and a hover card shows the author, year and source.

A page that follows the standard renders the **Underlining** toggle, **Read aloud**, hover citation cards and the style switcher with no extra work.

## Quote introduction words [#quote-introduction-words]

==These are common words that introduce a cite== — the verb that frames a quotation so it reads as an argument, not a dropped quote. Pick the one that matches how the author is speaking: neutral reporting (*says, notes, reports*), interpretation (*argues, contends, maintains*), or emphasis (*stresses, underscores, highlights*).

Says, states, writes, notes, explains, argues, reports, suggests, observes, points out, emphasizes, maintains, contends, describes, asserts, comments, remarks, indicates, claims, concludes, highlights, stresses, clarifies, outlines, holds, mentions, adds, acknowledges, warns, cautions, proposes, predicts, summarizes, details, illustrates, underscores, elaborates, examines, interprets, characterizes, questions, challenges, counters, disputes, criticizes, rejects, defends, advocates, insists, concedes, recognizes, admits, reasons, infers, finds, determines, estimates, anticipates, expects, speculates, posits, hypothesizes, conjectures, objects, draws attention to, calls attention to.

### Examples from the Synergy Slayer papers

- Hannah Arendt **argues** that civil disobedience, rooted in America's associational traditions, deserves legal recognition as organized, public dissent.
- Saul Alinsky **writes** that "with very rare exceptions, the right things are done for the wrong reasons."
- Nick Bostrom **posits** that at least one of three propositions must be true, one of which is that we are almost certainly living in a computer simulation.
- Michael Albert **observes** that "in the absence of insight into future possibilities, growing awareness of the scale of oppressions and their tenacity breeds cynicism and not resistance."
- Roland Bleiker **contends** that every social order "excludes what does not fit into their view of the world."
- Rav Arora **notes** that "the rising tide of political correctness has thwarted honest discussion about the centrality of culture in shaping life outcomes."
- Ken Booth **maintains** that emancipation, not power or order, produces true security.
- Maya Bodnick **warns** that high school debate "increasingly [becomes] a site of radicalization."

Match the verb to the author's stance: use **argues** or **contends** when the source is making a claim you will weigh, **notes** or **observes** for neutral description, and **stresses** or **underscores** when the quoted line is the author's own emphasis.
