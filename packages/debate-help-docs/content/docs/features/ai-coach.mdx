## LLM Card Scoring [#llm-card-scoring]

==Scores a card on relevance, clarity, uniqueness, evidence quality and
usability==, ranks your cards, flags likely duplicates and can ask AI for a
written assessment. Paste several cards separated by `---` to score them in
bulk. Find it at **/research/cards/scoring**.

## AI Response-Outcome Charts [#response-outcome-charts]

## Scout-to-Strategy Workflow [#scout-to-strategy]

==Turns opponent scouting and judge profiles into a case choice and risk

## AI Outcome Responses [#ai-outcome-responses]

In a live round, open the ⋮ menu next to the speech timer and choose **AI
Outcome Responses**. ==It reads the round so far and suggests three ways to
give your current speech==. Each one shows:

- ==an estimated chance of winning==, with the best bet marked;
- a speech-ready outline you can copy;
- the opponent's likely answers and how the judge would likely vote;
- what could lose it, and card searches to fill evidence gaps.

You can change the judge paradigm or add a focus ("go for the DA") and
regenerate. ==The last run for each speech is saved with the round==: reopen
the dialog to see it again (with when it ran, the judge and your focus)
without waiting for a new one, and **Regenerate** replaces it. Signed in, it
follows your account to other devices. The win chances are AI estimates, not
predictions checked against real ballots.

## How Team Ratings Are Computed [#bradley-terry-debate-rankings]

The [Team Rankings](/docs/features/team-collaboration#team-rankings) rate
every team from ==every decided ballot of the season at once==, so beating
strong opponents counts for more than beating weak ones, and the order rounds
happened in doesn't matter. The number is on the familiar **Elo scale**
(1500 is an average team, 400 points is 10-to-1 odds), but it is ==not a
round-by-round Elo updater==: there is no starting rating that moves by a
K-factor after each round, and no decay. The header tooltip on the **Rating**
column is the short version; this is the full one.

- ==Aff and neg are balanced==, so a team isn't credited for drawing the
  favored side.
- Each judge's ballot counts on its own (a 3–0 beats a 2–1), and ==major
  tournaments count double==. Byes are skipped.
- ==Teams with few rounds are pulled toward average==, so one hot weekend
  can't top the table.
- Ratings show on a ==0.0–9.9 scale==; the whole number is the team's tier,
  shaded gold at the top down to gray.

### Where the number comes from [#rating-sources]

The Rating column has two sources, and which one you see depends on the list:

- **Extinction.GG's Elo** (the default for PF, LD, Policy and college policy,
  every season from 2015-16): the site reads each team's `OverallELO`, takes
  ==450 points off== it, and applies the display scale below. Extinction.GG
  computes that Elo upstream from Tabroom results and openCaselist; ==its
  internals (K-factor, decay and so on) are not in this site's code==, so they
  are not described here. Ranks are the order of `OverallELO`, ties broken by
  TOC bid count and then school name.
- **The bundled ballot fit** (the `debate-rankings` project's `src/main.py`):
  used for the current season's aff/neg win rates, round counts and aff
  advantage on every list, for the rating itself on the Sep–Oct LD slice,
  and as the fallback if Extinction.GG can't be reached. It is the model
  described in the rest of this section.

### From Tabroom rounds to ballots [#rating-ballots]

For each tournament in a format's config, every round file (prelims and elims)
becomes rows of *aff team, neg team, winner*. A row counts when it names a
decided winner (`Aff`/`Pro` or `Neg`/`Con`) for two real entries. Rows with a
==bye== or a blank entry, and rows with no winner, ==are skipped==; a
forfeit is not detected specially, so one counts like any other win if Tabroom
lists a winner. ==Margins and speaker points aren't used==.

A row with panel votes (a `Votes` column such as `Aff Neg Aff`) adds one
ballot per judge; otherwise it adds one ballot for the winner. Tournaments the
config lists as **majors** (for HS LD: Heart of Texas, Glenbrooks, Greenhill,
Emory, Cal, Harvard, Harvard-Westlake, NDCA and TOC) multiply every ballot by
two. Eliminations are weighted the same as prelims, with ==no extra
multiplier== for later rounds; they are only split out for the Aff/Neg Elim
win rates (a round is an elim if its file name says runoff, double, triple,
quad, octo, quarter, semi or final, or it has `Judges` and `Votes` columns).

$$
n^{\mathrm{aff}}_k = w_k\,a_k, \qquad n^{\mathrm{neg}}_k = w_k\,b_k, \qquad w_k = \begin{cases} 2 & \text{major tournament} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
$$

Here $$a_k$$ and $$b_k$$ are the aff and neg ballots of round $$k$$ (from the
panel's votes, or 1 and 0 / 0 and 1 from the winner).

Entries are matched across tournaments by a hash of school and name (LD) or
school and the sorted pair of debaters (team formats). A short list per format
of debaters or partnerships who switch schools are matched by name alone.

### The model [#rating-model]

Every team $$i$$ gets one skill $$\lambda_i$$ on the log-odds scale, and the whole
field shares one **aff side effect** $$\beta$$. With team $$i$$ on aff against $$j$$:

$$
p_k = P(\text{aff wins round } k) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\lambda_i - \lambda_j + \beta)}}
$$

The skills and $$\beta$$ are the ==penalized maximum-likelihood fit== to all ballots,
found by Fisher scoring (`BradleyTerry(use_bias=True, l2=1.0)` from the
`fisher-scoring` package). The penalty is a ridge (a standard-normal prior)
on every skill and on $$\beta$$, so a team with few ballots is pulled toward
the field average and an unbeaten team's rating stays finite:

$$
(\hat\lambda, \hat\beta) = \arg\max_{\lambda,\beta} \; \sum_k \Bigl[ n^{\mathrm{aff}}_k \ln p_k + n^{\mathrm{neg}}_k \ln (1 - p_k) \Bigr] - \frac{\tau}{2} \Bigl( \beta^2 + \sum_i \lambda_i^2 \Bigr), \qquad \tau = 1
$$

The fitted skills are centered so they sum to zero across the teams that
played a decided round.

### From skill to rating [#rating-elo-scale]

Skills are converted to the Elo scale, where 400 points is a factor of 10 in
odds. The **deviation** is the skill's standard error (from the inverse of the
penalized Fisher information) on the same scale:

$$
R_i = 1500 + \frac{400}{\ln 10}\,\hat\lambda_i, \qquad D_i = \frac{400}{\ln 10}\,\mathrm{SE}(\hat\lambda_i)
$$

A team with no decided round is at the prior: skill 0 and standard error 1,
so $$R = 1500$$ and $$D \approx 173.7$$. The **adjusted rating** subtracts two
deviations, so a team with a short record has to earn its place:

$$
A_i = R_i - 2\,D_i
$$

There is ==no minimum-rounds cutoff==. Instead the deviation does the work: a
team with 3 rounds has a wide $$D$$ and sinks, one with 30 has a narrow one.
Rounds played is shown as the **Matches** count (aff plus neg rounds with a
decided winner).

### Ranks, ties and the 0–9.9 scale [#rating-sort]

Ranks are $$A_i$$ sorted high to low (the **Rank** column of the CSV). The site
then shifts and scales it for display and clamps it to 0 through 99:

$$
S_i = \min\!\Bigl(99,\ \max\!\Bigl(0,\ \frac{A_i - 1000}{8.2/1.2}\Bigr)\Bigr), \qquad \text{shown as } S_i / 10
$$

so $$S = 96$$ prints as **9.6**. The whole number is the tier. The clamp is
only for display: when you sort the Rating column, teams tied at the 9.9 cap
are ordered by their unclamped value, strongest first. Sorting any other
column leaves ties in rank order.

### Expected score [#rating-expected-score]

Because the ratings are on the Elo scale, the chance that $$i$$ beats $$j$$,
ignoring side, is the usual Elo expected score:

$$
E_{ij} = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_j - R_i)/400}}
$$

The fitted aff effect is published as the format's **Aff Rating
Advantage** $$\Delta_{\mathrm{aff}}$$ (about 45 points in the example below), added to whoever is
aff:

$$
E^{\mathrm{aff}}_{ij} = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{-(R_i - R_j + \Delta_{\mathrm{aff}})/400}}, \qquad \Delta_{\mathrm{aff}} = \frac{400}{\ln 10}\,\hat\beta
$$

The head-to-head **matchup simulator** is separate: it starts from the
site-scale ratings, undoes the display scale, and softens the gap by the
opponent's uncertainty, as Glicko-2 does (it then adds side and school effects
of its own):

$$
P(a \text{ beats } b) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\!\bigl(-g(\varphi_b)\,(\mu_a - \mu_b)\bigr)}, \qquad g(\varphi) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 3\varphi^2/\pi^2}}
$$

### Worked example [#rating-worked-example]

A made-up PF tournament with three teams (not a major), run through the real
`main.py`:

| Round | Pro | Con | Result | Ballots (pro–con) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Alpha | Beta | Pro wins | 1–0 |
| 1 | Gamma | *bye* | skipped | none |
| 2 | Beta | Gamma | Pro wins | 1–0 |
| 3 | Gamma | Alpha | Con wins | 0–1 |
| Final | Alpha | Beta | panel Pro, Con, Pro | 2–1 |

At a major, the final would be 4–2 and every other round would count 2. The
fit gives:

| Team | Rounds | $$R$$ | $$D$$ | $$A = R - 2D$$ | $$S$$ | Shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alpha | 3 | 1596.0 | 94.9 | 1406.2 | 59.4 | **5.9** |
| Beta | 3 | 1505.9 | 94.9 | 1316.1 | 46.3 | **4.6** |
| Gamma | 2 | 1398.1 | 111.4 | 1175.4 | 25.7 | **2.6** |

Gamma's bye is not a round, so it has one fewer ballot and a wider deviation.
Alpha is first in rank order. Alpha's side-neutral chance against Beta is
$$1 / (1 + 10^{-90.1/400}) \approx 62.7\%$$ (their gap is $$1596.0 - 1505.9$$), and
with Alpha on aff and $$\Delta_{\mathrm{aff}} = 45.0$$ it is
$$1 / (1 + 10^{-135.1/400}) \approx 68.5\%$$.

### What is not used [#rating-not-used]

- ==No K-factor, starting-rating drift or time decay==: all ballots of the
  season are fit together, so the order of rounds and tournaments doesn't
  matter. Ratings start over each season.
- ==No margins, speaker points or opponent records==, only who won each ballot.
- ==No minimum number of rounds==; the deviation penalty handles thin records.
- ==Forfeits are not special-cased==; only byes and blank entries are skipped.

## Coach Materials [#coach-materials]

==Upload lecture transcripts, camp files, documents and recordings, then ask a
private coach AI questions answered only from them==. Pick a coaching style
(Standard, Socratic, Devil's Advocate or Fact-Checker); your choice is
remembered. A reviewer approves which materials can be used. Open it from the
**Coach Materials** tab of [Coaching](#coaching).

- ==Audio and video uploads aren't transcribed==.

flow==, one card per session, with **Get AI feedback** for an open-ended

## Prediction Markets [#prediction-markets]

==Bet play-money points on who wins a round, who wins a tournament, and whose
team rating goes up==. Every account starts with **1,000 points**; points
==can't be bought, sold or cashed out==. Find it at **/practice/predictions**.

- **Site markets**: each month the site opens rating markets on each
  division's top five teams, and winner markets for the season's biggest
  tournaments.
- **Open your own**: a debate round, a tournament winner (up to 32 entries)
  or a team's rating move.
- ==Prices are the market's implied odds== and move as people bet; each
  winning share pays 1 point.
- Rounds and tournaments hosted on the site settle from their results; typed-in
  markets are settled by a moderator or by the creator if they hold no
  position. Voided markets refund every stake.

Good to know:

- You need a real (non-guest) account to bet, and can have ten markets open.
- ==You can't sell a position early, and a wallet never refills==.
