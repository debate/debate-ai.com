## Power, Freedom, and Emancipation

### 1. Power and Resistance

**If power shapes our identities, desires, and standards of truth, where can resistance obtain its independence?**

- Pro: Freedom does not require escaping all power relations; it requires making them contestable, reversible, and less dominating.
- Con: Without a standpoint independent of existing power, resistance cannot explain why its preferred arrangements represent liberation rather than another form of domination.

### 2. The Foundations of Critique

**Can we identify oppression without defending an account of justice or the good life?**

- Pro: Critique can expose contradictions, exclusions, and intolerable suffering without establishing universal principles for everyone.
- Con: Condemning domination presupposes a distinction between legitimate and illegitimate power; critique must explain and defend that distinction.

### 3. Consent and Socially Produced Desires

**If institutions shape what people want, can their acceptance legitimate those institutions?**

- Pro: Social influence does not eliminate agency; people can reflect on their desires and revise their commitments.
- Con: Consent cannot provide independent justification when the institutions seeking legitimacy shape the preferences through which it is granted.

### 4. Emancipation and New Domination

**Can liberation avoid creating its own norms, exclusions, and authorities?**

- Pro: Institutions can distribute authority, protect dissent, and establish norms without making people subject to arbitrary power.
- Con: Every emancipatory project establishes boundaries between acceptable and unacceptable conduct, empowering some practices while suppressing others.

## The State, Sovereignty, and Political Authority

### 5. Sovereignty and Liberation

**Can the authority needed to transform society avoid becoming a new source of subjection?**

- Pro: Public authority can restrain private power, redistribute resources, and establish institutions through which people govern themselves.
- Con: Sovereignty preserves the capacity to impose decisions, define political membership, and suppress resistance, even when exercised in liberation’s name.

### 6. Authority and the Right of Refusal

**Is the ability to influence government sufficient for legitimacy, or must people also be able to reject its authority?**

- Pro: Equal participation, accountability, and protection against domination can legitimate authority without an individual right to refuse it.
- Con: Participation cannot substitute for consent when people remain subject to an authority they cannot realistically escape or reject.

### 7. Political Membership and Exclusion

**Can a political community exist without constituting an excluded outsider?**

- Pro: Political membership can remain open, overlapping, and revisable without depending on an enemy or a permanently excluded population.
- Con: Every community must determine who participates in binding decisions; even an inclusive order draws boundaries around political authority.

### 8. The Authority to Declare an Emergency

**Can emergency powers be legitimate when authorities themselves determine when ordinary constraints no longer apply?**

- Pro: Emergency judgment can remain legitimate when subject to independent review, strict limits, and public accountability.
- Con: The power to declare ordinary constraints inadequate allows authorities to determine the limits of their own authority.

## Capitalism, Ideology, and Transformation

### 9. Reform and Structural Transformation

**Can reforms transform an unjust system rather than make its domination more sustainable?**

- Pro: Reforms can redistribute ownership, reduce dependence, and build collective capacities until the underlying relations are substantially transformed.
- Con: Reforms remain constrained by the system’s reproduction and can renew its legitimacy without overcoming its organizing logic.

### 10. Judging Revolutionary Change

**Can a transformation that changes what counts as rational, possible, and legitimate be justified by existing standards?**

- Pro: Transformative action must remain answerable to evidence, foreseeable consequences, and public justification.
- Con: Existing standards may encode the very order being challenged; requiring their approval can exclude fundamental change in advance.

### 11. Participation and Complicity

**Does knowingly participating in an unjust system create responsibility for its reproduction when withdrawal is difficult or impossible?**

- Pro: Awareness creates obligations to resist, reduce, or redirect one’s contribution, even when complete withdrawal is impossible.
- Con: Responsibility should track meaningful choice and actual power, not merely unavoidable involvement in collective structures.

### 12. Resistance and Systemic Reproduction

**Can opposition undermine a system while remaining within the political roles that system makes available?**

- Pro: Opposition can build solidarity, institutions, and political capacities that exceed the system’s ability to absorb them.
- Con: Accepted forms of opposition can provide symbolic release and substitute managed dissent for a challenge to the organization of power.

## Security, Threat, and Survival

### 13. Material Danger and Threat Construction

**Can a threat be established independently of assumptions about whose survival matters and what counts as dangerous?**

- Pro: Although interpretation is unavoidable, evidence can establish credible causal dangers independently of particular political narratives.
- Con: Identifying the threatened subject, relevant evidence, and acceptable response already constitutes a political construction of security.

### 14. Security and Emancipation

**Should security be judged by the freedom it makes possible rather than the political order it preserves?**

- Pro: A stable order can remain deeply oppressive; preserving institutions is insufficient unless doing so expands people’s freedom.
- Con: Freedom depends on a sufficiently stable order, while making security serve a contested emancipatory ideal can legitimate coercive transformation.

### 15. Survival and Political Freedom

**Can the pursuit of survival undermine the freedom that makes survival politically valuable?**

- Pro: An unconditional survival imperative can reduce people to populations to be managed and cast dissent as an unacceptable risk.
- Con: Protecting people against violence and deprivation can enable freedom rather than displace it; the problem is unaccountable power, not survival itself.

### 16. Catastrophe and Uncertainty

**Can catastrophic possibilities justify coercive action when their probabilities cannot be reliably established?**

- Pro: Uncertainty does not remove responsibility; waiting for reliable probabilities may eliminate the possibility of prevention.
- Con: Speculative catastrophe cannot provide an unrestricted warrant for coercion, especially when the harms of intervention are more certain.

## Knowledge, Discourse, and the Obligations of Critique

### 17. Objectivity and Power

**Can political knowledge be objective if the categories through which it is produced are shaped by power?**

- Pro: Historically situated concepts can still support reliable judgments through evidence, criticism, and comparison.
- Con: Standards of reliability may reproduce the same exclusions as the categories being tested, making claims of neutrality politically misleading.

### 18. Discourse and Harm

**Can the denial of recognition or equal standing constitute harm without producing further material consequences?**

- Pro: Degradation and exclusion can wrong people in the act itself, even without measurable downstream consequences.
- Con: Harm requires more than a contested interpretation of recognition; otherwise disagreement risks becoming indistinguishable from injury.

### 19. Universalism and Difference

**Can a project claim to liberate everyone without imposing one conception of how people should live?**

- Pro: Universal emancipation can protect the conditions for plural ways of life rather than prescribe a single ideal.
- Con: Even those conditions embody substantive values; imposing them on dissenters reproduces the authority universal emancipation claims to overcome.

### 20. Critique and Practical Responsibility

**Must an emancipatory critique show how people can act differently here and now?**

- Pro: Critique owes those facing oppression more than diagnosis; it must identify some meaningful possibility for action.
- Con: Critique can transform what becomes imaginable, while requiring immediate feasibility can shield existing institutions from fundamental challenge.
