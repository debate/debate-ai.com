title: "Public Policy Questions Topic Choice"
---

Thirty questions for public issue debates, grouped into ten policy areas. Each area lists the past NDT and high school policy topics it grows out of, then gives the one resolution that best captures the area's core clash. The questions under it are the deeper disagreements a round on that resolution usually turns on.

## Nuclear Policy

Past topics: Nuclear Weapons (2010), WMDs (2002), Nuclear Restraint (2024).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States should adopt a policy of no first use of nuclear weapons.

### 1. Deterrence and Restraint

**Does keeping the option to use nuclear weapons first make major war less likely?**

- Pro: Ambiguity about first use forces adversaries to doubt that any conventional or chemical attack could succeed, which keeps large wars from starting.
- Con: A first-use option makes every crisis more dangerous, because each side has a reason to strike before the other can, and allies gain little that conventional forces cannot provide.

### 2. Arms Control and Verification

**Are arms control treaties worth signing when cheating can never be fully ruled out?**

- Pro: Even imperfect inspections reveal intentions, cap arsenals, and build channels that prevent accidents and misreadings during crises.
- Con: Treaties bind the states that keep their word while rivals exploit gaps, leaving the honest party weaker at the moment it most needs strength.

### 3. Nonproliferation and Fairness

**Can nuclear-armed states credibly demand that others never build the bomb?**

- Pro: Every new nuclear state multiplies the chances of theft, accident, and use, so stopping the spread is justified even if the current order is unequal.
- Con: A rule that only binds the weak looks like domination, and it will hold only if the nuclear powers make real progress toward disarming themselves.

## Energy and Renewables

Past topics: Fossil Fuels (2005), Clean Energy (2009 and 2025), Energy Production (2013).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States federal government should substantially increase its investment in nuclear and renewable electricity generation.

### 4. Nuclear Power in a Clean Grid

**Should nuclear power be central to replacing fossil fuels?**

- Pro: Nuclear plants supply steady, carbon-free power around the clock on a small land footprint, filling the gaps that wind and solar leave.
- Con: New reactors cost too much and take too long to build, and the same money spent on renewables and storage cuts more emissions sooner.

### 5. Subsidies and Market Choice

**Should government pick which energy technologies win?**

- Pro: Markets ignore the costs of pollution and climate change, so targeted support is the only way to scale clean technology fast enough.
- Con: Subsidies lock in today's favorites, invite lobbying, and crowd out better options that a simple carbon price would let compete fairly.

### 6. Energy Independence

**Does producing more energy at home make a country more secure?**

- Pro: Domestic supply shields families and the military from price shocks and from foreign governments that use oil and gas as leverage.
- Con: Energy is traded on world markets, so domestic production barely changes prices, and the push for independence often means more drilling, not more security.

## Climate and Environment

Past topics: Ocean Protection (2004), Climate Policy (2017), Water Protection (2022), Ocean Exploration (2015).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States federal government should enact a carbon tax.

### 7. Who Pays for Climate Change

**Should the countries that polluted most in the past pay the most to fix climate change?**

- Pro: Wealthy nations built their economies on emissions that now harm poorer nations, so fairness requires them to carry most of the cost.
- Con: Most future emissions will come from developing economies, and a plan that asks little of them cannot stop warming.

### 8. Growth and the Planet

**Can economies keep growing while cutting emissions fast enough?**

- Pro: Many countries already grow while their emissions fall, and cheap clean energy makes that split easier every year.
- Con: The cuts so far are too slow, and real safety requires using less, not just switching fuels, which means limiting growth in rich countries.

### 9. Protecting Nature for Its Own Sake

**Do oceans, rivers, and species deserve protection even when it costs people jobs or development?**

- Pro: Ecosystems have value beyond what they do for us, and losing them is permanent in a way that economic setbacks are not.
- Con: Policy must answer to the people who live with it, and protection that ignores local livelihoods loses public support and fails.

## Healthcare

Past topics: Mental Health (2003), African Health (2008), Healthcare (2018), National Health Insurance (2027).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States federal government should establish a single-payer national health insurance program.

### 10. Health Care as a Right

**Does government owe every resident health care?**

- Pro: Illness strikes without regard to merit, and no one can plan for it, so a decent society guarantees care to all.
- Con: Calling care a right does not create doctors or medicine, and guarantees without limits lead to waiting lists and rationing by other means.

### 11. Single Payer and Choice

**Is one public insurer better than a market of competing plans?**

- Pro: A single payer cuts paperwork, bargains down drug prices, and ends the fear of losing coverage with a job.
- Con: Competition pushes insurers and providers to improve, and a single plan leaves patients nowhere to go when it fails them.

### 12. Mental Health and Coercion

**Should the state be able to require treatment for people with serious mental illness?**

- Pro: Some people in crisis cannot recognize their own need for help, and leaving them untreated on the street is not respect for freedom.
- Con: Forced treatment has a long record of abuse, and voluntary, well-funded care does more good without stripping people of their rights.

## Foreign Policy and Alliances

Past topics: Sanctions (2000), Europe (2004), China Relations (2006), Middle East (2008), Arab Spring (2012), Latin America (2014), China Engagement (2017), Alliances (2021), NATO Technology (2023).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States should substantially reduce its security commitments to its allies.

### 13. Alliances and Free Riding

**Do security alliances make the United States safer or pull it into other countries' wars?**

- Pro: Alliances deter attacks before they start, keep trade routes open, and spread the cost of defense across many partners.
- Con: Guarantees let allies underspend on their own defense and tie American decisions to conflicts it has no vital stake in.

### 14. Engagement or Pressure

**Do sanctions change the behavior of hostile governments?**

- Pro: Sanctions raise the cost of aggression without war and bring rivals to the table, as nuclear talks have shown.
- Con: Sanctions mostly hurt ordinary people, let leaders blame outsiders, and rarely topple or reform a regime.

### 15. Promoting Democracy Abroad

**Should foreign policy aim to spread democracy?**

- Pro: Democracies rarely fight each other and make steadier partners, so supporting them serves both values and security.
- Con: Imposed democracy often brings chaos, and pursuing it abroad costs credibility that stable deals with any government would preserve.

## Military Power and War

Past topics: Military Presence (2011), War Powers (2014), Military Withdrawal (2016), Arms Sales (2020), UN Peacekeeping (2005), Arms Transfers (2027).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States federal government should substantially restrict its arms sales to foreign governments.

### 16. Who Decides on War

**Should the president be able to use military force without the approval of Congress?**

- Pro: Threats move faster than legislatures, and a commander who must wait for a vote cannot protect the country in time.
- Con: War is the gravest choice a nation makes, and letting one person make it alone invites endless conflicts no one voted for.

### 17. Arms Sales and Influence

**Do weapons sales buy influence or fuel conflict?**

- Pro: Selling arms ties partners to the seller, gives leverage over how weapons are used, and keeps rivals from filling the gap.
- Con: Weapons outlast the deals that sold them, end up used against civilians, and implicate the seller in abuses it cannot control.

### 18. Humanitarian Intervention

**Is military force justified to stop atrocities in another country?**

- Pro: Standing aside during mass killing is a choice too, and force can save lives when nothing else will.
- Con: Interventions are chosen by interest, not need, and often leave the country worse off and the intervener stuck for years.

## Education

Past topics: Education (2000), National Service (2007), Education Funding (2018).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States federal government should substantially increase its funding and regulation of elementary and secondary education.

### 19. School Choice

**Should public money follow students to the school their family chooses?**

- Pro: Choice lets families escape failing schools, and competition pushes every school to improve.
- Con: Vouchers drain funds from public schools that must serve every child, while private schools can pick their students.

### 20. Testing and Accountability

**Should schools be judged by standardized test scores?**

- Pro: Common tests reveal which students are being left behind and hold schools responsible for teaching them.
- Con: Tests narrow teaching to what is measured and punish schools for poverty they did not cause.

### 21. Free College

**Should public college be free for everyone?**

- Pro: A degree is now as basic as a high school diploma once was, and tuition shuts out talented students who cannot afford it.
- Con: Free tuition mostly helps families who would pay anyway, while targeted aid would do more for the students who need it.

## Immigration

Past topics: Immigration (2011 and 2019).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States federal government should substantially increase legal immigration.

### 22. Open Borders and Self-Determination

**Does a nation have the right to decide who may enter it?**

- Pro: Communities must be able to shape their membership to keep the trust and shared rules that make democracy work.
- Con: Birthplace is luck, and keeping people from safety or work because of where they were born is hard to justify.

### 23. Immigration and Workers

**Does more immigration hurt the wages of native-born workers?**

- Pro: Immigrants fill labor shortages, start businesses, and grow the economy in ways that raise wages overall.
- Con: Gains flow to employers and wealthy consumers, while workers in the same jobs as new arrivals face more competition.

### 24. Enforcement and Humanity

**Should immigration enforcement focus on the border or inside the country?**

- Pro: Strong border control lets the country be generous with legal entry, because the public trusts that the rules are kept.
- Con: Walls push migrants toward deadlier routes, and fixing the legal system would reduce illegal crossing more than enforcement.

## Criminal Justice and Civil Liberties

Past topics: Privacy (2001), Civil Liberties (2006), Supreme Court (2007), Legalization (2015), Surveillance (2016), Criminal Justice (2021).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States federal government should substantially reform its criminal justice system.

### 25. Punishment or Rehabilitation

**Should prisons aim mainly to punish or to rehabilitate?**

- Pro: Most prisoners come home, and programs that prepare them to work and live lawfully protect future victims best.
- Con: Justice requires that serious harm carry serious consequences, and victims are owed punishment, not only programs.

### 26. Surveillance and Security

**Should government be able to collect data in bulk to prevent attacks?**

- Pro: Patterns across large data sets can reveal plots that targeted searches miss, and oversight courts can guard against abuse.
- Con: Mass collection treats everyone as a suspect, chills free speech, and has rarely stopped an attack that ordinary police work would not.

### 27. Drug Legalization

**Should drug use be treated as a health issue rather than a crime?**

- Pro: Arrests ruin lives and fill prisons without lowering use, while treatment and regulation reduce overdoses and violence.
- Con: Legal markets normalize use, and commercial sellers have every reason to create more addicts, as tobacco and opioids have shown.

## Economy, Labor, and Technology

Past topics: Farm Subsidies (2009), Poverty (2010), Infrastructure (2013), Antitrust (2022), Redistribution (2024), Intellectual Property (2025), Labor Rights (2026).

**Best resolution:** Resolved: The United States federal government should substantially increase protections for workers' right to organize.

### 28. Redistribution and Opportunity

**Should government reduce inequality directly, or focus only on reducing poverty?**

- Pro: Large gaps in wealth turn into gaps in political power and opportunity, so inequality itself is a problem to fix.
- Con: What matters is whether the poorest can live well; taxing success to narrow gaps can shrink the economy everyone depends on.

### 29. Unions and Labor Power

**Should the law make it easier for workers to form unions?**

- Pro: Individual workers cannot bargain with large employers on equal terms, and unions build the middle class and a voice at work.
- Con: Unions can protect insiders at the expense of new workers and consumers, and modern labor law already protects basic rights.

### 30. Big Tech and Antitrust

**Should the largest tech companies be broken up?**

- Pro: A few platforms control speech, commerce, and data, and breaking them up restores competition and democratic control.
- Con: Size lets these firms offer free services and fund research, and breaking them up would hurt users while rivals abroad grow.
