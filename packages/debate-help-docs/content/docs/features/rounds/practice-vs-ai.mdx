# Practice vs AI

![Practice vs AI — the opponent picker, a live timed round, and the scorecard](https://i.imgur.com/2awjrUp.png)

==Lets a debater run a full timed round against an AI opponent==. ==Setup is five
steps==: **difficulty** (Easy through Legends), **topic** (with your side and
whether the round uses evidence), **opponent** (one of the 13 personas at that
difficulty), **prep** (the opponent finds cards and cases and summarizes both
sides) and **arguments** (==you tick exactly which arguments you run and which
the opponent runs==). After that ==you debate through opening statements,
cross-examination and closing statements, then get an AI-judged scorecard==.

==The page opens on the current high-school policy topic==, "Resolved: The
United States federal government should establish national health insurance in
the United States", ==with you on the affirmative running a single-payer plan
(Medicare for All)== and ==the opponent running the disads against it== by
default: Spending/Debt, Pharma Innovation, Rural Hospitals and Politics. The
Arguments step also offers the aff's advantages (Uninsured Deaths, Cost
Control, Medical Debt, Pandemic Preparedness, Health Equity), the States and
Public Option counterplans, a Wait Times case turn and T on "establish". Pick
Neg and the sides swap: the opponent reads single payer and you pick the
disads.

==You can write your own resolution==. A custom topic ==defaults to a round
without evidence==, since the card library may have nothing on it; the
arguments then come from the opponent's prep brief, and you can add your own
on the Arguments step.

==This page is also where the== **Practice Round Simulator** lives now: ==its saved rounds, judge paradigms,
replays and team matchups are in the collapsed== **Round simulator** section
below the round (see [Practice Round Simulator](/docs/features/rounds/practice-round-simulator)).

Find it at **/practice/versus-ai**. ==You don't need to sign in to play a
round==; signing in saves your rounds to Debate History and earns points and
badges for them.

## What it shows

A five-step setup (Difficulty, Topic, Opponent, Prep, Arguments), the live
round (==two side-by-side panels with countdowns, typed or dictated turns==),
and a scorecard (==each phase scored out of 10 for both sides, totals, a
verdict, and follow-up drill recommendations==). Per-phase clocks sit in a
collapsed "Phase timings" box on the Arguments step. During the round, ==the
prep brief and the arguments each side chose stay one click away== in a
collapsed panel above it.

After you reveal the decision, the scorecard offers a ==**Coach debrief**==:
pick a coaching style (==Standard, Socratic, Devil's Advocate or
Fact-Checker==, the same styles as AI Coach Mode) and select **Get coach
debrief**. ==An AI coach reads the judge's scores and reasons and your own
speeches, and names the two or three things that would have won you the most
points==, with one thing to practise for each. ==The style you pick is
remembered==, and follows your account when you're signed in. The debrief needs
AI features (sign in) and isn't saved with the round; select **Get a new
debrief** to ask again, in the same or another style.

While you type or dictate a turn, ==possible fallacies (ad hominem, straw man, false dichotomy) are flagged under the box== with a one-line explanation. A hint is a prompt to look closer, not a verdict; it runs offline in your browser and nothing is stored or sent.

## Judge feedback style

Above the round, a **Judge feedback style** picker sets how the judge words the
scorecard: ==Standard, Socratic (leads with questions), Devil's Advocate (argues
the other side to stress-test your strategy) or Fact-Checker (flags claims that
need evidence)== — the same coaching styles as AI Coach Mode and "Ask the
coach". ==The style only changes the wording of the reasons and the verdict
notes; the rubric, the scores and the winner stay the same.== A styled
scorecard says so at the top ("Feedback in the Socratic style"). ==Your pick is
remembered==, and when you're signed in ==it follows your account to other
devices==, separately from the style you chose for the other coaches.

## With and without evidence

**With evidence** (the default for the national health insurance topic), prep
searches the card library and caselist, and ==the opponent cites those cards by
author and year during the round==. It is told never to invent a citation that
isn't in the list. **Without evidence** skips the search, and ==both sides argue
analytically==; the opponent is told not to cite or invent any source.

## How the opponent debates

Every bot turn carries the round plan: the aff plan, the arguments you chose,
the arguments it runs, and (with evidence) its cards. ==The opponent is told to
debate like a tournament competitor==: signpost each argument, run disads as
uniqueness, link, internal link and impact, do impact calculus, answer your
arguments line by line and point out drops, and ask pointed cross-ex
questions. As the negative ==it announces how many off-case positions it is
reading== ("4 off") and goes through each one. ==When you speak first, it sees
your opening and answers it== rather than giving a speech of its own in a
vacuum. Its persona still sets the tone, and turns are capped at 250 words.

## The opponent's prep

In an evidence round the Prep step ==searches the card corpus with the topic's key words== (filler
words such as "should" and "be" dropped) and, separately, the caselist
outlines. ==Up to eight cards and four outlines go to the AI==, which writes a
brief: a summary, then ==two to four arguments per side, each citing the cards
that back it==. When the AI can't be reached, or its reply can't be read, ==the
brief is built from the
topic's preset arguments, or for a custom topic from the cards alone==: ==each card's tag becomes an argument on the side the corpus says
read it==. ==A card search that is rate-limited or fails counts as finding
nothing, so prep still produces a brief==.

A "View Debate History" button on the setup screen opens **Debate History**:
==every past round (topic, opponent, result, date), newest first==, each
==expandable in place to its full transcript==. A round's link in Settings →
Saved data ==opens the history with that round already expanded==.

Above the list, **Fallacy hints across your speeches** runs the same fallacy
hints as the turn box over ==every speech you sent in those rounds==: the
total, hints per 1,000 words, a count per kind (ad hominem, straw man, false
dichotomy) and the speeches with the most hints. ==The opponent's speeches
are left out==. Nothing extra is saved — it is ==recounted from your saved
rounds each time you open the history==, so it covers up to the 50 most recent
rounds the history shows. Like the turn-box hints, it flags phrasing worth a
second look, not a verdict.

## Which AI it uses

==Your own OpenRouter or Anthropic key when you've added one== in Settings →
General, otherwise the site's own AI. ==If one AI service fails, the next one is
tried==. With no working AI at all, ==each persona answers with an in-character
"my systems are offline" line==.

## Score, badges and streak

==Your score and badges are saved to your account== and shown on the scorecard.
==Your streak goes up by one for a round on the day after your last one==, ==stays
the same for a second round on the same day==, and ==restarts at 1 after a missed
day== — so ==five days running earns the Streak5 badge==. ==Reloading mid-round
resumes the same round== instead of dropping back to the picker.

~~The prep brief is not yet fed into the opponent's turns.~~ **Fixed:** ==the
arguments chosen on the Arguments step, and the cards in an evidence round, go
to the opponent on every turn==. The brief and the chosen arguments are kept
with the in-progress round in the browser only, so ==they are not part of
Debate History==. ==Only the national health insurance topic has preset
arguments so far==; other topics take their arguments from the prep brief.
==The judge still scores the four phases, not the flow==: it doesn't know which
arguments were chosen.

The Coach debrief is shown once and not saved: it isn't in Debate History, an
export, or the account, and the coach only sees the scorecard and your
speeches, not the opponent's.
