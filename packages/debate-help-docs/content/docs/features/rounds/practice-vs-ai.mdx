# Practice vs AI

![Practice vs AI — the opponent picker, a live timed round, and the scorecard](https://i.imgur.com/2awjrUp.png)

==Lets a debater run a full timed round against an AI opponent==. ==Setup is five
steps==: **difficulty** (Easy through Legends), **topic** (with your side and
whether the round uses evidence), **opponent** (one of the 13 personas at that
difficulty), **prep** (the opponent finds cards and cases and summarizes both
sides) and **arguments** (==you tick exactly which arguments you run and which
the opponent runs==). After that ==you debate through opening statements,
cross-examination and closing statements, then get an AI-judged scorecard==.

==The page opens on the current high-school policy topic==, "Resolved: The
United States federal government should establish national health insurance in
the United States", ==with you on the affirmative running a single-payer plan
(Medicare for All)== and ==the opponent running the disads against it== by
default: Spending/Debt, Pharma Innovation, Rural Hospitals and Politics. The
Arguments step also offers the aff's advantages (Uninsured Deaths, Cost
Control, Medical Debt, Pandemic Preparedness, Health Equity), the States and
Public Option counterplans, a Wait Times case turn and T on "establish". Pick
Neg and the sides swap: the opponent reads single payer and you pick the
disads.

==You can write your own resolution==. A custom topic ==defaults to a round
without evidence==, since the card library may have nothing on it; the
arguments then come from the opponent's prep brief, and you can add your own
on the Arguments step.

==This page is also where the== **Practice Round Simulator** lives now: its old
address, `/practice`, redirects here, and ==its saved rounds, judge paradigms,
replays and team matchups are in the collapsed== **Round simulator** section
below the round (see [Practice Round Simulator](/docs/features/rounds/practice-round-simulator)).

- **Route:** `/practice/versus-ai` (`/practice` redirects here)
- **Package:** `debate-practice-vs-ai`, composed with `debate-practice-rounds`
  by `debate-webview`'s `PracticeVsAiSections`
- **API:** `POST /api/vsbot/create`, `/prep`, `/debate`, `/judge`, `/concede`;
  `GET /api/vsbot/history`; prep also reads `GET /api/search`

## What it shows

A five-step setup (Difficulty, Topic, Opponent, Prep, Arguments), the live
round (==two side-by-side panels with countdowns, typed or dictated turns==),
and a scorecard (==each phase scored out of 10 for both sides, totals, a
verdict, and follow-up drill recommendations==). Per-phase clocks sit in a
collapsed "Phase timings" box on the Arguments step. During the round, ==the
prep brief and the arguments each side chose stay one click away== in a
collapsed panel above it.

## With and without evidence

**With evidence** (the default for the national health insurance topic), prep
searches the card library and caselist, and ==the opponent cites those cards by
author and year during the round==. It is told never to invent a citation that
isn't in the list. **Without evidence** skips the search, and ==both sides argue
analytically==; the opponent is told not to cite or invent any source.

## How the opponent debates

Every bot turn carries the round plan: the aff plan, the arguments you chose,
the arguments it runs, and (with evidence) its cards. ==The opponent is told to
debate like a tournament competitor==: signpost each argument, run disads as
uniqueness, link, internal link and impact, do impact calculus, answer your
arguments line by line and point out drops, and ask pointed cross-ex
questions. As the negative ==it announces how many off-case positions it is
reading== ("4 off") and goes through each one. ==When you speak first, it sees
your opening and answers it== rather than giving a speech of its own in a
vacuum. Its persona still sets the tone, and turns are capped at 250 words.

## The opponent's prep

In an evidence round the Prep step ==searches the card corpus with the topic's key words== (filler
words such as "should" and "be" dropped) and, separately, the caselist
outlines. ==Up to eight cards and four outlines go to== `POST /api/vsbot/prep`,
which asks the model for a brief in a fixed JSON shape: a summary, then ==two
to four arguments per side, each citing the cards that back it==. When no model
key is set, or the model's reply can't be read, ==the brief is built from the
topic's preset arguments, or for a custom topic from the cards alone==: ==each card's tag becomes an argument on the side the corpus says
read it==. ==A card search that is rate-limited or fails counts as finding
nothing, so prep still produces a brief==.

A "View Debate History" button on the setup screen opens **Debate History**:
==every past round (topic, opponent, result, date), newest first==, each
==expandable in place to its full transcript==. A `?debate=<id>` link (from
Settings → Saved data) ==opens the history with that round already expanded==.

## How it works

==A port of the Go== `arguehub` vs-bot server and its Vite/React client (the
React client is kept as a reference source in the package; the Go server has
been removed). ==The 13 personas carry over field for
field== from the Go `GetBotPersonality` switch. Text generation goes through write-language, like `/api/reason-ai`: ==your own
OpenRouter or Anthropic key when you've added one== in Settings → General,
otherwise the site's `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, then `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, then
`GEMINI_API_KEY`. ==If one key fails (revoked, out of credit) the next one is
tried==, so a bad OpenRouter key no longer turns every turn into a canned line.
With no working key, ==each persona answers with an in-character "my systems are
offline" line==.
