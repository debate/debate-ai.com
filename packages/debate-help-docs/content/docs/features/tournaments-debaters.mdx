## Practice Drills [#drill-sets]

==Overview, frontline, cross-ex and collapse drills built from a round's
flow==, one set per round. Each drill has a difficulty, an optional AI script,
a **Mark practiced** toggle and a review reminder. Drill sets sync to your
account. Find them in the Practice Drills tab of **Practice Debates**.

## Judge Profiles [#judge-profiles]

==A roster of judges you've logged: side bias, average speaker points, speed
tolerance, theory receptiveness and paradigm==. Log rounds one at a time or
paste a CSV, and use **Compare judges** to prep for a panel. Open it from the
**Judge Profiles** tab of the [Prep Workspace](/docs/features/rounds#prep-workspace).

- ==Ballots aren't pulled in automatically==; you enter them yourself.

## Opponent Team Profiles [#opponent-team-profiles]

==Scouting records for opponent teams: overall and side record, and the
arguments and cases they run most==. Log rounds by hand or by CSV, download a
report, or **Compare vs. opponent** to see shared and unique arguments. Open
it from the **Opponent Team Profiles** tab of the
[Prep Workspace](/docs/features/rounds#prep-workspace).

## Debate formats & rules [#formats-and-rules]

==A one-page reference for the common high-school debate formats and the
evidence and conduct rules students need to know==.

<Callout type="warn" title="Important">
  ==This is a practical NSDA-style guide==. Tournaments, state associations and leagues may use different times,
  procedures or AI policies. ==Always follow the rules of your specific tournament==.
</Callout>

The official source is the NSDA **High School Unified Manual**, updated each season:
[Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1hq7-DE6ls2ryVtOttxR4BNpRdP7xUbBr0M3SMYefek8/edit?tab=t.0) or
[2026–27 PDF](https://s3.amazonaws.com/tabroom-files/tourns/40557/postings/71429/HighSchoolUnifiedManual202627.pdf).

### Formats at a glance [#formats-and-rules-formats-at-a-glance]

<RuleGrid>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Two-person teams" title="Policy Debate (CX)" summary="A policy-resolution event built around detailed research, comparative consequences, and technical argument interaction.">
    - **1AC:** ==plan, advantages, and evidence==
    - **Negative:** disadvantages, counterplans, topicality, critique, and case debate
    - **Rebuttals:** ==collapse to decisive voting issues==
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Individual" title="Lincoln-Douglas (LD)" summary="A value-judgment debate centered on ethics, philosophy, frameworks, and direct comparison of principles.">
    - **Framework:** ==value and standard==
    - **Contentions:** reasons to affirm or negate
    - **Rebuttals:** ==answer, weigh, and crystallize==
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Two-person teams" title="Public Forum (PF)" summary="A current-events debate designed for accessible, evidence-based persuasion and clear impact comparison.">
    - **Constructives:** introduce contentions
    - **Crossfires:** direct questioning
    - **Summary / final focus:** ==narrow and weigh the ballot story==
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Individual chamber" title="Congressional Debate" summary="A legislative simulation where delegates debate bills and resolutions through repeated floor speeches and questions.">
    - **Authorship:** open and explain legislation
    - **Floor speeches:** affirmative and negative advocacy
    - **Procedure:** questioning, motions, amendments, voting
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Individual" title="Big Questions (BQ)" summary="A research debate connecting science, ethics, and philosophy; clarity and source quality matter heavily.">
    - **Definitions:** frame the question
    - **Contentions:** empirical and ethical claims
    - **Weighing:** ==compare evidence, implications, and principles==
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Teams" title="World Schools / Parliamentary / BP" summary="Motion-based formats emphasizing fast analysis, direct refutation, teamwork, and persuasive comparative argument.">
    - **Case:** define and defend the motion
    - **POIs:** brief questions in many variants
    - **Replies / whips:** ==summarize clash and comparative reasons to win==
  </FormatCard>
</RuleGrid>

### Evidence rules [#formats-and-rules-evidence--citation-rules]

- ==Evidence is a fact, statistic or example attributed to a specific,
  authoritative source==. Unattributed claims are your own arguments.
- **Cite it:** say at least the author's last name and year aloud, and be ready
  to give the full citation (author, date, outlet, title, URL, access date,
  qualifications, pages).
- **Oral delivery:** Oral delivery of each piece of evidence must be
  identified by a clear oral pause or by saying phrases such as
  "quote/unquote" or "mark the card." The use of a phrase is definitive and
  may be preferable to debaters. Clear, oral pauses are left solely to the
  discretion of the judge.
- **Mark what you read** and keep the marked document
  accurate. ==A paraphrase must be as accurate and producible as a quote==.
- **Share on request**: any evidence used in the round must be available
  promptly to the opponent or judge.

<RuleGrid>
  <RuleCard title="Distortion">
    ==Never add or delete words in a way that changes an author's conclusion==. ==Bracket words you add==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Nonexistent evidence">
    ==Never cite material you cannot produce== or text missing from the source.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Clipping">
    ==Do not skip words while representing that you read all highlighted text==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Outside help">
    Devices can be used for files, evidence exchange and research, but ==take no arguments or strategic help from
    non-participants during the round==.
  </RuleCard>
</RuleGrid>

<Callout type="error" title="Consequences">
  ==Minor citation issues may get evidence discounted==. Clipping, distortion or nonexistent evidence ==can cost the
  round, zero speaker points or disqualification==.
</Callout>

### Rules by format [#formats-and-rules-rules-by-format]

- **Policy:** the 1AC presents a plan; the neg answers with case, disadvantages, counterplans, topicality or
  critiques. ==Rebuttals should focus on what decides the ballot==.
- **Lincoln-Douglas:** a value-judgment resolution; debaters compare frameworks. Times: AC 6, NCX 3, NC 7, ACX 3,
  AR 4, NR 6, AR 3; 4 minutes prep each.
- **Public Forum:** ==no formal plans or counterplans==. Times: 4–4 constructives, 3 crossfire, 4–4 rebuttals, 3
  crossfire, 3–3 summaries, 3 grand crossfire, 2–2 final focus; 3 minutes prep per team.
- **Congress:** recognition by precedence and recency; authorship and first negative get 3 minutes plus 2 of
  questions, later speeches 3 plus 1. ==Amendments must be written and germane==.
- **Big Questions:** ==keep scientific findings separate from moral conclusions==, and explain technical claims
  plainly.
- **World Schools / Parliamentary / BP:** times, POIs and new-matter rules ==vary by circuit==; in BP, closing teams
  need a distinct extension.

### Pre-round checklist [#formats-and-rules-pre-round-checklist]

1. ==Read the invitation==: timing, topic, evidence rules and AI policy.
2. ==Verify every card==: tag, full citation, accurate highlighting, saved original.
3. Have a fast way to share evidence, a charger and a backup copy of files.
4. ==Ask procedural questions before the round starts==.

## Judge Awards [#judge-awards]

==Five badges only a judge can give a debater at a tournament==: Most Improved,
Best Speaker, Best Critique Debater, Best Impact Calculus and Best Research
Award. They appear on the debater's leaderboard page with the judge's name,
and **Give an award** is on that page.

- ==Each judge can give each award once per tournament==, and never to
  themself.
- The judge's name is typed in and isn't verified, and awards don't add XP yet.

## Pre-Round Briefings [#pre-round-briefings]

==One briefing per upcoming round: opponent scouting, judge tendencies,
head-to-head record and your team's prep notes==. Open it from the
**Pre-Round Briefings** tab of the [Prep Workspace](/docs/features/rounds#prep-workspace).
Scouting and pairings are entered by hand.

### Debater Stats [#pre-round-briefings-debater-stats]

The **Debater Stats** tab turns your logged rounds into ==win rates overall
and by side, division, argument and case, plus your current streak==. Click
**Log a round** to add one (tournament, date, division, side and result are
enough). Optional words spoken (or a linked saved flow) add a speech-length
comparison between wins and losses. Stats follow your account.

## Post-Panopticism Tournament Tabroom [#tournaments-panoticon]

==How Foucault's "Panopticism" helps explain a debate app where students both
observe their peers and are observed==. These are interpretations of the app,
not claims that Foucault endorsed it.

> "Consequently, ==it does not matter who exercises power==."

The observer need not be a teacher: students can review rounds, check
evidence and evaluate classmates' work.

> "Hence the major effect of the Panopticon: ==to induce in the inmate a state of conscious and permanent visibility that assures the automatic functioning of power==."

Students who know peers may review their arguments regulate their own
practice, even when nobody is looking.

> "This Panopticon ... ==also enables everyone to come and observe any of the observers==."

Scrutiny can be reciprocal, with reviewers' judgments open to review too. ==But
this is also a caution==: peer monitoring could turn collaborative debate
education into continuous judgment, encouraging conformity or anxiety rather
than experimentation.

Foucault, Michel. *Discipline and Punish: The Birth of the Prison*. Translated
by Alan Sheridan. New York: Vintage Books, 1995. Part 3, chap. 3,
"Panopticism."

## Tournaments [#tournaments]

==Tournament invitations, pairings and results without leaving the app==:
live Tabroom tournaments plus ones hosted here. Open **Tournaments** at the
top of **Practice Debates** in the sidebar (`/tournament`).

- **Upcoming tournaments**, searchable and filterable by state and circuit;
  **Results** switches to the last 60 days of results.
- **Invite**, **Pairings** and **Results** tabs for each tournament, at
  addresses like `/tournament/2026/yale-invitational/results`.
- ==Elimination rounds show a full bracket==; hover a debate for each judge's
  vote.
- ==Entries show as team labels with school logo, last names and rating==
  (blue border for Aff, red for Neg). Hover one for the team's stats card.
- ==Click any judge to see their paradigm and voting record== (see
  [Judge paradigms](#judge-paradigms)).

Pairings and results refresh at most once a minute, so reload during a
tournament.

### Submitting a ballot [#tournaments-submitting-a-ballot]

Judges file a ballot from the **Ballot** tab. ==You must be signed in with the
same email as your Tabroom account==. Choose **Debate AI only**, or
**Tabroom** to also send it to Tabroom with your Tabroom API token (not
saved).

### Hosting your own tournament [#tournaments-hosting-your-own-tournament]

**Host Tournament** (`/tournament/host`) ==creates a tournament on this site,
not on Tabroom==. Pick virtual, in-person or long-term online, and any mix of
formats: Policy, LD, Public Forum, Parliamentary, British Parliamentary, World
Schools, Asian Parliamentary, Big Questions, IPDA and Congress. Each format
becomes a division with its own entry cap, fee and invite note.

Owners get an admin view at `/tournament/<year>/<name>/admin` listing events,
schedule, entries, judges, rooms and results. Adding schools, rooms and
rounds from it isn't built yet.

The **Debate AI Demo Invitational** is a full-size demo anyone can browse as
its admin (**Demo admin** on the upcoming page). ==Anyone can edit its mock
data== (**Reset demo data** restores it), and its rooms play ==simulated live
rounds== with captions and summaries under **Tune in**.

## Training tools [#training-tools]

==Turn a round you've flowed into practice material==. Every tool lives in the
[Coach Workspace](/docs/features/ai-coach#coaching) and works on the
**current round**, the flow selected in the round workspace (`/debate`).

1. **Flow the round**, or open a saved one.
2. **Read it** in the *Flow* section: argument tree, summaries and exposure
   charts show what was dropped and where each side is weak.
3. **Generate training** in the *Coaching* section: prompts and drills
   specific to this round.
4. **Practice** and mark drills done.
5. **Prep the next round** in *Prep* and *Scouting*.

## Judge paradigms [#judge-paradigms]

==Click any judge in a tournament's pairings to see how they judge==: their
Tabroom paradigm (or the one they wrote and verified here), and their voting
record with Aff/Neg split and **squirrels** (panel rounds where they were the
lone dissent).

### Write your own paradigm [#judge-paradigms-write-your-own-paradigm]

Judges can write a paradigm at **`/practice/paradigm`**:

1. Write it in the editor and paste your Tabroom paradigm link.
2. ==Copy the code shown into your Tabroom paradigm==, save it there, and
   click **Verify**.

==Until you're verified, debaters see your Tabroom paradigm==, so nobody can
publish one under someone else's name.
