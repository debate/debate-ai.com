## Practice Drills [#drill-sets]

==Shows every drill set the AI Drill Generator made for you==, grouped by round —
==overview, frontline, cross-ex, and collapse-scenario prompts derived from
that round's flow==.

Find it in the Practice Drills tab of **Practice Debates**, in the sidebar's
**Practice Debates** section.

### What it shows

Each drill set shows as its own card with kind badges
(Overview/Frontline/Cross-Ex/Collapse) and a difficulty rating
(easy/medium/hard, ==derived from the argument's vulnerability score==). Each
drill has a "Get AI script" action, ==a "Mark practiced" toggle with a
progress meter==, and a "Review reminder" date field with a "Due" badge. A
"Practice tier" card ==feeds practiced-drill counts into the Progress
Unlocks tier system==.

### Saved to your account

==Every drill set — scripts, completion state, reminders — is synced to your
account across devices==.
Signed in, your synced drill sets are also listed under **Settings → General →
Saved to your account → Drill sets**, where each one can be opened or exported
as JSON. Opening one goes straight to that drill set: the card is scrolled
into view and outlined, or a note says it isn't on this device yet. Remove a drill set with **Clear** here: Settings offers no Delete for
drill sets, because a copy still on another device would sync it back.

### Known gaps

The "Practice tier" card ==counts only drills from this page==, not your
progress across every tool in Progress Unlocks.

## Judge Profiles [#judge-profiles]

==Shows every saved judge profile as a roster== — ==side-vote bias, average
speaker points, delivery-speed tolerance, theory receptiveness==, and
most-tagged paradigm — ordered by rounds judged.

Open it from the **Judge Profiles** tab of the [Prep Workspace](/docs/features/rounds#prep-workspace).

### What it shows

A roster table, a "Log a judged round" form (==creates/updates a profile from
ballot history==), a **Bulk import (CSV)** section ==for pasting a whole
spreadsheet of judged rounds at once==, a logged-rounds list with
Edit/Delete/Undo/Redo actions, and a **Compare judges** section ==for
prepping a panel round== (side leans, recommended pace, theory risk, paradigm
conflicts).

### Good to know

- ==Ballots aren't pulled in automatically== — ==every round is entered by hand==,
  one at a time or in a batch with Bulk import.
- Profiles and their logged rounds sync to your account when you're signed in,
  but ==they are yours alone, not shared with the team==.

## Debate formats & rules [#formats-and-rules]

==A one-page reference for the most common high-school debate formats==, their key round sections, and ==the
research, evidence, conduct, and format-specific rules students need to know==.

<Callout type="warn" title="Important">
  ==This is a practical NSDA-style guide==. Tournament invitations, state associations, and local leagues may use
  different speech times, procedures, internet rules, or AI policies. ==Always follow the rules governing your
  specific tournament==.
</Callout>

### The official NSDA rulebook [#formats-and-rules-the-official-nsda-rulebook]

The National Speech & Debate Association ==publishes its rules for every event in a single== **High School Unified
Manual**, ==updated each season==. ==It sets the official speech times and prep time== for Policy, Lincoln-Douglas,
Public Forum, World Schools and Congress, the evidence rules (what a citation must include, how to produce
original sources when asked, and ==what counts as distortion or fabrication==), the policies on electronic devices
and internet use in rounds, and the procedures for protests, judging and disqualification at NSDA districts and
Nationals. This guide summarizes those rules; ==when a detail matters, read the manual itself==, as a
[Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1hq7-DE6ls2ryVtOttxR4BNpRdP7xUbBr0M3SMYefek8/edit?tab=t.0) or the
[2026–27 PDF](https://s3.amazonaws.com/tabroom-files/tourns/40557/postings/71429/HighSchoolUnifiedManual202627.pdf).

### Formats at a glance [#formats-and-rules-formats-at-a-glance]

<RuleGrid>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Two-person teams" title="Policy Debate (CX)" summary="A policy-resolution event built around detailed research, comparative consequences, and technical argument interaction.">
    - **1AC:** ==plan, advantages, and evidence==
    - **Negative:** disadvantages, counterplans, topicality, critique, and case debate
    - **Rebuttals:** ==collapse to decisive voting issues==
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Individual" title="Lincoln-Douglas (LD)" summary="A value-judgment debate centered on ethics, philosophy, frameworks, and direct comparison of principles.">
    - **Framework:** ==value and standard==
    - **Contentions:** reasons to affirm or negate
    - **Rebuttals:** ==answer, weigh, and crystallize==
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Two-person teams" title="Public Forum (PF)" summary="A current-events debate designed for accessible, evidence-based persuasion and clear impact comparison.">
    - **Constructives:** introduce contentions
    - **Crossfires:** direct questioning
    - **Summary / final focus:** ==narrow and weigh the ballot story==
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Individual chamber" title="Congressional Debate" summary="A legislative simulation where delegates debate bills and resolutions through repeated floor speeches and questions.">
    - **Authorship:** open and explain legislation
    - **Floor speeches:** affirmative and negative advocacy
    - **Procedure:** questioning, motions, amendments, voting
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Individual" title="Big Questions (BQ)" summary="A research debate connecting science, ethics, and philosophy; clarity and source quality matter heavily.">
    - **Definitions:** frame the question
    - **Contentions:** empirical and ethical claims
    - **Weighing:** ==compare evidence, implications, and principles==
  </FormatCard>
  <FormatCard eyebrow="Teams" title="World Schools / Parliamentary / BP" summary="Motion-based formats emphasizing fast analysis, direct refutation, teamwork, and persuasive comparative argument.">
    - **Case:** define and defend the motion
    - **POIs:** brief questions in many variants
    - **Replies / whips:** ==summarize clash and comparative reasons to win==
  </FormatCard>
</RuleGrid>

### Research rules [#formats-and-rules-research-rules]

<RuleGrid>
  <RuleCard title="Use authoritative, traceable sources">
    ==Evidence is a fact, statistic, or example attributed to a specific, identifiable, authoritative source==.
    ==Unattributed claims are your own arguments—not evidence==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Keep source context">
    Save the original article, PDF, webpage, or relevant pages with surrounding text. ==A quote should not be
    separated from context that changes its meaning==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Build complete citations">
    Record author/editor, publication date, outlet, article title, URL, access date, qualifications when
    available, and page numbers when available.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Test every evidence card">
    ==Ensure the highlighted language proves the precise claim in the tag==. ==Separate the author’s conclusion from
    your own inference==.
  </RuleCard>
</RuleGrid>

<Callout type="success" title="Recommended workflow">
  Save the source → create the citation → write a precise tag → ==highlight only what you will read== → preserve
  nearby context → ==verify the card against its tag== → keep a shareable copy ready for the round.
</Callout>

### Evidence & citation rules [#formats-and-rules-evidence--citation-rules]

| Rule | What to do in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Oral citation | When introducing evidence, state at minimum the primary author’s last name and the publication year. For later quotations from the same source, the author name can be sufficient. |
| Written citation | Be ready to provide the complete citation upon request, including author, date, source/outlet, title, digital access date, URL if applicable, qualifications, and page numbers if available. |
| Mark what is read | Identify evidence orally with a pause or phrase such as “mark the card,” and visibly mark the exact text read or paraphrased in the written document. |
| Paraphrasing | A paraphrase must be as accurate and citable as a direct quotation. You must be able to produce the original and identify the supporting passage. |
| Evidence exchange | Any evidence, case, or citation used in the round must be made available promptly to an opponent or judge who requests it, in a readable format. |

<RuleGrid>
  <RuleCard title="Distortion">
    ==Never add or delete words in a way that materially changes an author’s conclusion==. ==Bracket words you add==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Nonexistent evidence">
    ==Never cite material you cannot produce==, text missing from the source, or a paraphrase without a verifiable
    original.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Clipping">
    ==Do not skip words while representing that you read all highlighted or underlined text==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Ellipses & straw arguments">
    Do not use prohibited internal ellipses, and do not attribute an author’s description of another view to the
    author as their own position.
  </RuleCard>
</RuleGrid>

<Callout type="error" title="Consequences">
  ==Minor citation or marking issues may cause judges to disregard or discount evidence==. Serious violations—such as
  clipping, distortion, or nonexistent evidence—==can cause a loss, zero speaker points, disqualification, or
  further tournament sanctions==.
</Callout>

### Devices, internet & AI [#formats-and-rules-devices-internet--ai]

<RuleGrid>
  <RuleCard title="Allowed uses under current NSDA-style rules">
    ==Internet-enabled devices can generally be used to retrieve files==, exchange evidence or arguments, conduct
    research, and communicate with a debate partner.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="No outside competitive assistance">
    ==Do not obtain arguments, strategic advice, cross-examination questions, or other competitive help== from
    coaches, non-competing students, or any non-participant during the round.
  </RuleCard>
</RuleGrid>

### Rules by format [#formats-and-rules-rules-by-format]

<FormatRules title="Policy Debate (CX)" badge="Two-person teams" summary="Policy debate asks whether the affirmative plan should be adopted. It rewards clean organization, evidence comparison, and direct responses across a long speech sequence.">
  <RuleCard title="Key round sections">
    - ==Affirmative presents a plan, advantages, and evidence in the 1AC==.
    - Negative answers with case arguments, disadvantages, counterplans, topicality, and/or critiques.
    - ==Cross-examination clarifies evidence and commits opponents to positions==.
    - ==Rebuttals must prioritize the arguments that decide the ballot==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Evidence & procedure">
    - ==Evidence read must be shareable upon request==.
    - Track arguments on the flow and answer claims in the appropriate later speeches.
    - Use the team’s prep time carefully; tournament timing rules control.
    - ==Do not receive outside coaching or strategic help during the round==.
  </RuleCard>
</FormatRules>

<FormatRules title="Lincoln-Douglas Debate (LD)" badge="Individual" summary="LD uses a value-judgment resolution. Debaters compare ethical frameworks as well as practical outcomes and empirical claims.">
  <RuleCard title="Key round sections">
    - Affirmative constructive: framework and contentions.
    - Negative constructive: counter-framework and offense.
    - Cross-examination: clarify definitions and expose contradictions.
    - Rebuttals: answer, weigh, and ==explain the decisive framework==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Core rules">
    - ==The resolution requires a value judgment==.
    - At NSDA-style competition, ==each contestant debates both sides across the tournament==.
    - Standard sequence: AC 6, NCX 3, NC 7, ACX 3, AR 4, NR 6, AR 3; 4 minutes prep per debater.
    - Evidence and device rules follow the shared standards above.
  </RuleCard>
</FormatRules>

<FormatRules title="Public Forum Debate (PF)" badge="Two-person teams" summary="PF emphasizes an accessible case, direct questioning, and a clear explanation of why a citizen judge should vote for one side.">
  <RuleCard title="Key round sections">
    - Constructives present each side’s contentions and evidence.
    - Crossfires permit direct questioning and clarification.
    - ==Summaries identify the important contested issues and begin weighing==.
    - ==Final focus gives the concise ballot story==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Core rules">
    - ==Formal plans and counterplans are not permitted==; teams should advocate pro or con through reasons, though generalized practical solutions are allowed.
    - ==Oral prompting while a speaker has the floor is discouraged== and may be penalized.
    - NSDA-style sequence: 4–4 constructives, 3 crossfire, 4–4 rebuttals, 3 crossfire, 3–3 summaries, 3 grand crossfire, 2–2 final focus; 3 minutes prep per team.
    - ==Partners may consult when they do not have the floor and during grand crossfire==.
  </RuleCard>
</FormatRules>

<FormatRules title="Congressional Debate" badge="Individual chamber" summary="Congress simulates legislative deliberation. Delegates combine research, responsive speeches, questioning, procedure, and civility.">
  <RuleCard title="Recognition & speeches">
    - ==Recognition follows precedence/recency==: not yet spoken, fewer speeches, then least recently spoken.
    - Authorship/sponsorship and the first negative receive up to 3 minutes plus 2 minutes of questions.
    - Later speeches are up to 3 minutes with 1 minute of questioning.
    - Direct questioning is used at national and district competition; recognized questioners have up to 30 seconds.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Procedure & evidence">
    - ==Amendments must be written==, identify the relevant lines/clauses, and ==be germane to the legislation’s intent==.
    - Major votes are counted; presiding-officer elections use secret ballots.
    - Evidence is verifiable. When challenged, ==provide requested evidence, citation, or original source within two speeches==.
    - Visual aids may be allowed, but cannot require electronic retrieval devices in the chamber.
  </RuleCard>
</FormatRules>

<FormatRules title="Big Questions Debate (BQ)" badge="Individual" summary="BQ explores large questions where science, ethics, and philosophy overlap. The strongest speeches make complex research understandable and comparable.">
  <RuleCard title="Key round sections">
    - ==Define essential terms and frame the central question==.
    - Present empirical, ethical, and/or philosophical contentions.
    - Use questioning to test methodology, causation, definitions, and implications.
    - ==Weigh the strongest evidence and the most important principles or consequences==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Core rules">
    - Use the same shared evidence, citation, availability, and device standards described above.
    - ==Clearly distinguish scientific findings from moral conclusions or policy inferences==.
    - ==Be ready to explain technical claims in language the judge can evaluate==.
    - Check the tournament invitation for the current topic, timing, and event-specific procedures.
  </RuleCard>
</FormatRules>

<FormatRules title="World Schools, Parliamentary & British Parliamentary" badge="Motion-based team formats" summary="These formats use different speaker orders by league, but they share a focus on short preparation, clear motion analysis, direct clash, and comparative persuasion.">
  <RuleCard title="Key round sections">
    - Opening government/affirmative defines and defends the motion.
    - ==Opposition directly refutes and offers a competing account of the motion==.
    - ==Later speeches extend the team case rather than merely repeat it==.
    - ==Reply or whip speeches compare the central clashes and explain the winning side==.
  </RuleCard>
  <RuleCard title="Core rules to confirm locally">
    - Speaker times, preparation time, points of information, and new-matter limits ==vary by circuit==.
    - In BP, ==closing teams must make a distinct, relevant extension== and whips generally do not introduce new substantive material.
    - In World Schools and many parliamentary variants, ==points of information are brief questions offered during substantive speeches==.
    - Always check the invitation for evidence, electronic-device, and source-citation expectations.
  </RuleCard>
</FormatRules>

### Pre-round checklist [#formats-and-rules-pre-round-checklist]

1. ==Read the current invitation== and confirm the event’s timing, topic, procedures, evidence rules, and AI policy.
2. ==Verify every card==: ==correct tag, complete citation, accurate highlighting, and saved original context==.
3. Download or locally save sources and ==prepare a fast, readable method for sharing evidence==.
4. Bring charging equipment, ==a backup copy of files==, and a backup timing plan.
5. ==Do not contact or accept in-round help from non-participants==.
6. ==Ask procedural questions before the round begins== whenever possible.

<Callout type="success" title="Best habit">
  ==Treat every evidence card as if an opponent will request it immediately==. If you can quickly show the complete
  source, exact passage, and accurate citation, ==you are ready to read it==.
</Callout>

## Judge Awards [#judge-awards]

==Five badges a debater can't earn with XP — only a judge can give them at a
tournament==. ==They appear on the debater's page with the judge's name under
each badge==.

A debater's page on the contribution leaderboard shows their earned awards and
the **Give an award** form. The Research Workspace's **Level** tab lists all five
awards and what they recognize.

### The awards [#judge-awards-the-awards]

| Award | Recognizes |
| --- | --- |
| Most Improved | Came back sharper every round over the course of a tournament |
| Best Speaker | The clearest, most persuasive delivery the judge heard |
| Best Critique Debater | Took apart the other side's assumptions, not just their arguments |
| Best Impact Calculus | Explicit weighing — magnitude, probability, timeframe |
| Best Research Award | Evidence deeper, fresher and better cut than anything else in the room |

### The rule [#judge-awards-the-rule]

==Each judge can give== **each award once per tournament**. A judge who gives
Best Speaker at Glenbrooks can't give Best Speaker to a second debater there,
but can still give the other four, and ==can give Best Speaker again at the
next tournament==. Judge and tournament names are matched ignoring case and
extra spaces. ==A judge can't give an award to themself==.

### Good to know [#judge-awards-good-to-know]

- **No judge verification.** ==The judge's name is typed in by whoever fills in
  the form==; ==nothing checks that they judged the tournament==.
- **Per-browser, not shared.** ==An award lives in the browser (and account) of
  whoever gave it==. The debater sees it on their page only on that device or
  account, ==not on their own==.
- **No XP.** The badge art shows +200 XP, but ==judge awards don't add to the
  debater's level yet==, for the same reason: the level lives in the debater's
  browser, while the award lives in the judge's.

## Pre-Round Briefings [#pre-round-briefings]

==Shows every saved pre-round briefing== — opponent
scouting, judge tendencies, head-to-head record, and team prep notes
==combined into one briefing per round==.

Open it from the **Pre-Round Briefings** tab of the [Prep Workspace](/docs/features/rounds#prep-workspace).

### What it shows [#pre-round-briefings-what-it-shows]

A "create briefing" form (round ID, tournament, division, side, optional
opponent/judge pick, prep notes), ==every saved briefing as its own card==,
a "Log a round" form for this team's own round history, a "Download"
plain-text export, ==a freshness ("pending N hours") badge==, and a manual
"Pairing schedule" section (account-synced across devices).

### Good to know [#pre-round-briefings-good-to-know]

==Tournament results and ballots aren't pulled in automatically== — ==scouting and
judge data is entered by hand==, and pairings have their own entry form ==until
live Tabroom pairings can be read==.

### Debater Stats [#pre-round-briefings-debater-stats]

Once you have logged rounds, the **Debater Stats** tab of the Prep Workspace
(also shown in the Coach Workspace's Prep section)
==turns them into your overall win rate, Aff and Neg win rates==, results by
division, by argument and by case name, ==your current win or loss streak==, and a recent-form trend (needs at least ten logged
rounds). ==Stats follow your account, like the rounds themselves==.

You can log a round **right in the Debater Stats tab**: click **Log a round**,
fill in the tournament, date, division, side and result, and click **Save
round**. The opponent, case, arguments run and words spoken are optional here.
(The Pre-Round Briefings form asks for an opponent because it builds the
head-to-head record. If you type an opponent's team code here that matches one
of your opponent profiles, the round still counts toward that head-to-head.)
The tournament, division and date stay filled in, so logging a whole
tournament's rounds takes a few clicks each.

Under the stats, **Logged rounds** lists your rounds newest first (five at a
time, with **Show all**). **Delete** removes a round you logged by mistake,
after you confirm. Your stats update right away and the round is removed
from your account too. If another device still has the round from before
the delete, it may add it back the next time that device syncs. Delete it
there as well.

If you fill in the optional **Words spoken** box when logging a round (or pick one of your saved flows
to count the words you flowed for the side you selected), a
**Speech length** section also ==shows your average words per round== and ==how that
average differs between rounds you won and rounds you lost==.

The optional **Arguments run** box ==takes a comma-separated list== (for example `Capitalism K, Topicality`).
Those tags ==feed the Debater Stats results by argument==.

When the word count came from a saved flow, ==the logged round keeps a link to that flow== and shows an
**Open flow** link in your logged-rounds list.

If one of your saved flows has a label that mentions the tournament you typed (for example "Blake Semis"
for the tournament "Blake"), ==the form offers a== **Use suggested flow** button. Clicking it ==counts the words
and links the flow in one step==. Flows that also mention the division, or the optional **Round** you typed (for example "R3"), rank first. ==The Round field only narrows the suggestion==; it is not saved with the logged round.

### Application Paragraph [#tournaments-panoticon-application-paragraph]

A debate app in which students monitor one another ==can be understood through Foucault’s account of panopticism==. His statement that “it does not matter who exercises power” suggests that ==observation need not remain exclusively in the hands of a teacher==: students can become observers by reviewing classmates’ rounds, checking evidence, and assessing arguments. When students know their work may be inspected at any time, ==the possibility of observation could encourage them to regulate their own behavior==, reflecting the Panopticon’s production of “conscious and permanent visibility.” The app could also ==make this scrutiny reciprocal== by allowing students to evaluate their reviewers’ feedback and judgments, corresponding to Foucault’s description of a system that permits people to “observe any of the observers.” Yet ==this connection is also a caution==: ==peer monitoring could turn collaborative debate education into continuous judgment==, ==encouraging conformity or anxiety rather than intellectual experimentation==; the app would ==redistribute observation without necessarily escaping the power relations observation creates==. ## Tournaments [#tournaments]

==Tournament invitations, pairings and results, without leaving the app==.
**Tournaments** at the top of **Practice Debates** (formerly Coaching) in the sidebar (`/tournament`) is this
app's own view, built with the site's theme: ==live Tabroom tournaments plus the
ones hosted here==. Tabroom's own site, framed, is still reachable directly at
`/practice/tabroom`, but ==no longer has a sidebar row==.

### Tournament addresses [#tournaments-tournament-addresses]

==Each tournament's pages live under the year it starts and its name==, so
the Yale Invitational's results are at `/tournament/2026/yale-invitational/results`
and its pairings at `/tournament/2026/yale-invitational/rounds`. Links that
name a tournament by its Tabroom number (`/tournament/38436/results`) ==still
work and settle on the named address once the tournament loads==; so do old
`/tournaments/…` and `/practice/tournaments/…` bookmarks, which redirect here.

==An address that doesn't match a page sends you to the nearest one that does==:
a mistyped section (`/tournament/2026/yale-invitational/nope`) goes back to
the tournament, and a tournament name that matches nothing goes back to
`/tournament`. Anywhere else on the site, ==a missing page sends you one level
up the address the same way==.

### What you can see [#tournaments-what-you-can-see]

- **Upcoming tournaments**, ==searchable by name, state, circuit or event==.
  Tournaments hosted on this site, marked **Hosted here**, ==come first, then
  live Tabroom's==. **Host Tournament** opens the creation form and **Demo
  admin** opens the demo tournament's admin view.
- **State and circuit filters**: the two dropdowns in the top bar narrow the
  list to ==one state or one circuit== (NSDA, TOC, NDCA…), and work together
  with the search box.
- **Results button**: ==swaps the upcoming list for past tournaments with
  published results from the last 60 days==, newest first. Each opens straight
  to that tournament's results; the state and circuit dropdowns filter it too.
  Click **Results** again to go back to upcoming tournaments.
- **Invite**: ==dates, location, registration deadline, events with entry counts
  and fees==, posted documents (each opens the file), contacts and the
  tournament's info pages.
- **Pairings**: ==every published round, grouped by event==. Opening a round shows
  ==each room's entries (sides or speaker order) and judges==. Rounds are named
  R1–R8, Triples, Doubles, Octas, Quarters, Semis and Final.
- **Results**: ==every published result set==, with places, entries, schools and
  the tiebreak columns Tabroom reports (wins, points, ranks…).

### Elimination bracket [#tournaments-elimination-bracket]

Opening any elimination round (or the **Bracket** button beside an event's
rounds) ==shows all of that event's elims in one bracket above the round's own
pairings==: a column per round, ==each debate lined up beside the one its winner
went on to==, the winner in bold with a check and the ballot count beside each
entry. ==The round you opened is highlighted==.

- Hover a debate (or tap it) for its details: the decision ("won 2–1 on the
  Neg"), ==each judge's vote and speaker points==, and a link to that round's
  pairings. ==Judge names there open their paradigm==, as they do in pairings.
- Click a round's heading ==to collapse it to a thin strip==, or use **Collapse
  rounds** to fold them all; the chevron by the title folds the whole bracket.
- When a debate has no ballots posted, ==the entry that turns up in the next
  round is shown as the winner==. Tournaments that only post pairings (no
  ballots) ==still get a bracket, just without counts==.

### Team ratings and judge paradigms [#tournaments-team-ratings-and-judge-paradigms]

On a round's pairings and on a result set, each policy, Lincoln-Douglas and
Public Forum entry ==is looked up in the current season's team rankings for its
own format==: a Public Forum entry only in the PF rankings, and so on. Policy
events that don't say whether they're high school or college are checked in
both. ==When exactly one ranked team matches== (the same debaters' last names, or
the same school and initials), ==the entry shows its school logo when the site
has one, the debaters' last names and its rating==; hover the rating for its rank.

A tournament from an earlier season ==is matched against that season, never
the current rankings==. College policy events (those whose name says NDT, CEDA,
college or university) are looked up in [debatedata](https://debatedata.weebly.com)'s
top-100 table for the season the tournament started in, 2009-10 onward, and
==show that season's rank and rating==; other past events show the entries as
Tabroom sends them.

==Every debate entry is drawn as a==
[team label](/docs/features/team-collaboration#team-rankings-team-labels)
instead of its bare code: the school (logo or name), then the debaters' last
names, or the code's initials when the tournament doesn't list them. On
pairings ==the label's border is blue for the Aff and red for the Neg==; results
use blue. ==Hover the label for the entry's Tabroom code==.

==Every judge's name in a pairing is clickable==. It opens their **paradigm** and
**voting record** over the pairings: rounds judged, how often they vote Aff or
Neg, ==how often they were the lone dissent on a panel==, and every published
round with their vote against the panel's. See
[Judge paradigms](/docs/features/tournaments-judge#judge-paradigms). A judge
whose pairing doesn't carry their Tabroom id (common on tournaments hosted
here) ==is found by first and last name, and only when exactly one Tabroom judge
has that name==. ==Under the paradigm, the overlay also shows the judge's page on
[extinction.gg](https://extinction.gg) (`extinction.gg/judges/<name>`)==, with a
link to open it in a new tab; a judge with no paradigm found still opens that
page.
- **Tabroom**: on a live Tabroom tournament, the last tab opens Tabroom's own
  page for it in a frame, ==so you can register or enter ballots there==.
- **Admin**: on a tournament hosted here, ==the last tab is its admin view
  instead== (see below).
- **Ballot**: ==judges submit a ballot for a round== (see below).

### Submitting a ballot [#tournaments-submitting-a-ballot]

The **Ballot** tab (`/tournament/2026/yale-invitational/ballot`) ==is where a
judge files a ballot==: event, round, room, the two entries, the winner (with a
low-point-win box), optional speaker points and the reason for decision.
Open it from the tab strip at the top of any tournament page and enter the
pairing there.

==You must be signed in==, and the **Tabroom account email** on the ballot ==must
match the email you are signed in to Debate AI with==. The form shows whether it
matches as you type and ==won't submit until it does==; ==the server checks again
and turns the ballot away if not==.

**Send to** ==picks where the ballot goes==:

- **Debate AI only** ==stores it here==. Your ballots for
  the tournament are listed under the form.
- **Tabroom** ==also sends it on to Tabroom using your own Tabroom API token==,
  ==which is passed along and never saved==. ==A copy is kept here either way==, marked
  with whether Tabroom accepted it. ==This option is greyed out until the site
  is connected to Tabroom's ballot service==; until then, use
  **Open my Tabroom ballots** to enter it on Tabroom directly.

==AI agents can submit the same ballot as a tool call== on the judge's behalf,
==with the same email rule==.

### Hosting your own tournament [#tournaments-hosting-your-own-tournament]

**Host Tournament** on the upcoming page (or `/tournament/host`)
==creates the tournament on this site's own tournament hosting==.
==Nothing is created on Tabroom==. You need to be signed in. Give it a name, dates,
where it is held, and ==pick the formats it runs==:

- **Virtual** — ==fully remote, rounds run online via video==.
- **In-Person** — ==at a single physical venue==.
- **Long-Term Online** — online, ==scheduled across multiple days or weeks==.

#### Formats [#tournaments-formats]

A tournament runs any combination of these formats, ==each of which becomes its
own division you can customize==:

- **Policy** — 1AC/2AC/1AR/2AR against 1NC/2NC/1NR/2NR, ==four cross-examination
  periods==.
- **LD** — 1AC/1AR against 1NC/2NR, ==one debater a side==.
- **Public Forum** — CON/AFF/NS/AS/NAF/NEG with a shared grand
  cross-examination, ==where the flip decides who speaks first==.
- **Parliamentary** — PMC/MG/PMR against LOC/MO/LOR, ==with points of
  information instead of cross-examination==.
- **British Parliamentary** — ==four teams of two in each room, ranked first to
  fourth==.
- **World Schools** — ==three speakers a side plus a reply speech==, squads of up
  to five.
- **Asian Parliamentary** — three speakers a side plus a reply speech.
- **Big Questions** — constructive, rebuttal, consolidation and rationale on
  each side, ==with question segments==.
- **IPDA** — ==one-on-one==, with two cross-examination periods.
- **Congressional Debate** — ==a chamber of legislators==, with authorship,
  alternating pro and con speeches and questioning.

Per format you choose the division it runs in (Open, Novice or JV), how entries
are written in pairings, ==a cap on entries per school, an entry fee==, and a note
that appears on the public invite.

### The admin view [#tournaments-the-admin-view]

==Every hosted tournament has an admin web view== at
`/tournament/<year>/<name>/admin`, ==open to its owners and admins==. It shows the
events with their entry counts, the whole schedule (==including rounds that are
not published yet==), every entry and its status, schools, judges, rooms and
result sets, each linked to its public page.

#### The demo tournament [#tournaments-the-demo-tournament]

The **Debate AI Demo Invitational** ==is a demo tournament anyone can browse as its
admin==, signed in or not, under the mock username **demo.admin**. ==It is the
only demo, and it is a full-size tournament==: Policy, Lincoln-Douglas, Public
Forum and Parliamentary debate plus Original Oratory, Extemp, Dramatic Interp
and Informative, ==with 40 entries in each from 32 schools==. Every debate division
has ==six power-matched prelims and a 16-team break from octafinals to finals==,
and speech has three prelims, semifinals and a final. Its results include
==prelim seeds, final places, speaker awards and the elimination bracket==. **Demo admin** on the upcoming page and **Try the demo admin** on the
host page both open it. ==The site loads it by itself when it is missing or over==,
so ==its dates always stay current==.

##### Editing the demo's mock data [#tournaments-editing-the-demos-mock-data]

The demo's admin view ends with **Edit mock data**, ==where anyone can change
its entries== (code, name, status), judges (name, code, active), rounds (label,
whether pairings are published and results posted), pairings (each section's
judges and, in debate, its winner) and result sets (label, published). ==Each
row saves on its own== with **Save**. ==Edits only ever change the demo
tournament==, never a hosted tournament or Tabroom's data; ==they are shared by
everyone browsing the demo== and last until **Reset demo data** ==reloads the
original mock data== (or the demo reloads itself once it is over).

##### Watching the demo's rounds live [#tournaments-watching-the-demos-rounds-live]

==The demo's pairings show simulated live rounds==. Next to each room the
**Live** column ==shows the speech under way== (for example **2NC · Neg**) and how
far through the round it is, and each round on the **Pairings** tab shows the
same for its typical room. The **Results** tab ==lists the round each event is
debating right now== under **Live now**.

**Tune in** ==opens a room's stream under it==:

- ==live captions of the speech being given==, with the time left in it;
- the speech order, with finished speeches greyed and the current one filling up;
- **Live updates**: ==when each speech, cross-ex and prep began and ended, then the decision==;
- **Speech summaries**, ==one per finished speech==, each with **Listen** to hear it read aloud;
- **Listen live**, ==which reads each speech aloud as it starts==.

==Rounds run at six times real speed and loop== — check-in, the speeches, then the
decision — so ==there is always something on==. ==The speeches and summaries are
generated filler, not real debates==, and ==every viewer sees the same moment==.
==Read-aloud uses your browser's built-in voices==.

### Good to know [#tournaments-good-to-know]

- ==Pairings and results are refreshed at most once a minute==, so ==reload during a
  tournament to see the latest round==.
- Two tournaments with the same name in the same year can't share an address,
  so ==the second one keeps its Tabroom number in its links==.
- ==You are recorded as the tournament's owner==, and registered in every format it
  runs, so the first pairing has somebody in it.
- The admin view reads the tournament; ==adding schools, rooms, rounds and
  ballots from it is not built yet==.
- Signing in to Tabroom happens on Tabroom, inside the **Tabroom** row's frame;
  ==live Tabroom tournaments show public information only==.

## Training tools [#training-tools]

The training tools ==turn a round you've already flowed into something to practice from==. They all live in the
**[Coach Workspace](https://debate-ai.com/coach)** (`/coaching`), and ==each also has its own page==. Every one of
them ==starts from the same input==: the **current round**, meaning ==the flow selected in the==
[round workspace](https://debate-ai.com/debate) (`/debate`). The Coach Workspace ==shows which round that is at
the top of every section==, with a link to change it.

The Coach Workspace is the first tab of the [Coaching](/docs/features/ai-coaching#coaching) page. AI Coach
Mode, Coaching Programs, the Team Calendar and Coach Materials each have a tab of their own next to it, so
==the "own page" for each of those is its tab==.

### The workflow [#training-tools-the-workflow]

1. **Flow the round** ==in the round workspace, or open a saved one==.
2. **Read it** in the *Flow* section: the argument tree, per-argument summaries, and the exposure charts ==show
   what was answered, what was dropped, and where each side is vulnerable==.
3. **Generate training** in the *Coaching* section: ==coaching prompts and drills are derived from the flowed
   arguments==, so they're ==specific to this round rather than generic==.
4. **Practice**, ==mark drills done, and come back for review reminders==.
5. **Prep the next round** in the *Prep* and *Scouting* sections: ==briefings pull scouting on the opponent and
   judge together with your team's prep notes==.

## Judge paradigms [#judge-paradigms]

==Click any judge in a tournament's pairings== (`/tournament/…/rounds`) ==to see
how they judge before the round starts==.

### What the judge dialog shows [#judge-paradigms-what-the-judge-dialog-shows]

- **Paradigm**: ==the judge's paradigm from Tabroom==, with when they last
  reviewed it and any Tabroom certifications. ==If the judge has written and
  verified a paradigm on this site, that one is shown instead==.
- **Voting record**: ==every published round they judged on Tabroom== — tournament,
  date, event, round, both teams, their vote and the panel's result — with a
  summary on top: rounds judged, Aff/Pro versus Neg/Con votes, and
  **squirrels** ==(panel rounds where they were the lone dissent)==.
- **Tabroom page** opens their paradigm on tabroom.com.

==Judges are kept for a week, so a judge opened recently loads instantly==. An
older copy is refreshed from Tabroom when you open it; if Tabroom can't be
reached, ==the last copy is shown and marked== **Cached copy**.

### Write your own paradigm [#judge-paradigms-write-your-own-paradigm]

==Judges can write their paradigm here at== **`/practice/paradigm`** (also linked
from every judge dialog as "Are you …? Edit your paradigm"):

1. Write it in the editor — bold, italics, headings, lists, quotes and links
   from the toolbar, with a **Preview** tab.
2. Paste your Tabroom paradigm link (or judge id) and **Save paradigm**.
3. ==To prove the Tabroom judge is you, copy the code shown==, add it anywhere in
   your paradigm on Tabroom, save it there, and click **Verify**. You can
   remove the code afterwards. ==An admin can also verify you==.

==Until you're verified, debaters keep seeing your Tabroom paradigm==, so ==nobody
can publish a paradigm under someone else's name==. ==Linking a different Tabroom
judge resets the verification==.

### For admins [#judge-paradigms-for-admins]

The admin panel's **Imports** tab has a **Judge paradigms** card.
**Discover & sync all paradigms** ==walks Tabroom's list of every judge with a
current paradigm== and saves each one's paradigm and record; **Refresh stale**
re-fetches judges older than a week. ==Runs resume where they stopped==. The same
card lists accounts claiming a Tabroom judge, to verify or revoke.

==Tabroom only shows paradigms to a signed-in account==, so the site reads them
with its own Tabroom account. If that account isn't set up, the card says so, and
==only judges already synced can be shown==.
