## Create New Round — Registered-User Invites & Notifications [#round-invites-and-notifications]

==Links the Create New Round dialog's debater, judge and spectator fields to real
accounts==: ==typing suggests registered users==, and ==saving the round invites
everyone on it== — ==a registered user in the app, anyone else by email==.

Open **Create New Round** in the round workspace. Your invites and other
notifications are listed at **/notifications**.

### What it does

When you save the round, everyone you added (except you) is invited: ==a
registered user gets a notification in the app, everyone else gets an email
invite==. ==New notifications pop up while you have a tab open==.

==Editing an existing round also invites anyone you newly added==. ==People
already on the round aren't invited again==.

## Online Debate Versus AI [#ai-versus-rounds]

==Lets a debater start a full practice round against an AI opponent== — pick a
format and side, then ==submit their own speeches one at a time, in turn order==.

It lives in the [Coaching](/docs/features/ai-coaching#coaching) page. For a full
timed round with an AI opponent, see [Practice vs AI](/docs/features/rounds#practice-vs-ai).

### What it shows [#ai-versus-rounds-what-it-shows]

A form to start a round: a round ID, a format, and which side you're speaking
(formats with only one side, like Congress, only offer that side). ==Starting a
round lays out its full speech order==.

Once a round is active, ==the panel shows its full turn order== — each speech
tagged "You" or "AI" and marked Delivered, Next or Pending — and, when it's
your turn, ==a text box to type and submit the next speech==. On the AI's
turn, **Generate AI speech** ==asks the AI for its speech== and saves it in
that slot.

==Every delivered AI speech has its own **Regenerate** button==. It asks again
==from the same earlier speeches the AI saw the first time== and replaces that
speech in place — ==every other speech is untouched==.

**Download transcript** appears once ==every speech is delivered, giving a
plain-text transcript== labelled "You" or "AI" per speech.

The speech box also has a **🎤 Record** / **Stop recording** button ==to
dictate straight into it==, ==greyed out in browsers that can't do it==.

While you type (or dictate), a **Possible fallacies** hint appears under the
box when the draft reads like an ad hominem, straw man or false dichotomy.
It's a prompt to look closer before you submit, never a verdict, and nothing
is stored.

### Good to know [#ai-versus-rounds-good-to-know]

==Speeches are typed or dictated==; there is ==no way to transcribe an
already-recorded audio or video file==.

## Featured Rounds [#featured-rounds]

==Real rounds anyone can open== from the **Featured** section of the round
workspace's start screen, ==with every speech's doc already filled in and the timers set to
the round's format==.

Each featured round also has its own link you can share, such as
**/debate/2015-ndt/northwestern-mv-michigan-ap**.

### What's there [#featured-rounds-whats-there]

**NDT 2015 Finals** (Northwestern MV vs Michigan AP) ==is a college policy round
with eight speeches==: 1AC, 1NC, 2AC, 2NC, 1NR, 1AR, 2NR and 2AR. ==The seven
speech docs that were shared publicly (1AC through 2NR) open in their
speeches==; ==there is no 2AR doc, so that speech starts blank==.

Click the card and ==the round opens as one flow with college policy timers==
(9-minute constructives, 3-minute cross-examinations, 6-minute rebuttals and
10 minutes of prep per side). ==Open any speech to read its doc==. ==The round is
then yours like any other: flow it, edit the docs, pin it==. Clicking the card
again ==reopens that same copy rather than building a new one==; ==delete the
round's flow to start fresh==.

### Beside the video [#featured-rounds-beside-the-video]

The round's video in the library,
[2015 NDT Finals - Northwestern MV vs Michigan AP](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zoKowWVQ1wE),
gets a **Speech docs** ==tab in the column beside the player, opened first==. It
==shows one speech's doc at a time==: click a speech (1AC to 2AR) or ==use the
arrows to flip through them while you watch==. ==Each doc is shown read-only in
read mode==, so a card's body shows only its highlighted text. The 2AR button is greyed out
because there's no doc for it. **Open in debate view** ==goes to the full round
with timers==.

### Good to know [#featured-rounds-good-to-know]

- ==Opening a featured round's link builds the round for you==, so the link can
  be shared. ==A doc that fails to load is named in a notice== under the cards and
  ==the rest of the round still opens==.
- A featured round's docs are large, so ==it takes up a fair share of your
  browser's storage==.
- If one of the round's speech docs is removed from the Topic Starter library,
  ==that speech opens blank==.

## Live Stream (Round Radio) [#live-stream]

Check **Live stream** when you create or edit a round and ==the round is
broadcast as audio, like radio==. ==Only the speeches go out, each one in the
speaker's own voice==, back to back. Anyone can ==tune in from **Videos**== while
the round is on, and ==the replay stays there afterwards==.

- **Where:** **Debate** → **Create New Round** or **Edit Round** → **Live stream**.
  Listen from **Videos** (the **Live rounds** row) or on the round's own page.

### What it does [#live-stream-what-it-does]

- **One checkbox.** ==**Live stream** sits under the Private/Public switch== in
  both the create and the edit menus. Turning it off later ==stops the broadcast
  and keeps the replay==.
- **Only your speeches, only your voice.** ==Your browser goes on air when your
  speech timer starts== (with recording on, which it is by default) ==and stops
  when the timer stops==. Prep time and the gaps between speeches are never
  sent.
- **The speaker's device, nobody else's.** Everyone in the round runs a timer,
  so ==the site works out who is speaking from the round's invite list==: the
  1A broadcasts the 1AC and 1AR, the 2N the 2NC and 2NR, an LD debater every
  speech on their side. ==Judges and spectators never broadcast==. If the
  debaters were typed as names rather than emails, ==the device running the
  timer broadcasts==.
- **On air badge.** ==A pill in the corner of the round shows **Live stream**,
  and turns red with **On air · 1AC** while you are broadcasting==. Click it to
  open the listening page and share its link.
- **Tuning in.** ==A live round starts you at the beginning of the speech being
  given==, a few seconds behind live, so you hear it from its first word. When
  you catch up, ==the player waits for the next speech and carries on by itself==.
  **Go live** skips ahead to the newest speech, and ==each speech is a chip you
  can jump to==.
- **Replays.** ==A round that has finished (or has been quiet for two hours)
  shows as a replay== and plays from the 1AC to the last rebuttal.
- **Private rounds.** ==A private round's stream isn't listed in Videos==. Its
  link works only for the people on the round: debaters, judges and spectators,
  signed in.

### Known gaps [#live-stream-known-gaps]

- ==Audio goes out in 5-second pieces==, so listeners are at least that far
  behind, and there can be a very short gap between pieces in some browsers.
- ==Cross-ex is broadcast only from the device whose timer is running for it==
  (the format's speaker for that cross-ex); the other debater's questions come
  through that mic.
- ==The broadcast uses the default microphone==, not the one picked in the
  recording menu.
- ==Anyone with a debater's email on the invite can broadcast into the stream==,
  and the stream is keyed by the round's link, so two rounds with the same
  tournament, round and teams share a stream.
- ==Streams have no delete button yet==; switching Live stream off before the
  first speech hides one.

## Practice Round Simulator [#practice-round-simulator]

Lets a debater ==configure and track a simulated tournament round== — ==format,
side, AI judge paradigm, and AI opponent persona/difficulty==.

==Merged into== [Practice vs AI](/docs/features/rounds#practice-vs-ai): this
panel and the team matchup simulator ==sit in the collapsed== **Round simulator**
section below the round setup.

### What it shows [#practice-round-simulator-what-it-shows]

A setup form, plus ==every saved round as its own card==: judge-paradigm/
opponent-persona/difficulty badges, submitted-speech progress, a "Generate
AI opponent speech" action, a "Generate post-round feedback" form, and a
"Get AI judge decision" action, next to which a **Scoring rubric** card
==checks the round's own judge paradigm's voting priorities== (✅/⬜) against the
rendered decision. A **Compare your past attempts** section ==shows a
chronological win/loss trend across a debater's rounds==.

When a round's setup carried an AI opponent persona, ==its post-round
feedback gets an extra== "Facing the *persona name* persona again" section —
==a short, numbered list of what to prep before facing that style again== (e.g.
pre-writing a framework defense before a Critique opponent, or drilling
efficient tagline-first responses before a Fast Flow one). ==A custom persona
gets a general tip pointing back at its own style notes==, since free-form notes
==can't be turned into style-specific advice== the way a built-in persona's can.

The "AI opponent persona" picker now ==also reuses the AI Practice Opponent
idea's custom-persona library==: a "Custom opponent persona" option opens a
name/style-notes form with "Save to my persona library"/"Share with my
team" checkboxes, and "My persona library"/"Shared by your team" sections
below it ==let a saved or team-shared entry fill in that form with one click==
— the same library the AI Practice Opponent setup uses.

### Matchup simulator [#practice-round-simulator-matchup-simulator]

==Pair any two ranked teams in a division and see who the ratings favour==. The
simulator reads each division's full-season rankings, so ==every ranked team can
be picked== — filter
by name or school, or hit **Random opponent**.

It shows ==the single-round win probability for each side==, ==the odds of taking a
3- or 5-judge elim panel==, and a **Simulate 1,000 rounds** button that ==plays
the matchup out to show the spread a percentage hides==.

==The probability comes from the two teams' ratings and how certain each rating
is==, using the standard Glicko-2 formula. ==Panel odds assume each ballot
is independent at that probability==.

It appears in two places:

- **Practice round**: ==both sides are free to pick==.
- **Every team profile**: ==that team is locked in as side A==,
  across each division it is ranked in, and ==any other ranked team can be its
  opponent==.

## Practice tools [#practice-tools]

The practice tools ==let you debate when there's no tournament==: ==against another debater, against an AI opponent, in a simulated round
with a timer and an AI judge, or as a single speech under a word count==. They're the *Practice* section of the
**[Coach Workspace](https://debate-ai.com/coach?section=practice)** and ==each has its own page==. Unlike the
[training tools](/docs/features/tournaments-judge#training-tools), ==most of them don't need a flowed round to start from==.

### Run a round against the AI [#practice-tools-run-a-round-against-the-ai]

**[Practice vs AI](https://debate-ai.com/practice/versus-ai)** (`/practice/versus-ai`, [feature spec](/docs/features/rounds#practice-vs-ai)) is a full,
==timed round against an AI opponent in real turn order==. ==Choose a difficulty, a topic and your side==, and one of
the opponents at that difficulty. ==Your opponent then finds cards and caselist outlines on the topic== and
==summarizes the arguments for both sides before you start==. Debate through timed opening, cross-examination,
and closing phases; ==at the end an AI judge scores the round and explains its decision==. It's also on the dock
as **Practice vs AI**, and ==a round in progress can be resumed on the same account==.

The **Practice Round Simulator** ([feature spec](/docs/features/rounds#practice-round-simulator)) is on the
same page now, in the collapsed **Round simulator** section.
==It recreates a tournament round's *conditions*== — format, side, a timer, an AI judge paradigm, and an AI
opponent persona — and ==keeps your past attempts so you can compare them==.

### Practice against real people [#practice-tools-practice-against-real-people]

**[Online Challenges](/docs/features/rounds#online-challenges)** (the first tab of
`/practice/debates`, in the sidebar's Practice Debates section) ==are written debates against another player or the AI that run over days==.
Pick any format and any listed resolution, take a side, and send it; ==each side has 3 days to answer each
speech or forfeits==, and ==an AI judge decides after the last rebuttal==. ==Wins earn Debater Level XP with a
win-streak boost, and unlock trophies==.

**[Practice Partners](https://debate-ai.com/practice-partners)** (`/practice/debates?section=partners`, a tab of Practice Debates, [feature spec](/docs/features/team-collaboration#practice-partners))
==is where you find a human opponent or judge==. Set up a practice profile — switch on **Open to
challenges**, **Volunteer to judge**, or both, and pick the formats, styles, speed, and level you're
comfortable with. Then press **Find me a match**: ==you're paired at random with one of the most compatible
debaters for your profile==, ==shown anonymously== (level, speed, formats, styles, and how well you fit — ==never
who it is==). **Send request** proposes a format, resolution, and time; ==they get a notification and can
accept or decline==, and ==you both see who the other is once they accept==. **Find another** draws again.
==Accepted rounds get a webcam room code== that both debaters and the
judge paste into the round workspace's Cameras panel. If you volunteer to judge, ==accepted rounds that still
need a judge are listed for you to pick up==. Switch on **Volunteer to coach** or **Team that needs a coach**, with
coaching details, to be listed on the Online coaching board where coaches and teams find each other. It's the first panel in the Coach Workspace's Practice section.

**[Prediction Markets](https://debate-ai.com/practice/predictions)** (`/practice/predictions`, [feature spec](/docs/features/ai-coaching#prediction-markets))
==lets you bet play-money points== (everyone starts with 1,000) on who wins a debate, who wins a tournament, or
whether a team's team rating goes up. ==Prices move with every bet==, and markets tied to rounds and events
hosted on the site, or to the rankings, ==settle themselves==.

### Setting the judge and the opponent [#practice-tools-setting-the-judge-and-the-opponent]

==Both round tools take their judge paradigm and opponent persona at setup time==, from the same ==three built-in
AI judges and four built-in opponent styles==, or from one you describe yourself. The three judges are, in order:
==a tech-over-truth policymaker who judges off the flow, a critique (K) judge, and a middle-ground flow judge== who
applies common sense to dropped arguments. ==None of them promise points through "auto-30" arguments==: ==speaker
points stay an assessment of the actual performance==. A round saved under an older judge type ==opens with the
closest of the three==.

| Tool | Route | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI Judge Decision | `/practice/judge-decision` | Generate an AI decision for a round under its saved paradigm, grounded in the same flow summary the Argument Tree Outline shows. Pasted speeches show a **Possible fallacies** hint (ad hominem, straw man, false dichotomy) under the text when they match. A judge decision opened from **Settings → Saved to your account** is scrolled to and outlined. |

### Word-count speeches [#practice-tools-word-count-speeches]

**[Word-Count Speeches](https://debate-ai.com/word-count)** (`/word-count`, [feature spec](/docs/features/rounds#word-count-rounds))
==replaces the clock with a maximum word count==: one text area per speech in the chosen format, with ==a live
word-count badge recomputed on every keystroke==. It's ==the right tool for written practice==, for formats where
you'd rather cap length than time, and for drafting speeches you'll later deliver against the timer. ==Rounds
are saved to your account when signed in==.

### Suggested loop [#practice-tools-suggested-loop]

1. **Scout** ==the judge and opponent you're preparing for== in the
   [Prep Workspace](/docs/features/rounds#prep-workspace) (`/practice/prep`), which keeps briefings, scout-to-strategy,
   opponent and judge profiles, and prep notes on one page, and ==turn them into a paradigm and a persona==.
2. **Rehearse** ==in the Practice Round Simulator under that paradigm and persona==.
3. **Pressure-test** ==by playing the same side in Practice vs AI at a higher difficulty tier==.
4. **Flow the practice round** in the round workspace and run the [training tools](/docs/features/tournaments-judge#training-tools)
   on it — ==drills and coaching prompts work on practice rounds exactly as on tournament rounds==.

### Tips [#practice-tools-tips]

- **Sharing a section:** `/coaching?stage=practice` ==opens the Coach Workspace on the practice tools==.
- **Signed out?** ==Every practice tool except Practice Partners works without an account==; ==signing in is what
  makes rounds, attempts, and word-count speeches follow you across devices==. Practice Partners matches real
  people, so ==it needs a (non-guest) account==.

## Practice vs AI [#practice-vs-ai]

![Practice vs AI — the opponent picker, a live timed round, and the scorecard](https://i.imgur.com/2awjrUp.png)

==Lets a debater run a full timed round against an AI opponent==. ==Setup is five
steps==: **difficulty** (Easy through Legends), **topic** (with your side and
whether the round uses evidence), **opponent** (one of the 13 personas at that
difficulty), **prep** (the opponent finds cards and cases and summarizes both
sides) and **arguments** (==you tick exactly which arguments you run and which
the opponent runs==). After that ==you debate through opening statements,
cross-examination and closing statements, then get an AI-judged scorecard==.

==The page opens on the current high-school policy topic==, "Resolved: The
United States federal government should establish national health insurance in
the United States", ==with you on the affirmative running a single-payer plan
(Medicare for All)== and ==the opponent running the disads against it== by
default: Spending/Debt, Pharma Innovation, Rural Hospitals and Politics. The
Arguments step also offers the aff's advantages (Uninsured Deaths, Cost
Control, Medical Debt, Pandemic Preparedness, Health Equity), the States and
Public Option counterplans, a Wait Times case turn and T on "establish". Pick
Neg and the sides swap: the opponent reads single payer and you pick the
disads.

==You can write your own resolution==. A custom topic ==defaults to a round
without evidence==, since the card library may have nothing on it; the
arguments then come from the opponent's prep brief, and you can add your own
on the Arguments step.

==This page is also where the== **Practice Round Simulator** lives now: ==its saved rounds, judge paradigms,
replays and team matchups are in the collapsed== **Round simulator** section
below the round (see [Practice Round Simulator](/docs/features/rounds#practice-round-simulator)).

Find it at **/practice/versus-ai**. ==You don't need to sign in to play a
round==; signing in saves your rounds to Debate History and earns points and
badges for them.

### What it shows [#practice-vs-ai-what-it-shows]

A five-step setup (Difficulty, Topic, Opponent, Prep, Arguments), the live
round (==two side-by-side panels with countdowns, typed or dictated turns==),
and a scorecard (==each phase scored out of 10 for both sides, totals, a
verdict, and follow-up drill recommendations==). Per-phase clocks sit in a
collapsed "Phase timings" box on the Arguments step. During the round, ==the
prep brief and the arguments each side chose stay one click away== in a
collapsed panel above it.

After you reveal the decision, the scorecard offers a ==**Coach debrief**==:
pick a coaching style (==Standard, Socratic, Devil's Advocate or
Fact-Checker==, the same styles as AI Coach Mode) and select **Get coach
debrief**. ==An AI coach reads the judge's scores and reasons and your own
speeches, and names the two or three things that would have won you the most
points==, with one thing to practise for each. ==The style you pick is
remembered==, and follows your account when you're signed in. The debrief needs
AI features (sign in). ==When you're signed in, each debrief is saved with the
round==, and a line under it says "Saved with this round in Debate History."
Select **Get a new debrief** to ask again, in the same or another style; ==the
newest one replaces the one saved before==. A signed-out round is kept nowhere,
so the line says the debrief wasn't saved.

While you type or dictate a turn, ==possible fallacies (ad hominem, straw man, false dichotomy) are flagged under the box== with a one-line explanation. A hint is a prompt to look closer, not a verdict; it runs offline in your browser and nothing is stored or sent.

### Judge feedback style [#practice-vs-ai-judge-feedback-style]

Above the round, a **Judge feedback style** picker sets how the judge words the
scorecard: ==Standard, Socratic (leads with questions), Devil's Advocate (argues
the other side to stress-test your strategy) or Fact-Checker (flags claims that
need evidence)== — the same coaching styles as AI Coach Mode and "Ask the
coach". ==The style only changes the wording of the reasons and the verdict
notes; the rubric, the scores and the winner stay the same.== A styled
scorecard says so at the top ("Feedback in the Socratic style"), and when
you're signed in ==the style is saved with the round==, so Debate History says
which style it was judged in and the round's export includes it. ==Your pick is
remembered==, and when you're signed in ==it follows your account to other
devices==, separately from the style you chose for the other coaches.

### With and without evidence [#practice-vs-ai-with-and-without-evidence]

**With evidence** (the default for the national health insurance topic), prep
searches the card library and caselist, and ==the opponent cites those cards by
author and year during the round==. It is told never to invent a citation that
isn't in the list. **Without evidence** skips the search, and ==both sides argue
analytically==; the opponent is told not to cite or invent any source.

### How the opponent debates [#practice-vs-ai-how-the-opponent-debates]

Every bot turn carries the round plan: the aff plan, the arguments you chose,
the arguments it runs, and (with evidence) its cards. ==The opponent is told to
debate like a tournament competitor==: signpost each argument, run disads as
uniqueness, link, internal link and impact, do impact calculus, answer your
arguments line by line and point out drops, and ask pointed cross-ex
questions. As the negative ==it announces how many off-case positions it is
reading== ("4 off") and goes through each one. ==When you speak first, it sees
your opening and answers it== rather than giving a speech of its own in a
vacuum. Its persona still sets the tone, and turns are capped at 250 words.

### The opponent's prep [#practice-vs-ai-the-opponents-prep]

In an evidence round the Prep step ==searches the card corpus with the topic's key words== (filler
words such as "should" and "be" dropped) and, separately, the caselist
outlines. ==Up to eight cards and four outlines go to the AI==, which writes a
brief: a summary, then ==two to four arguments per side, each citing the cards
that back it==. When the AI can't be reached, or its reply can't be read, ==the
brief is built from the
topic's preset arguments, or for a custom topic from the cards alone==: ==each card's tag becomes an argument on the side the corpus says
read it==. ==A card search that is rate-limited or fails counts as finding
nothing, so prep still produces a brief==.

A "View Debate History" button on the setup screen opens **Debate History**:
==every past round (topic, opponent, result, date), newest first==, each
==expandable in place to its full transcript==, with ==its saved Coach debrief
above the transcript== (and the coaching style it was written in) when you
asked for one, and a "Judged in the … feedback style" line for rounds judged
since the style was saved. A round's link in Settings → Saved data ==opens the history with
that round already expanded==. ==A round's export from Settings includes its
debrief==, and **Restore from export…** brings it back.

Above the list, **Fallacy hints across your speeches** runs the same fallacy
hints as the turn box over ==every speech you sent in those rounds==: the
total, hints per 1,000 words, a count per kind (ad hominem, straw man, false
dichotomy) and the speeches with the most hints. ==The opponent's speeches
are left out==. Nothing extra is saved — it is ==recounted from your saved
rounds each time you open the history==, so it covers up to the 50 most recent
rounds the history shows. Like the turn-box hints, it flags phrasing worth a
second look, not a verdict.

### Which AI it uses [#practice-vs-ai-which-ai-it-uses]

==Your own OpenRouter or Anthropic key when you've added one== in Settings →
General, otherwise the site's own AI. ==If one AI service fails, the next one is
tried==. With no working AI at all, ==each persona answers with an in-character
"my systems are offline" line==.

### Score, badges and streak [#practice-vs-ai-score-badges-and-streak]

==Your score and badges are saved to your account== and shown on the scorecard.
==Your streak goes up by one for a round on the day after your last one==, ==stays
the same for a second round on the same day==, and ==restarts at 1 after a missed
day== — so ==five days running earns the Streak5 badge==. ==Reloading mid-round
resumes the same round== instead of dropping back to the picker.

~~The prep brief is not yet fed into the opponent's turns.~~ **Fixed:** ==the
arguments chosen on the Arguments step, and the cards in an evidence round, go
to the opponent on every turn==. The brief and the chosen arguments are kept
with the in-progress round in the browser only, so ==they are not part of
Debate History==. ==Only the national health insurance topic has preset
arguments so far==; other topics take their arguments from the prep brief.
==The judge still scores the four phases, not the flow==: it doesn't know which
arguments were chosen.

~~The Coach debrief is shown once and not saved.~~ **Fixed:** ==a signed-in
round keeps its latest debrief==, shown in Debate History and included in the
round's export. Only the latest debrief is kept, not every one you asked for.
The coach still only sees the scorecard and your speeches, not the
opponent's.

## Round Cloud Save [#round-cloud-save]

==Lets a signed-in user save a whole round== (the tournament, debaters and
judges around a set of flows) to their account and ==load it back on any
device==.

Find it in the Flow History dialog's "Rounds" tab: a cloud-upload icon on each
round, plus **Save all rounds** and **Save flows not in a round** buttons.

### What it does [#round-cloud-save-what-it-does]

==Saving a round also saves each of its flows==. **Save all rounds** and
**Save flows not in a round** ==cover the bulk cases==. The "Saved to account"
tab's Flows section badges a flow "via round" ==when it was only saved as
part of a round==.
The same tab's search box ==filters saved rounds by name== alongside saved
flows (see [Flow Cloud Save](/docs/features/flow-speech#flow-cloud-save)).

==Saving never quietly overwrites a newer copy==, just like [Flow Cloud
Save](/docs/features/flow-speech#flow-cloud-save): if someone saved a newer
version of the round from somewhere else since this browser last saw it, ==the
save is rejected with a conflict instead of silently overwriting it==, and the
round's cloud icon turns into a warning that ==a second click (an explicit
"save anyway") resolves==. ==Each of the round's flows gets the same check on
its own==.

### Bulk saves skip unchanged items [#round-cloud-save-bulk-saves-skip-unchanged-items]

**Save all rounds** and **Save flows not in a round** ==skip a flow or round

- **View controls above the speeches.** On desktop, when the sidebar shows a

- **Click a speech to make it active.** ==Clicking any speech that isn't the

  active pane==; otherwise the pair shifts to show it.

### Good to know [#round-sidebar-speeches-good-to-know]

- Fallacy hints are a quick wording check, so ==they miss
  fallacies worded another way and can flag fair arguments==. They are not
  stored, so there is no fallacy-frequency stat yet.
- Signposts for a speech linked to an editor document are read when the
  sidebar loads that document, so ==headings added since then show after the
  next refresh of the round== (the speech in view re-reads its doc every 30
  seconds for its word totals, but the signpost list does not).
- Grades are saved with the round, so ==they follow the round's own save and sync==. They are not kept per
  grader: ==two people grading the same speech overwrite each other==.

- The browser's speech recognition ==only reports when a phrase ends, not
  per-word times==, so ==word times are estimated by spreading each phrase
  across the time it took==. ==Clicking a word lands close to it, not exactly on
  it==.
- Recordings transcribed before captions existed have no phrase timings, so
  ==their words are spread evenly across the whole recording==.
- ==Transcription needs a browser with the Web Speech API== (Chrome, Edge,
  Safari). Elsewhere the ticker says there are no captions yet.
- ==AI sections are saved only in this browser==, not on the
  round, so ==other devices and other people in the round don't see them==.
  Section starts are snapped to the transcript's ~20-word lines, so ==a
  heading can land a few words early==.
- The clarity request reaches the speaker over the round's live room, so ==both
  laptops need the round open==. The speaker is found from the round's debater
  emails; a device not seated there gets it only while its own timer for that
  speech is running. ==Only judges listed on the round see the menu item==.

## Joined Round Recording [#round-recording]

Once the speeches of a round are recorded, **Join round recording** ==puts them
in round order in one MP3==, ==plays it back with captions and each debater's
webcam==, ==lets you fix where every speech begins, and saves it all to the
site's cloud storage==.

Open it from the round workspace's speech recording menu (⋮ or "Mic &
recording") → **Join round recording…**

### What it does [#round-recording-what-it-does]

- **One MP3 for the round.** ==Every speech with a recording is joined in round
  order with a one-second gap==, at a size of ==about 30 MB an hour==.
  ==Cross-ex has no recording of its own==; it is timed inside a speaker's
  recording. ==The MP3 is built in your browser and can be downloaded==.
- **Optional processing.**
  - *Read each speech's name* — an AI voice ==says
    "1 A C" before the 1AC==, and so on.
  - *Captions from the live transcription* — ==what the browser heard while each
    speech recorded==, moved to where the speech sits in the MP3.
  - *Transcribe with Moonshine* — ==runs the Moonshine speech model on your
    device over the joined MP3 for more accurate captions==. The model (about
    63 MB) ==downloads the first time and stays in your browser after that==.
    **Settings → Sound & speech → On-device speech models** can download it ahead
    of time or remove it.
- **Speech start times.** ==Each speech's start is tracked==. **Adjust speech
  starts** ==opens a modal listing every speech==: ==play to where it begins and
  press the crosshair==, type `m:ss`, nudge ±5 s, jump to it, or clear it. The
  same modal ==labels speeches on a YouTube round== (watch page → **Speech
  starts**), ==docked in the corner so the video stays visible==. The speech
  menu's **Select YouTube Start** ==uses the same list, with stop times too==.
- **Play with speech doc.** ==Plays the round beside each speech's speech doc==
  (the linked editor document, else the flow's own speech doc). ==The doc
  scrolls to the card being read== and highlights it; click a card's time to
  jump there. ==Live captions of the line being said sit at the top==, and ==an
  H1/H2/H3 outline of the speech to flow from sits in the bottom corner==,
  revealed as it is spoken. The outline comes from the doc's headings and card
  tags; with no doc it is guessed from signposts in the transcript, and **AI
  outline** summarizes the transcript instead.
- **Webcam video.** Turn on **Record webcam video** in the recording menu
  and ==your camera is recorded with each speech==. Debaters in the round's
  **Cameras** room ==are each recorded separately from what your browser
  receives==. In playback, ==every debater's clip for the current speech plays in
  step with the MP3==.
- **Seats on camera tiles.** ==You don't pick how you join the== **Cameras**
  room. ==Your signed-in email (or name) is matched against the round's invite
  list==, and ==your tile is labeled with your seat==: **1A**, **2A**, **1N**,
  **2N** for the debaters in speaker order, **1J**, **2J** and so on for the
  judges. If you aren't on the invite, ==you join as a spectator with no label==.
- **Save to cloud.** ==Uploads the MP3, captions and webcam clips to private
  storage under your account==. ==Only you can stream them back==.
  ==Nothing uploads until you press the button==.

### Good to know [#round-recording-good-to-know]

- ==Which card was on screen isn't recorded==, so the card being read is
  estimated: ==matched against the transcript== when its words match the doc,
  else ==spread by how many words each card reads==. Bad captions or a doc that
  changed since the speech make the estimate drift.

- ==A speech resumed after a pause keeps only its last webcam clip==.
- ==Cloud copies are per account==; other debaters see the round only from their
  own uploads.
- ==Files over 95 MB (about 25 minutes of webcam video) are refused==.
- ==A camera seat isn't verified==, and there is ==no manual override for someone missing from the
  invite==.

## Online Challenges [#online-challenges]

==Challenge another player, or the AI, to a written debate on any resolution
the site lists, in any format==. Rounds are asynchronous: ==each side has 3
days to answer the other's speech==. When the last speech is in, ==an AI judge
picks the winner and explains why==. ==Wins earn Debater Level XP, a
win-streak boost and trophies==.

Open it from the first tab of **Practice Debates** (the "Practice Debates" row
of the sidebar's **Practice Debates** section), beside the
[Practice Drills](/docs/features/tournaments-judge#drill-sets) and
[Practice Partners](/docs/features/team-collaboration#practice-partners) tabs.

### Sending a challenge [#online-challenges-sending-a-challenge]

1. **Opponent.** Pick **AI opponent** and a level (Novice, Varsity or
   Champion), or **Another player** and find them by name.
2. **Format.** Public Forum, Lincoln-Douglas, Policy, College Policy (NDT),
   Parliamentary or Worlds.
3. **Resolution.** ==Every topic in the site's topic list for that format,
   newest season first==, labelled like "2026-27 · Sep · 💾 Hyperscale Data
   Centers". Choose **Write my own…** for any other motion (Parliamentary and
   Worlds have no fixed topics, so they always start there).
4. **Side.** Affirmative or Negative. ==The Affirmative speaks first, so an Aff
   challenger writes the opening speech before sending==.
5. An optional note to a player.

### How a round runs [#online-challenges-how-a-round-runs]

Four speeches, alternating: Affirmative constructive, Negative constructive,
Affirmative rebuttal, Negative rebuttal (up to 9,000 characters each).

- ==The opponent's first speech is how they accept==. They can also
  **Decline**. Until they answer, the challenger can **Withdraw**.
- ==Every speech restarts the other side's 3-day clock==. The board shows how
  long is left on each round.
- ==Missing your window forfeits the round to the other side==. A challenge
  nobody answered just expires, with no winner.
- Either debater can **Concede** a round in progress.
- ==Against the AI, its speeches are written the moment it is its turn==, in
  the style of the level you picked.
- After the fourth speech, ==the AI judge decides on the arguments made in the
  round== and writes a short reason for decision.

You get a notification for every challenge, speech, forfeit and decision.

### XP, boosts and trophies [#online-challenges-xp-boosts-and-trophies]

| Result | XP |
| --- | --- |
| Finish a round (win or lose) | 20 |
| Win | +40 × your boost |
| Lose by running out of time | 0 |

==The boost is your win streak==: the second win in a row pays 1.5×, the third
and fourth 2×, and the fifth and every one after 3×. A loss resets it. The
board shows the boost your next win will get.

==XP is added to your Debater Level automatically== the next time you open the
page after a round ends, once per round (a second device doesn't collect it
again).

Trophies: **First Victory** (first win), **Iron Debater** (10 rounds),
**Hat Trick** (3 wins in a row), **On Fire** (5 in a row), **Contender** (10
wins), **Champion** (25 wins), **Machine Breaker** (beat the Champion AI),
**Format Master** (5 wins in one format), **All-Rounder** (wins in 3 formats)
and **Crowd Favorite** (beat 5 players).

### Limits [#online-challenges-limits]

- 10 challenges to players waiting on a first answer at once, and one at a
  time per opponent.
- 10 new AI challenges a day.
- Guest accounts can debate the AI but not challenge players.

## Round Timer & Shared Clock [#round-timer]

==Every device in a round runs on the same clock==. When anyone plays, pauses,
resets or edits a speech or prep timer, ==everyone else with the round open sees
the same change==. The sidebar also has a **Timer** section with ==the circular
progress timer from the browser extension==, which ==can pop out into its own
small window==.

In the round workspace's left sidebar, open the **Timer** section under the selected
round (above **Cameras**).

### What it does [#round-timer-what-it-does]

- **Play/pause reaches the whole room.** ==Starting or stopping a timer on one
  laptop starts or stops it on the judge's and every debater's laptop too==.
  Resets, cross-ex switches, edited times and both prep clocks ==travel the same
  way==. ==A running timer is sent as how long it has been running==, so each device
  counts down on its own and ==a laptop whose clock is a few seconds off still
  shows the same time==.
- **Late joiners catch up.** ==Opening the round after it started pulls in the
  timers that have already been used==. ==Opening it never resets anyone else's
  timers==.
- **Circular timer in the sidebar.** The **Timer** section ==shows the dial for
  the speech in view==: ==the ring empties as time runs out and the face darkens
  while it runs==. The dial doesn't repeat the speech name; it's in the speech
  list. ==Click anywhere on the dial to play or pause==. The number above the count
  ==is the cross-ex clock== (in formats with cross-ex), and the two numbers under
  it ==are the Aff and Neg prep clocks==; click one to show it on the dial, and
  click **Speech** to go back. ==Starting cross-ex pauses a running speech==, and
  the other way round. Starting prep pauses a running
  speech, and ==starting a speech pauses running prep==. The section collapses
  like Cameras, and ==shows the running time in its header while collapsed==.
- **Pop out.** The picture-in-picture button next to the section header ==opens
  the dial in a small always-on-top window (Chrome and Edge)==, so it stays
  visible over other apps. ==In other browsers the dial floats in the page's
  bottom-right corner==. Click the button again, or **Bring it back**, to close
  it.
- **Turn sidebar sections on and off.** The **Sidebar sections** button (the
  panel icon in the row of buttons at the top of the sidebar) ==has a checkbox
  for each section==: Speeches & prep, Circular timer, Cameras, Shared headings
  & timers, and Open tabs. ==Unchecking one hides it==; **Show all** brings
  everything back. ==The choice is saved on this device==.
- **Same dial as the extension.** The browser extension's timer window ==uses
  this same circular timer face==.

### Good to know [#round-timer-good-to-know]

- When two people press play or pause at almost the same moment, ==each device
  keeps whichever update reached it last==, so ==they can disagree until the next
  press==.
- ==The shared clock needs the round to have been saved==, and
  ==every device must be signed in==. ==A flow without a round keeps its timers on
  this device only==.
- The pop-out window needs Chrome or Edge 116 or later. ==Firefox and Safari get the in-page floating dial==.
- ==Sidebar section choices are saved per browser, not to your account==, so a
  second device starts with every section shown.
- The dial counts down on its own, but ==a speech's timer only rings and is
  marked done by the round group's speech bar== for the speech in view.

### What it does [#youtube-speech-start-what-it-does]

- **Upload audio or pick a video.** The "Upload audio" tab ==saves an audio
  file as the speech's recording==, the same as the menu item. The "YouTube
  video" tab ==takes a pasted YouTube link== (a `t=` start is kept) or a search,
  ==which looks through the site's own video library==. The "Round audio (MP3)"
  tab ==plays the round's joined MP3, when there is one, or any MP3 link==.
  ==Both tabs label speeches the same way==.
- **Preview and mark the speeches.** ==The video plays in the modal, above a
  list of every speech in the format with its start and stop==. Type `m:ss`,
  press the crosshair to use where the video is now, nudge ±5 s, jump to a
  start, or clear it. ==It is the same list as== **Adjust speech starts** on the
  joined round MP3 and **Speech starts** on a video's watch page, with stops
  added. ==This speech's row is highlighted==: **Play clip** ==plays just its
  stretch== and **Save for** keeps the clip for it.
- **AI suggests every speech.** **Suggest all speeches with AI** ==reads the
  video's captions, finds where words are spoken and where there is silence==,
  and ==fits the format's speeches to them==: all 12 in Policy, cross-examinations
  included, or the 7 in LD. ==Speech lengths come from the format but are
  allowed to vary==, and ==prep time between speeches is expected==. The AI then
  ==checks the captions for cues like a roadmap or "cross-ex" and adjusts==.
  Pick a different format if the video isn't the round's format; ==picking a
  video from the library sets it for you==. ==If you set a start point first,
  the suggestion starts there==.
- **Save them all.** ==A suggestion fills in the list, where any row can be
  fixed by hand==. **Save all** ==keeps a clip for every speech that has a start==;
  ==a speech with no stop runs to the next speech's start==.

### Good to know [#youtube-speech-start-good-to-know]

- If the AI can't be reached, ==the suggestion is placed from the caption
  pauses alone==. If the video has no captions, ==speeches are laid out by their
  lengths from your start point, so set one first==.
- ==Clips are saved in this browser==, beside the speech's recordings.
- Saving all speeches on a YouTube video ==also marks their starts on that
  video's watch page timeline==, when the format's speech order there matches.
- ==An MP3 has no captions unless the round's recording provides them==; without
  them, speeches are laid out by length from your start point.
