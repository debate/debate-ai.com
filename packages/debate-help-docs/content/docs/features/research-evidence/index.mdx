## Common Argument Library — Saved Collections [#argument-library-collections]

Find it at **/research/cards/argument-library**.

### What it shows

Under the Argument Library's tag filter bar, a **Saved collections**
==section appears whenever the library has at least one saved collection==:

- ==Every saved collection is a button== (its name plus tag count) —
  clicking it ==replaces the tags you have selected with that
  collection's saved tags==. Beside it are **Rename**, **Update** (shown once at
  least one tag is active — replaces the collection's tags with your current
  selection), and a small **✕** remove button.
- **Rename** swaps the button for a text field; ==renaming onto another saved
  collection's name is refused== (ignoring capitals) rather than merging.
- A **Save current selection as** field and **Save collection** button ==save the
  tags you have selected under a new name==. Disabled until at least one
  tag is selected and a name is typed.

==Collections sync to your account==, and changes made on two devices at
once are both kept rather than one overwriting the other.

## Team Brainstorm Assist [#brainstorm-board]

==Lets a squad submit, AI-generate, and upvote brainstorm ideas against an
argument block==, grouped into boards by category, ==each board ranked by
popularity with a near-duplicate badge==.

Open it from the **Sprint** tab of the [Research Workspace](/docs/features/research-evidence#research-collaboration),
or from the Tools page's Community & Progress group.

### What it shows

An optional topic switcher, a submission form (argument block, contributor
ID, category, idea text), and every board, sorted by argument block then
category, ==each idea ranked by popularity with a "possible duplicate"
badge== and a **Merge into…** picker. **Generate AI ideas** ==drafts
candidate ideas with AI==.

### Session timer

An optional **Session timer** ==lets a moderator time-box a sprint==: pick
a duration (3, 5, 10 or 15 minutes), then Start, Pause/resume, or Reset. ==A
countdown started in one tab shows live in every other open tab==, and when
you're signed in ==it follows you to another device too==.

### Good to know

- ==Anyone can use the merge and "Send to Argument Library" actions==; they
  aren't limited to a moderator.
- "Contributor ID" ==is free-form text, not a login==.

## Outline Filters and Argument Tree View [#argument-tree-outline]

==Renders a filterable, heading-grouped outline of a round's flow==.

Find it at **/outline**, in the Tools page's Prep & Practice group, or type
`t outline` in Debate Editor's search palette.

### What it shows [#argument-tree-outline-what-it-shows]

One card per round, for every round whose flow has some text. ==A flow that
was opened but never flowed has no outline==, and an outline disappears once
its flow is emptied. Each card has
==seven filter controls== — **Kind**, **Side**, **Speech**, **Argument type**,
**Contributor**, **Evidence status**, and an **Unanswered only** toggle.
==Changing a control re-filters the tree in place and remembers the chosen
filter==.

### Map views [#argument-tree-outline-map-views]

A switcher at the top of each round card ==picks how the (filtered) tree is
shown==: **Outline** (the plain row list, where tagging and bulk selection
live) ==or one of five map views==. ==The panel remembers the last view per
browser== and opens on **Tiered tree** the first time.

| View | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| Tiered tree | Kialo-style minimap: one small box per claim, in tiers |
| Sunburst | The round in the center, each ring one level deeper, wedges sized by how many claims sit under them |
| Mind map | The round in the middle, pros branching right and cons branching left, with labels |
| Bubble map | Nested circles: each claim's bubble holds its responses |
| Sankey | The round flowing into contentions, arguments and responses, widths by claim count |

==The map is a claim hierarchy==: ==the round at the root, each flow heading as
a contention, each argument row under its heading==, and each later speech's
entry on that row as a response to the entry before it. Every claim is
colored **green (pro)** or **red (con)** ==relative to its parent, the way
Kialo does it==: an aff/pro row supports the round, ==a response from the same
side extends its parent, and a response from the other side answers it==.
==A heading takes the side of most of its rows==.

Under the chart, ==the focused claim sits in a large card with its parent
above it== (the arrow walks back up) and its responses split into **Pros**
and **Cons** columns. ==Clicking any claim, in the chart or in a column,
focuses it==; hovering a chart mark shows its full text. If the focused claim reads like an ad hominem, straw man or false dichotomy, a **Possible fallacies** hint appears under it — a prompt to look closer (and a cue for how to answer it), never a verdict.

### Filter presets [#argument-tree-outline-filter-presets]

Each round card has a "Filter presets" row: ==apply a saved preset, or save
the round's current filter combination under a new name==. Presets are saved
in this browser and synced to your account when you're signed in.

The panel also has ==one global "Saved filter presets" list, above every
round card==. There, each preset's name records which round it was saved
from; clicking it ==applies that filter to its origin round and scrolls that
round's card into view==, so a preset saved from a round that isn't currently
on screen is still reachable. A preset saved before this existed, or whose
origin round's outline was since cleared, stays inert there — ==it's still
usable from any round's own "Filter presets" dropdown==.

### Tagging an argument from the flow grid [#argument-tree-outline-tagging-an-argument-from-the-flow-grid]

==Right-clicking any row in the live flow grid offers== **Tag Argument…**,
opening a popover with an Argument type select, Evidence status select, and
a Contributor field. ==Saving tags the whole argument row==.

### Outline export [#argument-tree-outline-outline-export]

Each round card has a "Download outline" button that ==downloads the
flattened, filtered rows as a plain-text== `.txt` outline.

Every map view also has a **Download SVG** button above the chart. ==It saves
the view you are looking at as an `.svg` image== — a vector file you can drop
into slides or a doc and scale without blurring. ==The file is always drawn on
a white background in the light colors==, whatever theme the site is in, and
is named after the round and the view (for example
`argument-map-round-4-sunburst.svg`).

Next to it, **Download PNG** saves the same view as a `.png` picture, for
slide decks, chat apps and anywhere else that won't take an SVG. ==The PNG is
drawn at twice the chart's on-screen size== so it stays sharp on a projector,
uses the same white background and light colors, and gets the same name
ending in `.png`. A very large map is scaled down to fit (no side over 4096
pixels). If your browser can't draw the image, a message appears under the
buttons — use **Download SVG** instead.

**Download PDF** saves the same view as a one-page `.pdf` you can print or
send to a judge or coach. ==The page is the size the map looks on screen==,
with the map drawn at twice that resolution so it prints sharply, on the same
white background, and the file gets the same name ending in `.pdf`. The PDF
holds a picture of the map, so its text can't be selected or searched — use
**Download SVG** or the text outline for that. Only one file saves at a
time; the buttons wait while one is being made.

==All three files use the same font you see on screen==: your colour theme's
font, or the one you picked with **Font family** in Settings (General tab). The font is
saved inside the file, so the map looks the same on a computer that doesn't
have the font, and in the PNG and PDF too. If the font can't be loaded (for
example, you're offline), the file uses a standard system font instead.

### Good to know [#argument-tree-outline-good-to-know]

- ==Tagging is row-level, not per-speech==.
- ==The contributor field is free-form text, not a signed-in user==.
- The map's root is labelled "Round …" because ==outlines don't store the
  resolution==, and a claim's pro/con stance comes only from which side's
  speech made it (==there is no per-claim stance override==).
- ==Tagging and bulk selection are only in the Outline view==.

## Card Library Import [#card-library-import]

==How the evidence behind **CARD search** gets into the site==. The card library is
built from the published card dump — ==one entry per cut card==, with the tag,
both cites, the highlighted and full text, the card's formatting, and which
caselist it came from — and from openCaselist's speech documents. ==Site admins
load it from the admin panel==; there is nothing for a debater to do.

### Importing card files [#card-library-import-importing-card-files]

The **Card library import** card on the admin panel's Cards tab ==imports card
files from an admin's computer==: pick one or more files, watch each one's
progress bar, and stop mid-run if needed. ==Keep the tab open until it
finishes==, since your browser does the reading. Below the queue it shows ==what
the library currently holds and every file imported into it so far==.

- ==Importing the same file twice changes nothing==, and ==a corrected file
  replaces the old cards cleanly==.
- ==Every card that can't be imported is listed with the reason== (no id, an
  empty card, a card far too large, or a file that isn't a card dump) instead of
  being dropped silently.
- ==Files are imported one after another==, and one that fails doesn't stop the
  rest.

### Original sources [#card-library-import-original-sources]

The **Original sources** card at the top of the Cards tab ==pulls the full text
of the article, paper or report each card was cut from==. It charts, for the
chosen season and format, ==how many cards cite a link== and ==how many have had
their source pulled==. A card with a link is read through QwkSearch's article
reader; a card with no link, or a dead one, is ==looked up on QwkSearch== by its
tag, author and year, keeping ==the result that holds the most of the card's own
words==. ==Finished cards are never redone==, and **Stop** and **Resume** let a long
run be split up.

- ==The pulled text isn't shown anywhere in the app yet==.
- ==The run happens in the browser tab==, so keep it open.
- A match is judged by shared words, so ==a page quoting the same passage can be
  taken for the original==.

### Syncing openCaselist speech docs [#card-library-import-syncing-opencaselist-speech-docs]

The openCaselist card on the admin panel ==pulls a season's openCaselist archives==
(HS Policy, HS LD, HS PF, NDT/CEDA, NFA LD) into the same library. ==A **Season**
picker chooses which season==: this one by default, or any earlier one back to
2013-14. ==The archives are read a few megabytes at a time==, with progress shown
as it goes; ==keep the tab open until it finishes==.

==Anything that goes wrong is written to the **Import log**==: files that could
not be read and cards that were rejected. ==The log survives a reload, can be
filtered to errors and warnings==, and can be copied.

### Search [#card-library-import-search]

==CARD search ranks matches by relevance, weighting the tag above the summary,
highlighted text, cite and body==, and ==newly imported cards are searchable
straight away==.

### Good to know [#card-library-import-good-to-know]

=="All text" and "Underlined" searches can miss words that appear only in a
card's body text==; tag, cite, summary, highlighted-text and outline searches
are unaffected.

## How Debate Editor keeps your files safe [#cardmirror-embed-persistence]

==Debate Editor saves your work to your account as you type==, and it is built so
that ==a file you've loaded can never be saved over with a blank page==. This page
explains what you'll see while it saves, and what happens when something goes
wrong.

It applies ==anywhere Debate Editor is open==: the editor itself and Flow's
speech-doc panels.

### Saving as you type [#cardmirror-embed-persistence-saving-as-you-type]

- ==Edits are saved a moment after you stop typing==, separately for each
  document, so ==switching between files, or renaming one and typing in
  another, never drops a change==.
- ==Closing or hiding the tab saves anything still waiting==, so ==closing a tab
  mid-sentence doesn't lose the sentence==.
- The header shows **Saving…**, **Unsaved changes**, or **Couldn't save —
  retrying…**. ==A failed save keeps retrying on its own==, and a retry never
  brings back older text.
- Moving a file to another folder is saved the same way.
- ==Deleting a document cancels any saves still waiting for it==.

### Deleting text is still an edit [#cardmirror-embed-persistence-deleting-text-is-still-an-edit]

==If you empty a file yourself, the empty file is saved==. What can't happen is
the editor replacing your file with a blank page on its own: ==if a blank page
appears where your document was, the editor puts your document back==.

### When a document can't be read [#cardmirror-embed-persistence-when-a-document-cant-be-read]

If a stored document can't be opened (for example markup from an older editor,
or a file that was cut short), ==the editor keeps whatever is already on screen
instead of showing a blank page==, ==shows a notice==, and ==stops saving that
document==, so ==nothing typed in the pane can overwrite the copy on the server==.
The notice has a **Download** button that saves what was in the pane, so ==you
can rescue it==. ==The copy on the server is untouched==. Opening the same
document again later, once it reads cleanly, saves normally again.

Footnotes are kept through every save.

### No pop-ups on open [#cardmirror-embed-persistence-no-pop-ups-on-open]

==Because the site opens Debate Editor on nearly every visit==, the embedded editor
==doesn't show its first-launch pop-ups==: no draft-recovery sidebar, no UI tour
and no Verbatim nudge (**Help → UI tour** still starts the tour), and the
"Receiving cards in-browser needs your account" notice ==shows only once per
browser==. ==The outline pane still opens beside the document as before==.

### `.cmir` files open in read mode [#cardmirror-embed-persistence-cmir-files-open-in-read-mode]

==Opening any `.cmir` document turns read mode on==, the same as the Read mode
button: Topic Starters, uploaded documents, and files you open from your
computer, recents or the command palette. Documents you wrote in the editor
still open editable. ==Turning read mode off sticks for that document until you
open another one==.

### Good to know [#cardmirror-embed-persistence-good-to-know]

==Flow's speech docs are protected the same way==, but they are saved as part of
their flow rather than one document at a time.

### Overview [#cards-overview]

==Critical times call for critical thinkers to create a crowdsourced annotated research dataset==, for AI models to recommend research quotes, to evolve crowdsourced chain-of-thought reasoning, unlock faster ways to read long articles, to monitor developments in a knowledge graph by topic modeling, and to provide a public service of answers to research. ==Debate should be a war of warrants where victories are vectorized as weights== — weights which lead to ==the emergence of Collective Consciousness==.

### Features & Capabilities [#cards-features--capabilities]

==Search within summaries, cites, highlighted and full text over millions of research quotes==. ==Scout what evidence selected teams and schools frequently read==. ==Sort by Speeches Read In Count== (statistics from over a decade in all styles). Use Words Bold & Highlighted ==to match speech times (~300 w/min)== and to read aloud warranted summaries while judge & opponents reads highlighted quotes on-screen, which ==makes context more understandable and publically persuasive==. ==Collaborate on topic outlines, share evidence, and download Topic Starter outlines==.

#### Recent quotes, outlines and debates [#cards-recent-quotes-outlines-and-debates]

The **Quotes**, **Outlines** and **Debates** buttons under the search box ==choose what the list shows==. ==Quotes are listed most recently popular first== — the current season's cards, the most read within it, newest card first on a tie; the **Outlines** and **Debates** buttons list the most recently uploaded documents and round videos, and ==typing a term narrows any of those lists==. ==With a term, quotes are listed best match first instead==. The search opens on **Quotes** with the **Recent & Popular** sort, ==so cards are there without searching==. Sorting by Most Read or Season overrides the recent-popular order for quotes.

#### Search tips [#cards-search-tips]

==Results are ranked by relevance==: ==a card whose tag names your words comes before one that only mentions them in its body==, and a card containing more of your words comes before one containing fewer. ==Put words in double quotes to match an exact phrase==, as in `"nuclear deterrence" escalation`. ==The last word is matched as you type it==, so `modern` already finds *modernization*, and ==the search box suggests completions drawn from the words in the quotes themselves==. The top completion appears greyed out after what you typed: ==press Tab or → to take it==. Use ↑ and ↓ to step through the suggestions, and ==each one's results load as you reach it==. Press Enter or pick a suggestion to search right away; ==a new search always replaces the one before it==. Choosing **Highlight**, **Summaries** or **Blocks** under *Search in* ==narrows the match to those parts of a card==.

#### On a phone [#cards-on-a-phone]

==The list of quotes is the whole screen==. ==Tap a quote to focus it==: a **Back** button ==returns to the list exactly where you left it==, and three tabs sit under it — **Quote** (the card), **AI summary** (the card's saved find-flaws-and-extensions analysis when one exists — ==opening a quote never starts a new one==; press Generate for that) and **Full page** (the source article, extracted through qwksearch). ==Every quote you open has all three==. ==On a wide screen the list, card and AI panel stay side by side== as before.

==Only a newly generated analysis counts toward your plan's daily card AI analyses==: ==loading a saved one is free, and an analysis that fails is not counted==.

#### Read the source article beside a card [#cards-read-the-source-article-beside-a-card]

==Every quote in the result list that names a source page shows a link badge== — **Link works**, **Link broken**, **Link unreachable** or **Not checked** — so you can skip cards whose source has gone dead before you open them. The badges come from a periodic recheck of every card's source link, run from the admin panel.

When a card's citation (or its body) links the web page it was cut from, ==two buttons appear under the citation==:

- **Open page** ==opens the original article in a new tab==. ==Its icon says whether the link still works==: a green check when the page opened the last time it was checked, a red cross when it answered with an error such as *404 Not Found*, an amber warning when it timed out or could not be reached, and a grey question mark when it hasn't been checked yet. Hover the button to see the details and when the check ran.
- **Full article** ==pulls the article's full text and a formatted citation== through [qwksearch](https://qwksearch.com)'s article extractor and shows it beside the card — side by side on a wide screen, below the card on a narrow one. ==The highlighter is on by default==: ==select any passage to mark it==, tap a mark to remove just that passage, the eraser clears all your marks, the highlighter button turns marking off and on, and the copy button puts the citation plus your highlighted passages (or the whole article, if you haven't marked anything) on the clipboard, ==ready to cut a new card==.

==Some pages can't be extracted== — paywalled articles, PDFs and sites that block crawlers. ==The panel says so and offers== **Retry** or **Open page** instead. ==The article closes when you select a card from a different source==.

### 1. The Debate Singularity is Happening [#cards-1-the-debate-singularity-is-happening]

==AI now researches topics in depth, highlights key quotes, frames both sides of arguments==, coaches preparation, exposes flaws in evidence cards, and crafts strategic closing speeches that compare competing claims. ==Integrating AI into debate unlocks the next stage of emergent complexity== in socio-political governance.

### 2. Collective Thought Engine [#cards-2-collective-thought-engine]

Language Models can ==distill collective thought into a vector space where every point carries a weighted value==, reflecting its contribution to decision-making. This collective AI consciousness can ==synthesize complex arguments, judge their validity and relevance==, and support a democratic, AI-mediated economy where public votes reward influence and insight.

==AI agents learn which arguments and sources persuade different audiences==. ==Crowdsourced automation enables AI to organize argument outlines==—much like GitHub's reusable code model—helping it evaluate complex decisions with greater depth and consistency.

### 3. Transparent Reasoning [#cards-3-transparent-reasoning]

==AI reveals the exact sentences and citations behind its reasoning==, allowing users to verify alignment with collective interests through ==sentence-by-sentence interpretability==.

Debate Garden ==aggregates perspectives across the ideological spectrum==, ==highlighting the most persuasive arguments on every side of an issue==. As public trust in media and institutional expertise has declined, ==achieving genuine neutrality requires presenting all viewpoints== — not just the mainstream consensus. Rather than offering reductive caricatures of opposing positions, this platform ==equips users with the strongest, most rigorously reasoned arguments available==.

### 4. Outcome Simulation Trees [#cards-4-outcome-simulation-trees]

Users can engage in multiple AI-powered practice rounds that ==map trees of possible outcomes and responses==, ==surfacing the most persuasive strategies== across diverse contexts.

### 5. Outlines of Current News Issues [#cards-5-outlines-of-current-news-issues]

==Each debate card fits into a broader narrative==—==a topic tree linking evidence, assumptions, and values== within a collective framework. This transformation ==shifts debate from isolated rounds to a shared model of research crowdsourced to the public== by a few thousand editors, like Wikipedia or Github.

### 6. Solving Post-Self Alignment [#cards-6-solving-post-self-alignment]

==Recognizing the interconnectedness of research articles== mirrors how we should relate to global citizens as part of an emergent collective consciousness. ==Losing sight of that bigger picture fuels bias, tribalism, and division== at the root of modern conflicts between isolated social news bubbles. Only by ==mapping the whole internet as a debate outline== enables seeing how each idea fits into ==a whole greater than the sum of its parts==. This sets a socio-political model to build synergistic consciousness ethical awareness across both discourse and institutional governance. This can ==reduce LLM hallucination and steer alignment with common social values== as AI gains capacity to replace human leaders of organizations.

### 7. Topic Research Unified Tree Hierarchy (TRUTH) [#cards-7-topic-research-unified-tree-hierarchy-truth]

Key metadata extracted for LLM analysis allows models to ==detect logic flaws, flag overstatements, strengthen warrants==, and place each claim within the ==Topic Research Unified Tree Hierarchy (TRUTH)==. This consensus-driven system ==seeks grounded truth, reduces hallucination, and aligns AI reasoning with shared human values==—laying groundwork for responsible AI governance.

## Shared Evidence Library [#evidence-library]

==A submission form plus a free-text/kind search panel over the team-wide
evidence repository== — ==cut cards and reusable analytic blocks==.

- **Where:** **/research/cards/library**

### What it shows [#evidence-library-what-it-shows]

A submission form (kind, topic, case area, argument block, citation, tags,
==body text with a live word count==) plus a search box, kind filter, and
topic, case-area and tag filters over everything in the library.
==Each result has Edit and Delete actions==; ==editing scores the revision== for
the [Revision Incentives](/docs/features/gamification#revision-incentives) leaderboard. A **Check this
page** box ==checks whether a pasted URL has already been cut==, ==both in your
library and against the shared reuse index== the browser extension uses.

The **Argument Library** (**/research/cards/argument-library**) ==combines this
library with tagged Contributions Feed submissions== into topic and case-area
folders and tag collections that cut across them, ==with rename, merge and bulk
tag-editing tools==.

### Formatting a citation [#evidence-library-formatting-a-citation]

Under the Citation field, **Format citation** ==rewrites a pasted full citation
short-cite first==: `Jane Smith, Professor at MIT, 2024, …` becomes
`Smith 24 — Jane Smith, Professor at MIT, 2024, …`. Undated sources get `ND`
(`Smith ND`), organizations keep their full name (`Brookings Institution 23`)
and two authors are joined (`Doe & Roe 19`). An affiliation written as an
acronym is not mistaken for a second author: `Jane Smith, MIT, 2024` becomes
`Smith 24`. ==If the Source URL field is filled
and the citation doesn't already contain that link, it is added at the end==.
Pressing it again changes nothing, and the result stays editable before you
save. When no author can be found the citation is left as it is, with a note
asking you to start it with a last name.

### Search [#evidence-library-search]

==Library search ranks results by relevance==, and ==stays fast as the library
grows==.

### Links to a search [#evidence-library-links-to-a-search]

==Other pages can link straight to a card search== with the term and filters
(year, event, school, team or tournament) already filled in — the topics
explorer, for one, ==links every resolution to its cards==. ==The address bar
doesn't update as you change the search afterwards==.

### The first search is ready on open [#evidence-library-the-first-search-is-ready-on-open]

Opening the card search with nothing filled in ==shows the default list==
(newest cards, quotes) ==from your last visit straight away==, while the page
fetches the current list and ==swaps it in without a spinner==. ==Every search
still counts toward your daily search limit==.

### A new search always wins [#evidence-library-a-new-search-always-wins]

Typing waits a moment before searching, but changing the term, sort or filters
==cancels the previous search at once==, so ==results for an older search can't
land after a newer one==. Pressing Enter, clicking a suggestion or accepting the
inline completion ==searches right away==.

The search box ==shows the top completion inline as greyed-out text==, accepted
with Tab or →. ↑ and ↓ step through the suggestions, and ==stepping quickly
through the list costs one search, not one per suggestion==. ==Results aren't
loaded ahead for suggestions you haven't reached==, since every search counts
toward your daily limit.

### Good to know [#evidence-library-good-to-know]

- ==Tags are matched exactly==, though autocomplete ignores capitals, ==typed
  tags take the casing of an existing tag==, and the library browser shows and
  merges any variants that slip through.
- ==**Format citation** produces one style only== (debate short cite plus the
  pasted citation); there is no MLA / APA / Chicago output, and the author is
  guessed, so an unusual citation can get the wrong last name — check the
  short cite before saving.

made while it plays. The voice is generated in the background, so ==the page
keeps scrolling and typing normally while a card is read==.

==Paragraphs are shared==. When you're signed in, every paragraph your Kokoro
finishes is saved to debate-ai.com's storage, and ==anyone who reads the same
text in the same voice later, signed in or not, hears that saved copy at once==
instead of waiting for their own device. Speed isn't part of it: the same copy
plays at whatever speed each reader picked. Signed out, you still hear saved
paragraphs; you just don't add new ones. ==To get the download out of the way before a round==, use
**Settings → Sound & speech → On-device speech models**, which shows whether the
voice is downloaded and how much space it takes, and can remove it.

If your browser can't run Kokoro, or the download fails, ==the card is read by
a server instead==. Those voices come from QwkSearch (Kokoro or Deepgram), or from Cloudflare's
Deepgram Aura voices when the site has Cloudflare AI set up. Signed out, you
==share QwkSearch's free allowance of 10 reads a day==; ==link your QwkSearch account
in settings to read on your own account==. When an AI voice isn't available, or
the daily allowance is used up, the card is read with your browser's voice
instead, ==so Read aloud always works==.

### Which AI it uses [#card-hover-actions-which-ai-it-uses]

If you have turned on AI features with your own Anthropic or OpenRouter key in
the editor's settings, ==the buttons use that key==. Otherwise they use
debate-ai.com's AI: ==each new answer counts toward your plan's daily card AI
analyses==, ==a card that already has a saved answer costs nothing==, and ==the
summary needs you to be signed in==.

### Where it works [#card-hover-actions-where-it-works]

==Anywhere Debate Editor is open==: the editor itself and Flow's speech-doc
panels.

### Good to know [#card-hover-actions-good-to-know]

- ==Shared paragraphs are trusted as uploaded==. The site checks each one looks
  like audio of a sensible length, but it can't tell whether the audio really
  says that text, so a wrong clip could be shared. The first upload for a
  paragraph is kept.
- Making a whole paragraph before it plays means a long paragraph waits a few
  seconds longer to start than sentence-by-sentence playback would.

## Debate Editor remembers your zoom [#debate-editor-zoom]

==Zoom once, and every file opens at that zoom==. When you change the zoom in
Debate Editor (the **−** / **+** buttons, **Reset**, Ctrl + mouse wheel, a
trackpad pinch or the mobile zoom slider), ==the editor remembers it in this
browser== and ==opens every file after that at the same zoom==, including after
you reload the page or come back another day.

- ==It is remembered per browser==, so your laptop and a big monitor can each
  keep their own zoom.
- ==It is remembered per account==: if someone else signs in on the same
  browser, they get their own zoom, and signed-out use keeps one of its own.
- **Reset** sets it back to 100%, and that is remembered too.
- Until you zoom for the first time, files open at the **Default zoom** from
  Debate Editor's settings.
- With several files open side by side, ==zooming one pane sets the zoom new
  files open at==; panes already open keep their own zoom.

Clearing this site's data in your browser forgets the zoom, and the editor goes
back to the default.

## File Market [#file-market]

==Sell your research files to other debaters, or buy theirs==. Anyone can read a
file's **table of contents** ==for free; buying it unlocks every card== — cites,
card text and highlighting — and ==saves the whole file to your own files==.

Find it at **/research/market**: every file for sale, and your wallet. Each
file has its own page there too.

### Your wallet [#file-market-your-wallet]

==Every account has a wallet balance in US dollars==. Add money with the **Add
$10** button: ==it opens Stripe's secure payment page for a $10 credit==, and ==the
money lands in your wallet as soon as Stripe confirms the payment==. ==Money from
files you sell is added to the same wallet==. ==Guest accounts have no wallet==.

### Selling a file [#file-market-selling-a-file]

==Right-click a file in the== **Files** sidebar (or open its **…** menu) and
choose **Sell…**. The **Share with contacts** dialog has a **Sell…** button
for the open file too. ==Set a price between $0.50 and $500 and check the free
preview== — the file's pockets, hats, blocks and tags — then choose **Sell**.
==Copy the link from the confirmation to share it==.

- ==Buyers get a copy of the file as it was when you chose Sell==. To give buyers
  a newer version, choose **Sell…** again; ==that also changes the price==.
- **Stop selling** in the same dialog ==takes it off the market==. ==Anyone who
  already bought it keeps it==.
- ==Folders can't be sold==; sell the files inside them.

### Buying a file [#file-market-buying-a-file]

Open a file from the market to read its table of contents. **Buy** ==pays the
price from your wallet to the seller's==, ==saves the full file to your files and
opens it in the editor==. If your wallet doesn't cover the price, add money
from the same page. ==Each file can be bought once per account==; **Open a copy
in my files** ==saves another copy any time==.

### How it works [#file-market-how-it-works]

- The free preview is the file's ==headings and nothing else==, up to a limit
  of 1,000 headings.
- ==Your wallet is credited only once Stripe confirms the payment==, once per
  payment, ==and never for more than the amount you paid==.
- ==Every change to your balance is recorded== in your wallet's history.

### Good to know [#file-market-good-to-know]

- ==Wallet balance can't be withdrawn yet==; ==sellers' earnings stay in their
  wallet to spend on other files==.
- ==No refunds from the site==; a disputed purchase is handled in Stripe and by
  hand.
- ==The market lists the 200 most recently updated files, with no search yet==.

## Read mode in the card editor [#cardmirror-read-mode]

Read mode (the **Read mode** toolbar button in Debate Editor) ==hides everything
you would not read aloud==, so ==each card shows only what you read in round==:

1. **The tag**, ==the card's one-line summary==.
2. **The first author's last name and the year's last two digits**, in bold,
   such as **Smith 21**, ==in place of the whole citation==. ==Read mode reads
   them from the cite line==, whether it starts with a short cite like *Smith
   21*, *Smith '21*, *Smith & Jones 21* or *Smith et al. 2021*, or is written
   out like *John Smith, 2021*. Co-authors are dropped, so *Smith & Jones 21*
   reads **Smith 21**. ==When the author or the year can't be found, read mode
   shows the whole citation line instead==, so you still see who wrote it.
   Turn on **Read mode: keep entire cite** ==to always see the whole citation==.
3. **The card text you read**: ==only the highlighted words when the card has
   any highlighting==, or ==only the underlined (and emphasized) words when it has
   none==, so ==a card that hasn't been highlighted yet still reads==.

==Each card is checked on its own==, so ==one document can mix highlighted cards and
cards that are only underlined==. **Convert Cards to Read Mode** ==writes the same
result into the document==: the tag, the *Smith 21* cite, and the highlighted
(or underlined) text.

### How it works [#cardmirror-read-mode-how-it-works]

==Read mode never edits the document; it only hides text==. For each card it
==checks whether any body text is highlighted== (background color counts too when
**Read mode: show background color** is on), and it ==reads the first author
and year from the cite== (*Smith 21*, *Smith '21*, *Smith & Jones 21*, *Smith
et al. 2021*, or a full citation). Read mode then shows **Smith 21** ==as a
bold label at the start of the cite and hides the cite's own text== (only when
both the author and a year were found; otherwise the whole cite stays visible),
and ==picks highlight or underline per card== for the body and any table inside
it. ==A card is re-checked whenever you change it==.

### Good to know [#cardmirror-read-mode-good-to-know]

- ==Cites outside a card, and analytics, keep the old rule== (their marked text).
- A cite whose first words are not the author (for example an organization
  spelled out in a sentence) ==can produce the wrong name==; turn on **keep entire
  cite** ==for those documents==.
- ==Read aloud speaks its own short cite== (the cite's first word and first year),
  ==which can differ from read mode's label on unusual cites==.

## Research Publishers [#research-publishers]

==Give your files an author, a publisher and the students who wrote them==, and
==let other debaters subscribe to your research group==. Every research
publisher has its own page, and ==every file published under it shows who
published it, right in the editor's sidebar==.

### A file's details [#research-publishers-a-files-details]

Open one of your files. Under **My Personal Documents** in the sidebar, the
**About this file** note shows its details: the author, ==who published it==,
the research group and its students, the seasons it covers and the date it
was published. ==Choose== **Edit details** ==to change them in a dialog==.

- **Published by** lists the publishers you run. Pick **New publisher…** to
  ==create one right there==, with a name and a monthly price.
- ==Click the publisher's name in the note to open its page==.

Find it at **/research/publishers/**`name`. It shows ==the research group, its

If you run the publisher, **Edit page** changes its name, description,
research group, students, seasons, website and monthly price.

### Subscribing [#research-publishers-subscribing]

**Subscribe** ==pays one month (30 days) from your wallet to the publisher==.
==A free publisher costs nothing to follow==. ==While your subscription lasts,
every file on the page has== **Save a copy**, which ==puts the whole file in your
own files and opens it==. Add money to your wallet from the same page if it
doesn't cover the price (see [File Market](/docs/features/research-evidence#file-market)).

### Good to know [#research-publishers-good-to-know]

- ==Subscriptions don't renew by themselves==; choose **Renew** once a month
  runs out.
- ==You can only publish under a publisher you run==.
- ==Files you copy are yours to edit==; later changes by the publisher don't
  reach your copy.
