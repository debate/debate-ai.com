# Team Rankings

==A leaderboard of debate teams and debaters for every season from 2015-16 to
now==, for PF, LD, Policy (CX) and college policy (NDT). ==Ratings and TOC bids
come from live Elo ratings== compiled from Tabroom round results and openCaselist. ==For the current season, each
team's aff/neg win rates, round count and the field's side bias are merged in
from this site's own ballot-level Bradley-Terry fit== (see
[How Team Ratings Are Computed](/docs/features/ai-coaching/bradley-terry-debate-rankings));
past seasons show the Elo ratings alone. LD's Sep–Oct topic slice still
comes from the Bradley-Terry fit only.

- **Route:** `/coaching/rankings`
- **Package:** `debate-rankings` (the page, under `./leaderboard`; the Elo ratings loader in `src/extinction.ts`), `debate-rankings-sync-data` (the current season's win rates)

## What it shows

Pick a division tab. ==Every column below can be sorted by clicking its
header==, and the ⓘ next to a header ==explains how the number is worked out==.

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Rank | Position by Elo rating |
| Name / School | The team (`Last & Last`) or LD debater, and their school |
| Rating | The Elo rating on the site's 0–10.9 scale — what the rank is sorted on |
| Bids | High school divisions: Tournament of Champions bids earned that season, from the season's bid list. Hover the count to see the tournaments they came from. A dash means no bids |
| Matches | Current season only: decided rounds debated, aff plus neg. A dash in past seasons |
| Aff Win / Neg Win | Current season only: share of rounds won on each side, from the Bradley-Terry fit's ballots, matched to the Elo-rated team by debaters and school |
| Aff Elim Win / Neg Elim Win | Share of elimination rounds won on each side; a dash means no elims on that side |
| ID | A short stable id for the entry across tournaments |

Above the table: the number of ranked entries, ==the side bias across the
whole field== (one chart with two aff vs. neg bars, prelims and elims, and the aff
side's rating advantage from the model's side term), and ==the list of
tournaments the ratings come from, with majors marked==. ==A filter box narrows
the table by name or school==.

### TOC bids

==The Bids column on PF, LD and Policy comes from the TOC bid list for
the selected season==: each team's distinct bid tournaments, joined to its
rating by the exact team code. College policy has no TOC, so no Bids column.

If the Elo ratings can't be reached, the current season falls back to the
Bradley-Terry rankings alone, and the Policy tab's Bids column falls back to
==a community-maintained Google Sheet of this season's high school policy bids== (columns Bids, Team, Debaters,
State, Bid 1, Bid 2, …). The site downloads the sheet as CSV through
`/api/toc-bids` and ==keeps it for up to an hour==, so ==edits to the sheet show on
the rankings within the hour==. If Google can't be reached, ==a copy of the sheet
bundled with the site is shown instead==.

==Sheet rows are matched to ranked teams by their debaters' last names (in
either order) and school==, ==allowing for differently spelled school names==
("Barack Obama MLA" and "Barack Obama Male Leadership Academy"). A team listed
under two schools, such as an independent entry and its home school, ==gets
the bids from both rows==. ==Teams on the sheet that aren't in the rankings yet
are left out==. The parser and matcher live in
`packages/debate-rankings/src/sync/sync-toc-bids-sheet.ts`; the bundled
copy is `toc-bids-snapshot.ts` next to it.

### Schools tab

The **Schools** tab ==rolls every ranked entry up by school== (All events, or one
division). ==Schools are ranked by their== **Balanced** score (below).

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Best Rating | Highest adjusted rating among the school's entries |
| Top Entry | The entry holding that rating, and its event |
| Avg Rating | Mean adjusted rating across every ranked team (or LD debater) from the school |
| Balanced | What the rank is sorted on: 1.1 × (0.7 × the average of the school's top three entries + 0.3 × (½ Avg Rating + ½ depth)), capped at 10.9. Depth grows with the number of teams and reaches 10 at 15. Schools under four teams are diluted (×0.80 for one team, ×0.87 for two, ×0.93 for three) |
| Teams | Number of ranked entries |
| Events | Events the school has entries in |

When any listed school has attended the National Debate Tournament (College NDT, or All events), an **NDT** column adds its record, in this order: ==NDT titles, CEDA national titles and NDT elim qualifiers== (a title count of zero is left out), for example `1 NDT title · 12 CEDA titles · 61 elims`. It reads the same NDT school history as the school page.

The table is ==worked out ahead of time on the server==, not in your browser.
Each season's school table (every scope, plus the NDT column) is saved in the
database once; the current season is ==rebuilt every day at 06:00 UTC== and
only rewritten when something changed. ==Your browser keeps its own copy== and
shows it at once, checking for a newer one at most once a day (once a week for
a finished season). If the server can't answer and there is no saved copy, the
browser works the table out itself, as it used to.

Ranking on **Balanced** ==is led by a school's best three entries==, with
==average quality and depth as the rest==. ==A school with one or two strong
entries is diluted==, ==so it doesn't top the table over a deep program whose top
three are just as strong==.

### Legendary and gold rows

In every division table and on the Schools tab, ==a small group at the top is==
**Legendary**: ==a thicker gold border, a soft glow and a small crowned
label naming the format==, such as "PF Legend", "LD Legend" or "NDT Legend"
(after the team's label in division tables, beside the rank on the Schools
tab, where it reads just "Legend" with all events shown). ==Each list sets its own cutoff==: it counts the
rows rated 9.0 or more and the rows rated 8.0 or more, and ==uses whichever count
is closer to five== (on a list with nothing in the 8s it keeps going down a
whole point at a time). So LD, with four teams in the 9s and fourteen in the
8s, has four legendary teams, while a list whose best rating is in the 8s takes
everyone rated 8.0+. ==A list
never has more than seven legendary rows==, and ==searching doesn't change who is
legendary==. Any other row whose rating reads 8.0 or more (a bold leading **8**
or **9**) ==gets a thin gold border==. The division tables use the adjusted
rating; the Schools tab uses the Balanced score.

### Team labels

Everywhere the site names a team (the rankings, the Schools tab's Top Entry,
the College NDT history tables, team and school pages, the matchup simulator, video cards and rows, the watch page and tournament
pairings and results), ==it draws the same== **team label**: ==a small bordered
box holding the school, then the debaters==. ==The school shows as its logo when
the site has one and by name otherwise==. ==The debaters show as their last names==
("Le & Luo", or just "Daswani" in LD) when the rankings or the tournament
know them, and ==otherwise as the initials from the team's code== ("LL"). When
the school has a logo, the debaters sit on a second row under it; a school
shown by name keeps the names beside it on one line. The
==border is blue for the affirmative and red for the negative==, around a mostly
white background with just a faint blue or red tint ==so logos stay readable==; where no side
applies, as in the rankings, it is blue. ==Last names always show in full==,
wrapping onto another line when space is tight. In the video library and on
the watch page ==a ranked team also carries its rank as a== **#N** badge inside
the box; ==hovering it shows the team's rating, rounds and aff/neg win rates==.

In the division tables the **Team** column holds that label (==the logo links
to the school's page, the names to the team's page==) and sorts by school. It
also ==shows each school's rank on the Schools tab== for that division as a
small **#N** after the label.

LD has a second tab, **Sep–Oct topic**, ==rated only from the tournaments on
the September–October resolution==.

The season selector still changes the champion and topic banner. ==Ratings
exist for the current season only==, so ==earlier seasons show the banner alone==,
with one exception: on **College NDT**, the 2009-10 through 2025-26 seasons
also show that season's top 100 teams as
[debatedata](https://debatedata.weebly.com) ==rated them==. Those tables come from
a different model (a skill estimate, Mu, with an uncertainty, Sigma, ranked on
a conservative Rating below Mu), so ==their numbers are on that site's own scale
and can't be compared with current-season ratings==. Seasons from 2010-11 through
2012-13 ==list schools by the short names the source used== ("Georgetown",
"Cal State Fullerton").

Each of those seasons has a **Win probability** calculator above the table.
Pick an aff and a neg team, or type any Mu and Sigma, and it ==shows the aff's
chance of winning one round==: ==each side's performance is drawn around its Mu==,
spread by its Sigma plus a fixed 4.17 points of round-to-round noise, and ==the
higher draw wins==. 2025-26 ==also shows debatedata's season totals across all
divisions== (schools, teams, debaters, rounds, and aff win rate overall and in
elims), and lists the 27
tournaments its ratings come from under the table. ==Every team in those tables
links to its team page.==

## Team and school pages

Clicking a team or school ==opens its profile at an== `@` handle. ==A team's
handle is its school plus its debaters' last names== (`/@strath-haven-yang-smith`
for a pair, `/@harker-daswani` for an LD debater), and a school's is its name
(`/@michigan`) ==without "University"==: Emory University is `/@emory`, the
University of Kansas `/@kansas`, and Yale University (CT) `/@yale`, so every
link to a school, whether it spells the full name or the short one, opens the
same page. ==Older full-name links== (`/@emory-university`) ==still open the
school and switch the address to its handle==. ==A handle that names a ranked school opens the school==;
otherwise it opens the team with that handle. ==Older initials links==
(`/@strath-haven-ys`) ==still open the team and switch the address to the
last-names handle==, and the older `/teams/<team>` and `/schools/<school>`
==links redirect to the handle==. ==A
profile shows its stats per division, its radar chart, the round
videos it debated in, and its caselist documents==. A school page also shows
==its logo and its roster of teams and students when a coach or administrator
has claimed it== — see [Claiming a School Page](/docs/features/team-collaboration/school-pages).

When the school has a logo, ==the logo at the top of a team or school page
stands in for the school's name== and ==clicking it opens the school's page==;
a school with no logo is still named in text. ==A small **i** icon sits in the
logo's lower-left corner==: hovering or tapping it shows the trademark notice,
that ==the school's name and logo belong to the school, are shown only to
identify it, and do not mean the school is affiliated with or endorses Debate
AI==. Its link opens the full clause in the
[Terms and Privacy Policy](/legal/privacy#school-trademarks).

### Caselist section

Below a team's videos, ==the **Caselist** section shows the team's
openCaselist page== for the division you are viewing (PF, LD, HS Policy or
NDT/CEDA), searching this season's caselist and then last season's. It has two
tabs, both filterable by side (Aff/Neg, or Pro/Con in PF):

- **Rounds & files**: ==every disclosed round== with its tournament, round,
  side, opponent, judge and round report, and ==a download button for the
  round's open-source `.docx`==.
- **Cited arguments**: ==the team's wiki cites==. Each argument title expands
  to its cite text, with a copy button and **Expand all**.

==The team is matched by school name and debater last names==; an
**openCaselist page** link opens the original. openCaselist only answers
signed-in requests, so the server needs a `CASELIST_TOKEN` secret (or
`CASELIST_USERNAME` and `CASELIST_PASSWORD`, a Tabroom login). ==Without one, or
when no caselist page matches, the section shows the documents already
imported from the bulk archives instead==. Lookups are cached for six hours and
files for 30 days, because openCaselist allows ten downloads a minute.

### Past-season team pages

A college policy team that ==isn't in the current season's rankings== still has
a page when an older source names it: a past season's debatedata table, the
NDT school history or the CEDA champions list. Its name links there from the
past-season tables, a school's NDT and CEDA sections, and the video list's Aff
and Neg cells. ==The data is limited to what those sources kept==: each season
the team made debatedata's top 100 (rank, Rating, Mu and Sigma on that site's
scale), its NDT results (elim round, record and seed, first-round bids,
Copeland Award) and its CEDA titles, then ==the library's college policy rounds
for the school and the team's initials==. There is no radar, simulator or
Follow button, since those need current-season ratings.

The address is ==the school's short name plus the debaters' last names==
(`/@kansas-katz-robinson`, `/@wake-forest-louis-kingston`). Every source
reduces to the same address, so ==an NDT entry, its CEDA title and its
debatedata seasons share one page==. Older links still open the page and
==switch the address bar to the short one==: the full school and names
(`/@university-of-kansas-will-katz-quaram-robinson`) and the school plus
initials (`/@university-of-kansas-kr`). The current season's table links
its teams to their ranked profile instead.

A school page for a college that has attended the National Debate Tournament
also has a **College NDT record** section: ==NDT championships, elim
qualifiers== (round, debaters, prelim record and seed), first-round at-large
bids, Copeland Award winners, speaker awards and coaching awards, ==each year
linking to that year's NDT results==. ==It comes from the NDT's
published school history== (last updated 2025-10-22) and ==is matched to the
school by name== (`Harvard University (MA)` is the rankings' `Harvard`). When a
college and a university share a name, ==a plain name means the university==
(`Georgetown` is Georgetown University (DC), not Georgetown College (KY)) and
==a name that says "College" means the college== (`Boston College`). A state
system's campuses answer to the circuit's short forms (`UC Berkeley`, `Cal`,
`UT Dallas`, `UMKC`), and ==the bare system name means the main campus==:
`Texas` is UT Austin, never UT Arlington. ==A school the
NDT has no record of shows no section==.

The section's summary leads with **==Championships, NDT + CEDA==**: the school's
NDT titles and CEDA national titles added into one total, with the split
(`3 NDT · 12 CEDA`) underneath, then its best finish. ==It then shows the last
decade of NDTs first== (the ten years ending with the latest NDT in the history,
2016–2025 today): **elim qualifiers** in that span and the **==elim finish
rank==**; ==then the same two across every year==. ==Each elim entry is
scored by the round it reached==, Doubles 1, Octas 2, Quarters 3, Semis 4,
Finals 5 and Champion 6, and ==the school's mean score is ranked against every
college's mean for the same span==, deepest first. ==The tile shows only the
rank, never a round name==: Wake Forest's all-years tile reads **#4** with
`of 61 schools · 76 elims` underneath. ==Every team in elims counts, so a year
with two teams there counts twice==. ==Only schools with at least 3 elims in the
span are ranked==, so one lucky run can't top the table; a school with fewer
shows "—" and "unranked", and a school with no elims in the span shows "—" and
"no elims".

Beside the section's title, **All seasons** opens a ==ranking of every NDT
team and school since 1947 by all-seasons NDT points==. A team is one pair of
debaters at one school across every season they debated together (Georgetown's
Arsht & Markoff, Northwestern's Miles & Vellayappan), so ==a debater with two
partners has two teams== (Harvard's Herman & Sanjeev and Midha & Sanjeev). Each
row shows its total, ==its breakdown by elims, Copeland, bids and speakers==
with the counts behind them (`Elims 72 (3 elims, 2 titles)`), and the seasons
it scored in. Teams link to their past-team pages and schools to their school
pages; ==the school whose page you opened is highlighted==, its school rank
and best team are shown at the top, and a filter box and an "Only this school"
box narrow the list. The points use the same weights as the program strength
chart below:

| NDT result | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Each NDT a team broke at, by the deepest round reached | Doubles 3, Octas 5, Quarters 8, Semis 12, Finals 18, Champion 30 |
| Copeland Award (the season's top first-round at-large seed) | 12 |
| Each first-round at-large bid | 4 |
| Each NDT speaker award, credited to the speaker's team | top speaker 10, 2nd–5th 5, 6th–10th 3, 11th–20th 1 |

==A title is Champion, not Finals plus a bonus==, and ==the Copeland winner also
holds a first-round bid, so it scores both== (16 for the season before the NDT).
A school's total adds every team it sent, ==plus speaker awards that can't be
matched to exactly one of its listed teams that year== (a speaker whose team
didn't break or get a bid). ==CEDA titles and top-100 ranks don't count here==;
they are in the program strength chart. The modal prints the same table under
**How the points work**.

A college that has won the CEDA (Cross Examination Debate Association)
national tournament also has a **CEDA national championships** section
==listing each title year and the winning team, newest first==, from CEDA's
history of winners (1986 through 2026; the 2020 tournament was canceled).
==A school that has since been renamed shows the name it won under==, such as
Central State University for the University of Central Oklahoma. Names
must match exactly once "University", "College" and state tags are dropped,
==so Texas State never shows Texas's title==.

A college school page also opens its history with **Program strength by
season**: ==a bar chart of the program's strength score for every season from
its first result to the latest==, labeled by the year the season ends, and
==its rank among every college program for the latest season, the last ten
seasons and all time== (`#3 of 85`). Hovering a bar breaks that season's score
into its parts. ==A season's score adds up what the program's teams did that
year==:

| Result | Points |
| --- | --- |
| NDT elims, per team by round reached | Doubles 3, Octas 5, Quarters 8, Semis 12, Finals 18, Champion 30 |
| Copeland Award (the NDT's top first-round seed) | 12 |
| CEDA national championship | 20 |
| NDT speaker award | top speaker 10, 2nd–5th 5, 6th–10th 3, 11th–20th 1 |
| First-round at-large bid, per team | 4 |
| Each of the program's best two teams in debatedata's top 100 (2009–10 on) | (101 − rank) ÷ 20, so #1 is 5 and #100 is 0.05 |
| Attending the NDT | 2 |

The NDT history, CEDA's winners and the top-100 tables are merged into one
program by the same name matching as the sections above, so ==`Kansas`,
`University of Kansas (KS)` and CEDA's `University of Kansas` are one
program==. The page prints the same formula under the chart. ==Only a
program's two best-ranked teams count each season==, so a big squad can't
outscore a strong one, and under the chart **Best ranked teams by season**
==lists those two teams and their ranks for every season, newest first==.

==Everything about a school's past sits under one **Historical legend**
heading==: the strength chart, then the NDT and CEDA records on a college page,
or the strength chart and the Tournament of Champions record on a high school
page.

A high school page has its own **Program strength by season** chart, ==one
stacked bar per season from the school's first Policy, LD or PF finish at the
TOC (results go back to 1972) to now, split into Policy, Lincoln-Douglas and
Public Forum==. Rankings start in 2015–16, so ==earlier seasons score TOC
finishes only==. For each season and event ==it takes the
school's best one or two entries by Elo rank== (rank among
every entry rated that season) and scores them:

| Result | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Each of the school's best two entries per event in the top 100 | (101 − rank) ÷ 20, so #1 is 5 and #100 is 0.05 |
| Each of those entries that qualified for the TOC (2+ bids) | 4 |
| TOC finish in Policy, LD or PF | champion 30, runner-up 18, top speaker 10 |

Above the chart are the school's best season and ==its rank among every high
school for the last ten seasons and all time== (`#4 of 2,310`), counting only
finished seasons; a school that only placed at the TOC before rankings began
is ranked too. Under those, ==its Tournament of Champions championships and
top speaker awards== (every event, as in the TOC section). ==Ranks are
precomputed every day== (with the Rankings → Schools table) and whenever a past
season is first loaded; until every past season is in, the card says how many
are still missing and the ranks count only the seasons loaded so far. Under it, **Best ranked
entries by season** ==lists each season's entries per event with their rank
and bids, newest first==. ==The current season counts the rankings so far==;
finished seasons are built once on the server from the saved ratings
files, so the very first visits after a new season can take a few seconds to
fill in. ==School names must match the rankings' spelling==, so a school
listed under another name in an older season misses that season.

==The strength chart runs across the top of a college's history, with the NDT
record and CEDA titles side by side under it on wide screens==. ==The NDT
record's lists (titles, elims, bids, speakers, coaches) sit in two columns, and
each long list scrolls inside its own box== instead of stretching the page.

A high school that has placed at the Tournament of Champions (TOC) has a
**Tournament of Champions** section: ==its TOC titles, runner-ups and
top speakers==, the span of years, then ==every finish by event== (Policy,
Lincoln-Douglas, Gold and Silver Public Forum, World Schools, Congress and the
speech events) with the competitors, newest first. ==It comes from the
University of Kentucky's official TOC results==: champions, runners-up and top
speakers, policy from 1972. A close-out, where teammates split the title without debating the
final, and the shared 2006 Public Forum title ==count as titles for both==. ==A
school's spellings count as one school== (`Harvard School`, `Harvard Westlake`
and `Harvard-Westlake` are all Harvard-Westlake), and ==a name shared by schools
in two states matches only with its state tag==: `Highland Park (MN)` and
`Highland Park (TX)` are different schools, so a bare `Highland Park` shows no
section. `Montgomery` ==never shows Montgomery Bell's record==.

==The NDT and CEDA sections show only on college school pages and the TOC
section only on high school pages==. A school ranked in College Policy is a
college; one ranked only in high school divisions is a high school, so ==a
Texas high school never shows UT Austin's NDT record==. A claimed school with
no ranked entries counts as a college when its name says "University" or
"College" (but not "College Prep").

==A team page opens with a blue record card==: the team name, school and Follow
button, ==a pill per division it is ranked in== (click one to switch the card and
radar to that division), and each division's rating. Below that ==the stats are
grouped into three bands==: **Standing** (rating, rank, field percentile,
matches), **Win rates** (aff, neg, elim aff and elim neg) and **Rating model**
(argument-type splits). The raw rating and its deviation aren't shown; the
rating everywhere on a team page is the adjusted one.

==The radar reads the same way everywhere==: **Ranking** at the top, **Matches**
at the bottom, ==the aff spokes== (**Aff win**, **Elim aff**) ==on the left== and the
neg spokes (**Neg win**, **Elim neg**) ==on the right==.

Under a team's radar chart, a **Show school average** checkbox (on by default)
==overlays a dashed polygon with the average of every ranked team from the same
school== in that division, ==so you can see where the team beats its school==. The
checkbox only appears when the school has another ranked team in the division.

The **Videos** section ==lists rounds, not lectures, and only in the divisions
the team or school is ranked in== — a school ranked only in Policy shows only
Policy rounds, one ranked only in NDT only college rounds. ==A round counts when
its recorded aff or neg team names the school as whole words==; the title is
searched only for a round with no teams recorded, and ==the description and
channel never are==. The school ==is matched by its full name and by its short
forms==: trailing words like *HS*, *Independent* or *College Prep* are dropped
(`Gunn HS Independent` → `Gunn`) and ==known circuit shorthand is added==
(`Glenbrook North` → `GBN`). ==A team page also requires the team's initials
after the school== (`Strake Jesuit FS`, in either order), and ==an LD debater
also matches by full name==. Clicking a card's badge ==narrows the list further==.

## Following a team or school

==Every team and school page has a== **Follow** button with its follower count
beside it. ==Signed out, the count still shows== and the button reads *Sign in to
follow*. ==Following adds that team or school to the== **Following** filter of
the [News Stream](/docs/features/team-collaboration/news-stream) (`/news`), which posts:

- **New rounds**: ==its three latest round videos, with who won on which side==.
- **New research**: ==its two latest caselist documents==.
- **Tournament results**: ==one line per tournament in the last 120 days==, with
  the record and how far it went (*reached Quarterfinals*, *finished
  runner-up*, *won the tournament*).
- **Monthly recaps**: for each of the last three completed months it debated
  in, ==the month's record, its deep elim runs called out as big results, and
  an estimated rating change==.

==A user can follow up to 50 teams and schools==. Follows are stored in the
`profile_follows` table (migration in `packages/debate-videos/migrations`)
and served by `/api/follows`; the feed items are built in `debate-videos`'
`lib/follows/follow-feed.ts` and loaded by its `useFollowingNews` hook.

## Picking an opponent when creating a round

==The Create New Round dialog reads the same rankings==. Once a school is
entered on either side, ==up to five of that school's teams in the round's
format appear as round badges== under the field, best rank first. ==Clicking a
badge fills that side's debater names== (`Falk & Sabnani` fills 1st and 2nd
speaker; an LD entry fills the one debater). ==A side's badges go away once its
debaters are filled in==, so after both teams are entered none are left.

The tournament field ==suggests Tabroom tournaments running now or coming up
as soon as it is clicked==, running ones first. When the round's tournament is
one of them, ==its schools in the round's format are listed first== in the
(scrollable) school dropdown, and ==the badges show that school's entries at
the tournament instead of its ranked teams==. ==Badges stay hidden for formats
without rankings== and for schools with no teams. The lookups live in
`debate-round`'s `round/school-teams.ts` and `round/tournament-field.ts`; the
tournament data is read through `@debate-ai/tournaments/client` from the app's
`/api/tabroom-beta` proxy.

## Matchup simulator

==Pair any two ranked teams in a division and see who the ratings favour==. The
simulator reads each division's full-season rankings
(`<division>_full_rankings.csv`), so ==every ranked team can be picked== — filter
by name or school, or hit **Random opponent**.

==Each side is simulated separately==: team A locked **aff**, then team A locked
**neg**, ==so you can see which side a team does better on==. Pick **Aff**,
**Neg** or **Both**, and **Prelim** (one judge), **Elim** (3- and 5-judge
panels) or **Both**. With both sides shown, ==it says which side team A should
take on a coin flip==, and by how many points. **Simulate 1,000 rounds per
side** ==plays each side out to show the spread a percentage hides==.

### What goes into the rating

==Each team's rating is a blend of three signals==, set by two sliders:

- **Team**: ==the pairing's own rating==.
- **Individual performance**: ==how each debater rates in their *other*
  entries at the same school==, in any division (other partners, or LD). A
  pair that only does well together, or a debater who carries every partner,
  ==shows up here==. Teams whose debaters have no other entries ==just use the
  team rating==.
- **School performance**: ==the average rating of every entry the school has
  in the division==. This is ==program strength (coaching, shared files) rather
  than the pair itself==.

Under each team the simulator shows all three numbers, the blend, and a
**recommended** mix ==for that team==. ==A team with few rounds of its own leans
more on its school== (15 rounds → 50%, 60 → 20%). A team whose debaters have
other entries ==leans more on those, up to half==. ==Click it to apply it==.

==The side effect has two parts==: the field's aff/neg split for the stage (elims
often lean further than prelims), and each team's own aff-vs-neg record
beyond the field's, ==shrunk toward zero for teams with few rounds==. A team's
elim-only rates come from a handful of rounds, so ==its tilt always comes from
its overall record==.

==The base probability is Glicko-2's expected score==, the same formula as
upstream's `simulate_round.py` (skelo's `Glicko2Model.compute_prob`): ratings
are converted back to Glicko-2's 1500 scale, and the result is
`1 / (1 + exp(−g(φ_opp) · (μ − μ_opp)))`. ==The side shift is added in
log-odds==. ==Panel odds assume each ballot is independent at that probability==.

### Your team, pre-filled

**Settings → General → My team** ==sets your role== (debater, coach, or assistant/judge),
your partner, and ==the ranked team you represent in each division==. It's saved
in this browser and, when you're signed in, to your account
(`/api/settings`' `myRankedTeams`). ==The simulator starts from it==.

It appears in two places:

- **Practice round** (`/practice`): ==side A starts as your team for the
  division==, and both sides can be changed.
- **Every team profile** (`/teams/<team>`): if you have a team set for that
  division, **Simulate vs. any ranked team** ==pits your team (side A) against
  the profile's team==. On your own profile, or with no team set, ==the profile's
  team is locked in as side A== and any other ranked team can be its opponent.

==The math lives in== `@debate-ai/rankings`: `match-simulation.ts` (Glicko-2)
and `matchup-model.ts` (blending, side terms, recommendations). The UI is
`debate-rankings`'s `MatchupSimulator` (from its `./leaderboard` entry), composed on
the practice page by `debate-practice-rounds`' `TeamMatchupSimulatorPanel`. The setting is
`debate-rankings`'s `leaderboard/my-ranked-teams` and `MyRankedTeamsSettings`.

## Known gaps

The **All seasons** NDT ranking ==starts first-round bids and the Copeland Award in
1973==, when they began, so earlier teams can only score elims and speakers. A
pair is matched by last names at one school, so ==two partnerships there with
the same last names are merged==, and a speaker award goes to a team only when
the speaker's full name matches one listed team that year (about 9% of awards
count for the school only). The NDT history stops at the 2025 NDT.

The Schools tab can be ==up to a day behind== the division tables for the
current season: it shows the copy saved by the last daily rebuild, while a
division table loads the live ratings.

The matchup simulator's individual rating ==finds a debater's other entries by
surname and school==, so ==two debaters with the same surname at one school are
merged==, and ==someone who changed schools isn't linked==. ==The rankings carry no
speaker points==, so individual skill is only inferred from other pairings.
The partner name in **My team** ==is informational and doesn't change the
model==.

A ranked team's handle uses last names only, so ==two partnerships at one
school with the same last names share a page==, and an old initials link that
fits several pairs at one school ==shows them all on one page== without
switching the address.

An old school-plus-initials link ==matches by school and initials==, so two
pairs from one school with the same initials in different seasons ==show on
one page== there (the short link keeps them apart). Two partnerships at one
school with the same last names would share a short link. debatedata's
tables only go back to 2009-10 and list the top 100, so an older or lower
team shows only its NDT or CEDA results, and a team in none of the sources
has no page. Its Videos section ==searches by initials== and so can show
another pair's rounds.

A profile's Videos section ==only finds a round whose team label or title spells
the school the way the rankings do==, or by one of the short forms above. ==A
round recorded under an unlisted abbreviation is missed==, and a school whose
short name another school shares (two *Memorial* high schools) ==can show the
other's rounds==.

Followed teams' results and recaps ==come from the round video library==, so ==a
tournament's record counts only the rounds someone recorded==, and ==a round is
dated by its upload, not the day it was debated==. The rankings keep no
history, so ==a recap's rating change is an estimate==: each decided round moves
the rating by 32 × (result − expected) on the upstream scale, the expected
score coming from both teams' current ratings. ==Nothing is emailed==; the recap
only appears in the News Stream.

==Ratings cover only the tournaments listed above the table==; ==a team that hasn't
attended one of them is not ranked==. ==The list updates when the rankings are
recomputed, not live==.
