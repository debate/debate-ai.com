## Card Sharing & Co-editing [#card-sharing-co-editing]

==Send cards straight into a teammate's editor and edit a document together
live==, ==using the account you are signed in with== — the same one that syncs your
files and settings. ==There is no separate membership to link==.

Works in any Debate Editor surface: the REASON editor and speech documents.

### Turning it on

==There is nothing to turn on==. Sign in, and card sharing and co-editing are
ready on every device you use. ==Your name on cards you send and on your live
cursor comes from your account==. There are no pairing codes, recipient lists
or collaboration settings to fill in.

### Sending and receiving cards

==Cards teammates send you land in the== **Receive** pill next to the dropzone.
To share with a teammate, add them under **Contacts** and use **Share with
contacts**. ==Both of you need to be signed in==. ==Cards wait on the server for
up to a week if your editor is closed==.

### Editing a document together

Start a co-editing session from the Send pill's invite button, the **Start
Collaboration Session** command, or **Share with contacts** in the
editor. ==Everyone sees each other's cursors and edits as they type==.
==Starting a session needs you signed in==; ==someone you invite can join from the
invite link even without an account==.

==Everything is end-to-end encrypted in the editor before it leaves your
browser== — ==the server only ever passes along scrambled data it can't read==.

## Collaboration Prep Room [#collaboration-prep-room]

==A topic's shared prep space==: a switcher for every topic with either a
tracked-argument checklist or submitted evidence, ==a keyword search over
that topic's evidence and draft blocks==, and the topic's ==coverage-gap
research tasks routed to available contributors==.

Open it from the **Sprint** tab of the [Research Workspace](/docs/features/research-evidence#research-collaboration).

### What it shows

- A topic switcher and a one-line summary of the topic's prep.
- A keyword search over the topic's evidence and draft blocks.
- The research tasks routed to contributors.
- An "active now" roster of who is working on the topic.
- A timeline of the room's activity.

A **Shared task checklist** ==lets any teammate add a freeform todo item,
toggle it done, and remove it==.

### Good to know

- Everything in the room ==syncs to your account== when you're signed in, but
  ==it is yours alone, not one shared room==: each teammate ==keeps their own
  copy==.
- ==There are no reviewer permissions==.
- There is no shared file or attachment area yet.

## Contribution Leaderboard [#contribution-leaderboard]

==Ranks community contributors== — cards, summaries, analytics, and other
research contributions — ==by a blended helpfulness score==, and ==shows each
contributor's unlock tier, earned badges, and current daily-quest streak==.

Find it at **/coaching/leaderboard**.

### What it shows

| Column | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Rank | Position by helpfulness score |
| Contributor | Who made the contributions |
| Tier | novice/apprentice/veteran/expert |
| Contributions | How many contributions they've made |
| Total/Avg score | Blended helpfulness score |
| Streak | Current consecutive-day quest streak |
| Badges | Tier + streak-milestone badges |

A **Range** filter (All time/This week/This month) and a **Category**
filter ==re-derive the whole roster==. Each row has a "History" toggle showing
endorsement history.

### Good to know

- ==There are no contributor roles or permissions yet==.
- "My endorsement activity" ==only appears for a signed-in visitor==.

## Contributions Feed [#contributions-feed]

==Lets a contributor submit a contribution== (a card, summary, highlight,
annotation, original argument, or refutation), then ==lists every contribution ranked by blended helpfulness score==, with
Like/Save/Endorse actions per entry.

Open it from the **Rewards** tab of the [Research Workspace](/docs/features/research-evidence#research-collaboration).

### What it shows

A submission form followed by the ranked feed. Each entry shows its kind
badge, contributor, helpfulness score, likes/saves/endorsement counts, and
Like/Save/Endorse buttons. A "Flagged for review" moderator toggle ==narrows
the feed to popularity-driven outliers==.

A **Comments** toggle on each entry opens ==the same threaded discussion== used
on video watch pages and forum threads (signed-in accounts can comment and
like). ==Comments are collapsed until you open them==.

### Good to know

- ==A new contribution starts with a neutral quality score==.
- The Contributor ID is ==filled in for you, not locked==: you can still submit
  under another typed ID.

## Top Contributor Awards [#contributor-awards]

==Recognizes the top contributor in each contribution category== by total
helpfulness score within that category, and ==lets a day's standings be
frozen as an official "announced" result==.

Find it at **/research/cards/awards**.

### What it shows

One card per contribution category that has entries (Best Evidence Finder, Best Explainer, etc.), plus **Live standings**, **Announce
today's awards**, a **Hall of Fame**, **Announced history**, and **Peer
Nominations** with a "Second" action.

### Good to know

- ==Awards are not announced automatically==; someone has to press **Announce
  today's awards**.
- ==Anyone can nominate or second==; there are no permissions on it.
- ==Announced awards and nominations sync to your account== when you're signed
  in, but they are ==yours alone, not shared with the team==.

Open it from the **Review** tab of the [Research Workspace](/docs/features/research-evidence#research-collaboration).

Every review with its status, lifecycle action buttons, a

### Good to know

==The reviewer is a typed name, not a verified account== — signing in only
fills it in for you.

### Good to know

- ==Anyone can reassign tasks or set priority==; there is no coach-only
  permission yet.

## Contacts & Shared Cards [#contacts]

==The account-linked half of Debate Editor's real-time collaboration==. Co-editing
sessions ==used to reach a partner only over the clipboard==: start a session,
copy the share code or invite link, paste it somewhere. With Contacts, ==a
signed-in user has a contacts list==, ==can share the document they're editing
straight to a contact's account==, and the shared card shows up as **available**
==on that account on every device they sign in on==.

Find it at **/contacts** (tabs: Contacts and Shared cards). It's also in the
dock's Settings menu ("Contacts", with a count of pending requests), the Tools
page's Prep & Practice group, the editor sidebar's Documents row (the share icon
beside Upload), and `t contacts` in the Ctrl/Cmd-K search palette.

### Contacts list [#contacts-contacts-list]

==A typical friends list, saved to your account==:

| Action | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Add by name or email search, or by typing an email | A **pending** request. If that person already sent *you* one, it's accepted on the spot. |
| Accept / Decline (incoming) | Accept makes you contacts; decline removes the request. |
| Cancel (outgoing) / Remove (accepted) | Removes it; either side can remove an accepted contact. |
| Block | One-sided. Removes the contact *and stops every shared card* between the two of you, both directions. Blocked people can't send you requests or shares, and their attempts get the same generic refusal as any other, so they can't tell they're blocked. |
| Unblock | Lifts the block. Nothing removed by it is restored. |

Contacts show an **online / away** dot. ==A contact counts as online while they've
had the site open in the last two minutes==, so the dot can lag a little.

==Requests and accepts also send you a notification==.

### Organizations [#contacts-organizations]

An **organization** ==is a shared group, like a team or a school squad==. The
account menu no longer has an **Organizations** submenu (it was taken out of
the user menu in November 2026), so for now there is no button to create or
switch organizations; one you already picked stays active.

==The organization you pick changes what this page shows==:

| | Personal | An organization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contacts list | Your accepted contacts | The organization's other members (minus blocks either way); Remove is hidden, since members aren't contacts |
| Shared cards | Every live share | Only shares with its members |
| Who you can share with | Accepted contacts | Accepted contacts and its members |

==Members are added by an owner or admin from== **their own contacts** (Members of
… → Add), so ==nobody lands in a group without a contact who trusts them==; ==there
are no invitation emails==.

### Sharing a card [#contacts-sharing-a-card]

In Debate Editor, **Share with contacts** ==opens a picker of your contacts plus
an optional note==. It's the share icon in the sidebar's Documents row, beside
New file / New folder / Upload — ==sharing is something done to the open file==.
==It only shows when you're signed in and a document is open==, since ==sharing
starts a live co-editing session==. On Share:

1. If the document has no live session yet, ==the editor's own Start
   Collaboration Session step runs== (same confirm dialog), and collaboration is
   switched on if it was off, since sharing *is* the opt-in.
2. ==Each contact you picked gets a notification==, and ==the card appears in their
   Shared cards tab marked== **Available** (and **New** until first opened). ==They
   also get an "invited you to collaborate" email== with your note and a link
   (see [Emails](/docs/features/team-collaboration#emails)).

==Shares only ever reach== **accepted contacts** with no block in either
direction; ==anyone else you picked is reported back as skipped==. Sharing the
same document with the same contact again ==refreshes the share rather than
duplicating it==. ==The owner can== **Stop sharing** from the Shared cards tab;
==the recipient can== **Dismiss** their copy.

The Shared cards tab also has a paste box: a share code or full invite link
copied from the editor ==can be shared to contacts from there==, if you started
the session the old way.

#### Privacy [#contacts-privacy]

==Co-editing is end-to-end encrypted==, and the server that passes edits between
you ==never sees the encryption key==. A share that follows a *person* across
devices does have to be kept with their account, so the site stores the share
code with it. ==The site already holds your editor documents, so this adds no new
kind of exposure==. See also [Card Sharing & Co-editing](/docs/features/team-collaboration#card-sharing-co-editing).

### Opening a shared card [#contacts-opening-a-shared-card]

**Open** on a received share opens Debate Editor, which then:

1. ==creates a== **fresh document** to hold the shared copy, so ==joining never
   overwrites the document you had open==;
2. joins the session ==the same way as pasting an invite link== (you confirm);
3. ==marks the share opened==.

==A share that arrives while the editor is open pops up with its own Open
button==. ==Co-editing needs the desktop layout==: on a phone ==the open is refused
with a message==.

### Your name on the cursor [#contacts-your-name-on-the-cursor]

Your cursor in a shared document shows your name. ==It's filled in from your
account the first time==, and ==never overwrites a name you typed== in the editor's
settings.

### Good to know [#contacts-good-to-know]

- ==Online status updates every half minute or so==, and a new share appears
  after the recipient reloads.
- ==Opening a shared card again always creates another fresh document==, though
  it rejoins the same session.
- ==A share goes to one person at a time==; there's no sharing to a whole
  organization at once yet.
- ==Organizations have no rename, leave, remove-member or role-change controls
  yet==, and no invitation by email.
- ==Your editor documents aren't separated by organization==.

## News & Forums [#news-stream]

==One page for what's new in the community==, so a debater ==doesn't have to
separately check several pages==. It has two tabs: **News**, ==the feed of new
rounds, new files and forum activity==, and **Forums**, ==the discussion
threads==. It is the "News & Forums" row in the sidebar's
Practice Debates section (formerly Coaching).

Find it at **/news**. Old forum links still work and open the Forums tab or the
thread they pointed to.

### Forums [#news-stream-forums]

==Anyone signed in can open a thread== from the form at the top of the tab,
and ==files it under a category==: General, Policy, Lincoln-Douglas, Public
Forum, College, Evidence & research, Tournaments, Coaching & judging or
Off-topic. Threads opened before categories existed are in General.

- **Categories** — ==the pills above the list show one category's threads==;
  "All" shows every one. Each category has its own link, so it can be shared or bookmarked.
- **Sort** — ==Recent activity== (the default: the thread most recently
  replied to is on top), ==New== (newest threads first; replies don't move
  them) or ==Top== (most liked first). The choice is kept in the link too.
- **Likes** — ==every thread has a heart==, in the list and on the thread
  page, and so does every reply. Signed-out readers see the counts but need
  to sign in to like.

Opening a thread shows the opening post and its replies as a tree, ==the way
Reddit does==: a reply sits under the comment it answers, every level opens
on arrival, and ==any comment can be folded to one line== with the `[–]`
button beside its time or by clicking the line that runs down beside its
replies. A folded comment shows its author and how many replies it hides;
`[+]` opens it again. The back link returns to the thread's category.

### What the News tab shows [#news-stream-what-the-news-tab-shows]

==Only new rounds, new files and forum activity, newest first==. ==A filter tab
appears for each category the feed has something in==:

- **Rounds** — ==the newest round videos on the site==.
- **Files** — ==the newest caselist files, plus one digest per day of new
  Argument Library cards== (the last three days). A digest lists a few card
  names, cleaned of Verbatim heading markers, then "and N more".
- **Forums** — ==each forum thread's latest activity==: "New thread" or "New
  reply in …". ==A new reply shows up as a new, unread item==.

Product updates, tool spotlights, Daily Best Card, Contributor Awards,
quest streaks, group challenges, revision incentives, prep notes and
coaching sessions ==are no longer posted here==; ==those features keep their own
pages==. ==Each item can be liked and is marked read on hover==.

**Following** ==shows updates from the teams and schools the reader follows==
from their profile pages: ==new rounds, new caselist research, tournament
results and monthly recaps== (see
[Team Rankings](/docs/features/team-collaboration#team-rankings-following-a-team-or-school)).
==The followed profiles are listed above the feed==, and their updates load just
after the rest of the page.

### Likes and read items follow your account [#news-stream-likes-and-read-items-follow-your-account]

==Your likes and read items sync to your account==. Unliking an item on one
device ==clears the like on your other devices the next time they load the page==.

## Practice Partners [#practice-partners]

==Lets debaters find each other for practice rounds between tournaments==.
==Volunteer to be challenged, volunteer to judge, or both==, and say which
formats, styles, speed, and level you're comfortable with. Then get matched
— ==anonymously — with a compatible debater and send them a request for a
virtual round==.

Open it from the **Practice Partners** tab of **Practice Debates**, in the
sidebar's **Practice Debates** section, beside [Online Challenges](/docs/features/rounds#online-challenges)
and Practice Drills. It's also the first panel in the Coach Workspace's Practice
section. Old links to it still work. For written debates that run over days
instead of a scheduled live round, see Online Challenges.

### What it shows [#practice-partners-what-it-shows]

- **Your practice profile.** This has two switches: **Open to challenges**
  ==lists you as a debater==, and **Volunteer to judge** ==lists you as a judge==.
  Under them you set formats (PF, LD, Policy, Parli, Worlds/BP, Congress),
  the styles you're comfortable with (traditional/lay, progressive/tech,
  plans/CPs/DAs, critiques, theory & T, philosophy/framework, truth-testing),
  speed, experience level, availability, and a note. ==Turning both switches
  off takes you out of matching and judging but keeps your preferences==.
  Next to them are two coaching switches: **Volunteer to coach** ==lists you
  as a coach offering online coaching==, and **Team that needs a coach**
  ==lists your team as looking for one==. Either one opens a **Coaching
  details** box (required, up to 1,000 characters) for the instructions the
  other side reads: what you offer or need, how sessions run, when you're
  free, and how to reach you.
- **Your challenges.** They're grouped as *Waiting on you* (challenges to
  answer, judging invitations), *Upcoming rounds* (soonest first), *Sent*,
  and a few closed ones. ==An accepted round shows its== **room code**. Debaters
  and the judge ==paste it into the round workspace's Cameras panel to join
  the same peer-to-peer webcam room==.
- **Rounds needing a judge.** ==This only appears for judge volunteers==. It
  lists accepted rounds with an empty judge seat, and ==picking one up
  notifies both debaters==.
- **Find a practice partner.** ==There is no public list of volunteers==.
  Pick a format (or any) and press **Find me a match**: ==the server scores
  everyone open to challenges against your profile== — ==shared formats count
  most, then shared styles, then speed and level== — and ==draws one of the five
  most compatible at random, weighted by score==. ==The match is shown
  anonymously==: level, speed, formats and styles (the ones you share
  highlighted), a compatibility percentage, and whether they also judge — ==no
  name, avatar, availability or note==. **Send request** opens a short form
  with the format (shared formats first), resolution, optional start time and
  a message. **Find another** draws again, ==skipping everyone already shown
  and anyone you already have a request waiting on==.

- **Online coaching**. ==Two lists, by name==: *Volunteer
  coaches* and *Teams that need a coach*, each with their formats, level,
  availability and coaching details. ==Every signed-in member sees it==, so a
  team can find a coach (and a coach a team) without signing up first. Unlike
  practice matching it isn't anonymous, because being found is the point of
  signing up. ==Guests can't sign up, and blocks hide people from each other==.

### How it works [#practice-partners-how-it-works]

A challenge is **pending** until the opponent accepts or declines it, or the
challenger cancels it. ==The challenger can cancel at any time before the
round==. ==The opponent can call it off once they've accepted==. The judge seat is
empty, **invited** (the challenger named a judge), or **confirmed** (the judge
said yes, or picked up an unjudged round). ==The judge can decline an invitation
or step down later==. If two judges pick up the same round at the same moment,
==the first one gets it and the second is told it's taken==.

==Every change sends a notification to everyone else in the round==, linking back
to Practice Partners.

**Anonymity.** ==Matching never shows you the other debater's name or account==,
and a match can only be used by you, for a couple of hours. ==Until a challenge
is accepted, each debater sees the other as "Your practice match"==, and the
notifications for sending, declining or calling off a pending request don't
name the sender. ==Accepting reveals both names==.

**Safety rules.** ==Matching requires an account==, and ==guest accounts can't
volunteer or send challenges==. ==A block from Contacts in either direction keeps
each person out of the other's matches==, stops them challenging each other, and
keeps the round off the other's "needing a judge" list. A blocked challenge gets
the same "not open to challenges" reply as a volunteer who has switched off, so
==the reply doesn't reveal the block==. Each debater can have ==at most ten
challenges waiting for an answer, and only one per opponent==. A closed challenge
stays in your list with its final status.

### Judge Pool [#practice-partners-judge-pool]

Below the practice board, the same page has a **Judge Pool** ==for
tournament judging, as opposed to practice rounds==.

- **Volunteer as a judge.** ==Pick up to three upcoming weekends from the
  circuit calendar's season weeks== (the same A–Y + Z weeks as the Debate
  Majors calendar). Each weekend row shows its week letter, dates and the
  majors running that Friday–Sunday. Add the states you're in (up to five),
  switch on **Able to travel** ==if you'll judge out of state==, pick the formats
  you judge, and write your judge philosophy. **List me for hire** off ==hides
  you without losing the listing==. ==Weekends drop off once they pass==; with
  none left you're out of the pool until you pick new ones.
- **Hire a judge.** ==Every listed judge, by name==, with their weekends,
  states, travel, formats and philosophy. Filter by weekend, tournament state
  and format. ==A judge fits a state if they're in it or will travel; in-state
  judges come first==. **Hire** ==sends a request: tournament name, one of the
  judge's weekends, a pay offer and a message==.
- **Hire requests.** ==The judge accepts or declines==; the organizer can cancel
  while it's waiting, and ==either side can call off an accepted one==. Each
  change sends a notification to the other side, linking to the Judge Pool.

**Payment isn't connected.** ==The pay offer is text==. ==Nothing is charged or
paid out on the site==; ==the two sides settle pay directly once a request is
accepted==. The site's Stripe setup only sells subscriptions through Payment
Links, ==which can't pay a judge==.

**Limits.** ==Guests can't list or hire, blocks hide people from each other==, an
organizer can have 20 requests waiting, and there's ==one open request per judge
per weekend==.

### Good to know [#practice-partners-good-to-know]

- ==There's no in-app messaging==. Beyond the challenge message, debaters arrange
  details elsewhere, for example through [Contacts](/docs/features/team-collaboration#contacts).
- ==A proposed time is a single suggestion==. There's no counter-proposal. To
  change it, ==decline and send a new challenge==.
- ==Nothing links a finished practice round back to its challenge==, so results
  and judge feedback aren't recorded here yet.
- ==Matching draws from the 200 most recently updated volunteers==.
- ==Compatibility is scored from the profile fields==; the free-text
  note and availability ==aren't read by the matcher== (and aren't shown, since
  they could identify someone).
- A pending request's judge seat can't be filled by invitation any more —
  the judge list isn't public — so ==judges pick up accepted rounds instead==.
- A request that is called off after being accepted ==shows the other debater
  as anonymous again==, since only an accepted challenge reveals names.
- ==The judge pool takes no payment==, and has no contract or confirmation beyond
  the accepted request.
- ==Judge weekends are limited to the 2026–27 circuit calendar== until next
  season's calendar is added.
- ==The pool lists the 200 most recently updated listings==.
- ==The coaching board has no request or messaging flow==: people reach each
  other however the coaching details say. It lists the 200 most recently
  updated coaching signups, with no filters yet.

## Claiming a School Page [#school-pages]

==Coaches and school administrators can claim their school's page==. ==Once a
moderator approves the claim, they manage the page==: ==which teams and students
are part of the school, and the school's logo==.

Claim a school from **Settings → General → My school**, and manage it from the
school's own page (**/@** followed by the school's name).

### Claiming a school [#school-pages-claiming-a-school]

1. Open **Settings → General → My school**.
2. ==Start typing your school's name and pick it from the list==. If it isn't
   there, tick **My school isn't listed — create it** and type the name.
3. Choose whether you're the school's **Coach** or **School administrator**.
4. ==Confirm the claim one of two ways==:
   - **Verify my school email** — enter the address your school gave you.
     ==We email a six-digit code==; enter it under **Your claims**. ==Codes expire
     after 15 minutes and allow five tries==; **Send a new code** issues another.
     ==Personal addresses (Gmail, Outlook, iCloud and the like) can't verify a
     school==.
   - **I'll explain instead** — ==tell the moderators how you're connected to
     the school== (at least 20 characters).
5. Submit. ==The claim then waits on a moderator==, and ==you get a notification
   when it's decided==, with any note the moderator left.

==You can withdraw a claim any time before it's approved==. ==You can't have two
open claims on the same school==.

### Reviewing claims (moderators) [#school-pages-reviewing-claims-moderators]

The **School claims** card at the bottom of the admin panel's **Users** section
(moderators see only this card there) ==lists every claim waiting on review,
oldest first==: the school, whether it's new, the claimant's name and account
email, the verified school address (if any), their explanation, and — when
the school already has owners — who they are. ==Approve or reject, optionally
with a note to the claimant==. ==Claims still waiting on an email code aren't
listed until the code is entered==.

Approving a claim on a school that isn't on any ranking list ==creates its
page==, so it shows the school with its logo and roster ==before
any ranking lists it==.

### Managing the school page [#school-pages-managing-the-school-page]

On the school's page, ==owners (and staff) see controls under the school's
name==:

- **Teams** and **Students** — ==add an entry by name== (students may have an
  optional email, shown only to the page's managers) and remove one with ×.
  ==A school's roster holds up to 300 entries==.
- **Logo** — **Set logo** ==uploads a PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF, scaled down to

## Team Calendar [#team-calendar]

==A month calendar for each group of researchers you coach==. ==Put research
tasks and assignments on a due date==, assign them to members of the group,
==watch who has finished==, and ==schedule virtual tournaments that only that
group enters==.

Open it from the **Team Calendar** tab of [Coaching](/docs/features/ai-coaching#coaching).

### Using it [#team-calendar-using-it]

1. **Pick a group.** Groups are your [Coaching Programs](/docs/features/ai-coaching#coaching-programs)
   rosters. ==You can also add one right on the calendar==: give it a name and a
   comma-separated list of researchers.
2. **Pick a day** on the month grid. Arrows step between months; ==dots under a
   day show what's on it== — blue for tasks, amber for assignments, violet for
   tournaments.
3. **Schedule** a task, assignment or tournament for that day. ==Choose who it is
   for== (or **Everyone**), an optional time, and notes.
4. **Track it.** The day's agenda is split into **Tasks**, **Assignments** and
   **Tournaments** tabs. ==Tick a researcher's name when they finish their part==;
   ==an entry everyone has finished shows== **Done**, one past its date that isn't
   shows **Overdue**.

### Group virtual tournaments [#team-calendar-group-virtual-tournaments]

==A tournament is a calendar entry whose entrants are the group's roster== (all
of it unless you pick some). Choose the format (PF, LD or Policy) and up to
eight rounds; ==the card shows round-robin pairings, with a bye when the
group has an odd number of entrants==. ==Nobody outside the group can be entered==.

### Upcoming deadlines and workload [#team-calendar-upcoming-deadlines-and-workload]

The right-hand column ==lists every unfinished task and assignment, earliest
first so overdue work leads==, and a table of each researcher's open, overdue
and finished items.

### Data and privacy [#team-calendar-data-and-privacy]

==Entries are saved in your browser and, when you are signed in, synced to
your account== like the other Coaching tools. An assignee must be on the
group's roster — ==the calendar refuses to assign work to anyone outside it==.

### Good to know [#team-calendar-good-to-know]

- ==Researchers are names on the roster, not linked accounts, so the calendar
  doesn't notify an assignee==; completion is ticked by whoever has the calendar open.
- ==Tournament pairings are generated for display only== — rounds aren't yet
  launched into the round workspace or scored on the group's standings.
- ==No recurring entries==, and no export to other calendar apps.

## Team Rankings [#team-rankings]

==A leaderboard of debate teams and debaters for every season from 2015-16 to
now==, for PF, LD, Policy (CX) and college policy (NDT). ==Ratings and TOC bids
come from live Elo ratings== compiled from Tabroom round results and openCaselist. ==For the current season, each
team's aff/neg win rates, round count and the field's side bias are merged in
from this site's own ballot-level Bradley-Terry fit== (see
[How Team Ratings Are Computed](/docs/features/ai-coaching#bradley-terry-debate-rankings));
past seasons show the Elo ratings alone. LD's Sep–Oct topic slice still
comes from the Bradley-Terry fit only.

- **Where:** **/coaching/rankings**

### What it shows [#team-rankings-what-it-shows]

Pick a division tab. ==Every column below can be sorted by clicking its
header==, and the ⓘ next to a header ==explains how the number is worked out==.

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Rank | Position by Elo rating |
| +/− | Places moved since the rankings were last saved (==positions are saved every 90 days==; hover the header for the date), or since last season's rankings until the first save: `+8` moved up eight places, `-4` dropped four, `0` held the same spot, and **new** means neither debater was ranked last season. Teams are matched by school and debaters (partner order doesn't matter); a team with a new partner is compared with the average of each debater's best rank last season, found by last name and school on any team, or the one debater's rank if only one is found. Shown from 2016-17 on, since rankings start in 2015-16 |
| Name / School | The team (`Last & Last`) or LD debater, and their school |
| Rating | The Elo rating on the site's 0–10.9 scale — what the rank is sorted on |
| Bids | High school divisions: Tournament of Champions bids earned that season, from the season's bid list. Hover the count to see the tournaments they came from. A dash means no bids |
| Matches | Current season only: decided rounds debated, aff plus neg. A dash in past seasons |
| Aff Win / Neg Win | Current season only: share of rounds won on each side, from the Bradley-Terry fit's ballots, matched to the Elo-rated team by debaters and school |
| Aff Elim Win / Neg Elim Win | Share of elimination rounds won on each side; a dash means no elims on that side |
| ID | A short stable id for the entry across tournaments |

Above the table: the number of ranked entries, ==the side bias across the
whole field== (two short lines such as "Neg Bias +1" and "Aff Elims Bias +3",
the side and how many points its win rate sits above 50%, with the exact rates
in the tooltip), and ==the list of
tournaments the ratings come from, with majors marked==. ==A filter box narrows
the table by name or school==.

#### TOC bids [#team-rankings-toc-bids]

==The Bids column on PF, LD and Policy comes from the TOC bid list for
the selected season==: each team's distinct bid tournaments, joined to its
rating by the exact team code. College policy has no TOC, so no Bids column.

If the Elo ratings can't be reached, the current season falls back to the
Bradley-Terry rankings alone, and the Policy tab's Bids column falls back to
==a community-maintained Google Sheet of this season's high school policy bids== (columns Bids, Team, Debaters,
State, Bid 1, Bid 2, …). The site ==keeps a copy for up to an hour==, so ==edits to the sheet show on
the rankings within the hour==. If Google can't be reached, ==a copy of the sheet
bundled with the site is shown instead==.

==Sheet rows are matched to ranked teams by their debaters' last names (in
either order) and school==, ==allowing for differently spelled school names==
("Barack Obama MLA" and "Barack Obama Male Leadership Academy"). A team listed
under two schools, such as an independent entry and its home school, ==gets
the bids from both rows==. ==Teams on the sheet that aren't in the rankings yet
are left out==.

#### Schools tab [#team-rankings-schools-tab]

The **Schools** tab ==rolls every ranked entry up by school== (All events, or one
division). ==Schools are ranked by their== **Balanced** score (below).

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| +/− | Places the school moved since the 90-day saved rankings (or last season, before the first save) in the same view (All events, or that division): `+8` up, `-4` down, **new** if it wasn't ranked last season |
| Best Rating | Highest adjusted rating among the school's entries |
| Top Entry | The entry holding that rating, and its event |
| Avg Rating | Mean adjusted rating across every ranked team (or LD debater) from the school |
| Balanced | What the rank is sorted on: 1.1 × (0.7 × the average of the school's top three entries + 0.3 × (½ Avg Rating + ½ depth)), capped at 10.9. Depth grows with the number of teams and reaches 10 at 15. Schools under four teams are diluted (×0.80 for one team, ×0.87 for two, ×0.93 for three) |
| Teams | Number of ranked entries |
| Events | Events the school has entries in |

When any listed school has attended the National Debate Tournament (College NDT, or All events), an **NDT** column adds its record, in this order: ==NDT titles, CEDA national titles and NDT elim qualifiers== (a title count of zero is left out), for example `1 NDT title · 12 CEDA titles · 61 elims`. It reads the same NDT school history as the school page.

The current season's school table is ==rebuilt every day==, and ==your
browser keeps a copy== so the tab opens at once.

Ranking on **Balanced** ==is led by a school's best three entries==, with
==average quality and depth as the rest==. ==A school with one or two strong
entries is diluted==, ==so it doesn't top the table over a deep program whose top
three are just as strong==.

At the top of the Schools tab's College tab, ==the **Top 10** chart==
follows the ten schools with the most all-seasons NDT points over the last
decade (the last 10 seasons). Each school is one line showing ==where it ranked
among every NDT school that season==, by its points over the last four NDTs
(one college career), so you can see who rose and fell. Filled points are NDT
titles. ==Hover a line or a
school's chip== to fade the other nine (click a chip to keep it highlighted),
and ==hover or tap a season's point== to see what the school did at that NDT:
titles, elim finishes, the Copeland Award, first-round bids and top-20 speakers.
Each chip shows the school's rank and logo (hover it for the full name); a
school with no logo, or with school logos turned off in Settings, shows its
short name instead.

The **Policy**, **LD** and **PF** tabs each draw ==their own **Top 10**== the
same way: the ten high schools with the most all-time program points in that
event, charted over the last decade, each season ranked among every program in
the event by their points over
the last four seasons (one high school career). Points are Tournament of
Champions finishes (back to 1977 in Policy, 1986 in LD and 2004 in PF; champion
30, runner-up 18, top speaker 10) plus, from 2015-16 on, each season's two best

Under the school table, ==the **All seasons: NDT points** ranking== (every NDT

#### Legendary and gold rows [#team-rankings-legendary-and-gold-rows]

In every division table and on the Schools tab, ==a small group at the top is==

#### Past-season team pages [#team-rankings-past-season-team-pages]

for the school and the team's initials==. There is no radar, simulator or
Follow button, since those need current-season ratings.

The address is ==the school's short name plus the debaters' last names==
(`/@kansas-katz-robinson`, `/@wake-forest-louis-kingston`). Every source
reduces to the same address, so ==an NDT entry, its CEDA title and its
debatedata seasons share one page==. Older links still open the page and

(`/@university-of-kansas-will-katz-quaram-robinson`) and the school plus

A school page for a college that has attended the National Debate Tournament
also has a **College NDT record** section: ==NDT championships, elim
qualifiers== (round, debaters, prelim record and seed), first-round at-large

school by name== (`Harvard University (MA)` is the rankings' `Harvard`). When a
college and a university share a name, ==a plain name means the university==
(`Georgetown` is Georgetown University (DC), not Georgetown College (KY)) and

system's campuses answer to the circuit's short forms (`UC Berkeley`, `Cal`,
`UT Dallas`, `UMKC`), and ==the bare system name means the main campus==:
`Texas` is UT Austin, never UT Arlington. ==A school the
NDT has no record of shows no section==.

==A **Shirley Classic finals** card follows== on college school pages that
reached a final of Wake Forest's Franklin R. Shirley Classic: each year is
marked **Shirley champion** or **Shirley second place** with the team, newest
first. It comes from [Wake Forest's historical winners list](https://debate.wfu.edu/history/historical-lists/shirley-debate-tournament-winners/)
(1956–2022), ==with the years that list skips (2007, 2008 and 2023 onward)
filled from [College Debate Results](https://collegedebateresults.vercel.app)'
elimination brackets, which give surnames only==. 1994 has no result: the
tournament moved to February. 1996's two editions, the 2001 tie and 2011's
co-champions are kept as listed. ==The card and the championship and
finalist totals only include Shirley finals from 2000 on==; every Shirley
final, older ones included, ==still counts toward program strength (below)==. The data lives in
`packages/debate-rankings/src/sync/shirley-results.ts`.

==A high school page's tiles include **Academics rank · Niche**==: the school's
place on [Niche's 2027 Best High Schools in America](https://www.niche.com/k12/search/best-high-schools/)
list (public and private combined, top 200), the only academics ranking the site
uses; a school outside that list shows "—". The list lives in
`packages/debate-rankings/src/schools/niche-academics.ts`.

The summary tiles sit ==at the top of the school page==, beside the mascot and under the ranking tiles, while the lists stay in the section. The summary leads with **==Championships==**, **Finalists** and **Top speakers**: the school's
NDT, CEDA national and Shirley titles added into one total, its second-place finishes (NDT
finals losses and Shirley second places; CEDA lists champions only) and its NDT top speaker
awards, each with the split (`3 NDT · 12 CEDA · 1 Shirley`) underneath. ==It then shows the last
decade of NDTs first== (the ten years ending with the latest NDT in the history,
2016–2025 today): **elim qualifiers** in that span and the **==elim finish
rank==**; ==then the same two across every year==. ==Each elim entry is
scored by the round it reached==, Doubles 1, Octas 2, Quarters 3, Semis 4,
Finals 5 and Champion 6, and ==the school's mean score is ranked against every
college's mean for the same span==, deepest first. ==The tile shows only the
rank, never a round name==: Wake Forest's all-years tile reads **#4** with
`of 61 schools · 76 elims` underneath. ==Every team in elims counts, so a year
with two teams there counts twice==. ==Only schools with at least 3 elims in the
span are ranked==, so one lucky run can't top the table; a school with fewer
shows "—" and "unranked", and a school with no elims in the span shows "—" and
"no elims".
==The list icon in each elim finish rank tile's corner opens the full ranking==,
like the program strength tiles: every ranked school with its elim count, a tab
for the last decade and one for all years, a school filter, and the school
whose page it is highlighted.

Beside the section's title, **All seasons** opens a ==ranking of every NDT
team and school since 1947 by all-seasons NDT points==. A team is one pair of
debaters at one school across every season they debated together (Georgetown's
Arsht & Markoff, Northwestern's Miles & Vellayappan), so ==a debater with two
partners has two partnerships== (Harvard's Herman & Sanjeev and Midha &
Sanjeev), the lesser one shown under the better one (see below). Each
row shows its total, ==its breakdown by elims, Copeland, bids and speakers==
with the counts behind them (`Elims 53.5 (3 elims, 2 titles)`), and the seasons
it scored in. ==Every school name carries its logo== (unless Settings →
Preferences has school logos off), on the school rows, beside each team's
school and on the summary line at the top. Teams link to their past-team pages
and schools to their school pages; ==the school whose page you opened is highlighted==, its school rank
and best team are shown at the top, and a filter box and an "Only this school"
box narrow the list. ==The list scrolls all the way to the last row==, loading
more as you near the end, with no "Show more" button. The points start from the program strength chart's
weights below, with ==the Copeland Award counting 1.5×== in this table:

| NDT result | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Each NDT a team broke at, by the deepest round reached | Doubles 3, Octas 5, Quarters 8, Semis 12, Finals 18, Champion 30 |
| Copeland Award (the season's top first-round at-large seed) | 18 (12 × 1.5) |
| Each first-round at-large bid | 4 |
| Each NDT speaker award, credited to the speaker's team | top speaker 10, 2nd–5th 5, 6th–10th 3, 11th–20th 1 |
| This season, before its NDT: each team in the live college rankings' top 100 | (101 − rank) ÷ 20: #1 is 5, #16 is 4.25, #100 is 0.05 |

==A title is Champion, not Finals plus a bonus==, and ==the Copeland winner also
holds a first-round bid, so it scores both== (22 for the season before the NDT).
A school's total adds every team it sent, ==plus speaker awards that can't be
matched to exactly one of its listed teams that year== (a speaker whose team
didn't break or get a bid). ==CEDA titles and past seasons' top-100 ranks
don't count here==; they are in the program strength chart.

==The season under way counts too==, before its NDT is held: each team in the
College rankings' current top 100 adds the points above to its row and its
school (shown as **This season** in the breakdown, with the team's rank), so
this season's best teams appear even before they reach the NDT. Once that
season's NDT results are added, they replace its rankings.

==Results from seasons before 2000 count 0.6× (40% less)==, season by season:
a 1977 title is worth 18 points instead of 30, and a team that debated on both
sides of 2000 has only its earlier results reduced. This applies to NDT
results, speaker awards and high school results alike. The modal prints the
same rules under **How the points work**.

==A team also gets a quarter of the points each of its debaters scored with
other partners==, and those side partnerships are listed under it with the
points they add. Northwestern's Miles & Vellayappan add a quarter of what
Vellayappan scored with Peyton Lee, and Lee & Vellayappan is listed under them. ==A partnership gets its own row only when it is both debaters' best==
(by its own points), so each debater's lesser partnerships appear under their
best team instead of as separate entries. School totals don't add this
partner credit, so no result counts twice for a school.

==Each debater's high school record counts too, at half weight==: their
Tournament of Champions policy results with any partner, from the TOC list
the school pages use (1972 on), and ==their NDCA national championship policy
results== (the National Debate Coaches Association tournament, 2006 on). A TOC
title is 5 points (10 halved, a sixth of an NDT title), runner-up 3 and top
speaker 2; an NDCA title is also 5 and NDCA top speaker 2. ==NDCA elimination
finishes count as well==, from the full 2006 and 2009 brackets, the 2011 and 2012
finals and the 2015–2018 finals and semifinals (a team the source names only by
code, like La Salle GH, shows on its school page but credits no debater): a finalist is 3 (like a TOC runner-up), a semifinalist 2, a
quarterfinalist 1.25, an octafinalist 0.75 and a double-octafinalist 0.5 (2009
broke 17 teams, so one team lost in double-octas). 2007 and 2008 list only elim
seeds, which aren't finishes, so ==seeds don't count==. ==NDCA speaker places for

==A team's score is elims + 1.5 × Copeland + bids + speakers + 0.25 × other
partners + 0.5 × high school==, with pre-2000 results then times 0.6: it ranks what a partnership did together first,
gives extra credit for the season-long best team, and treats results with other
partners and in high school as supplementary. Georgetown's Arsht & Markoff

Fisher & Spies (112.75), then Emory's Inamullah & Weil and Michigan's Pierry &

### Following a team or school [#team-rankings-following-a-team-or-school]

### Matchup simulator [#team-rankings-matchup-simulator]

==Pair any two ranked teams in a division and see who the ratings favour==. The
simulator reads each division's full-season rankings, so ==every ranked team
can be picked== — filter
by name or school, or hit **Random opponent**.

==Each side is simulated separately==: team A locked **aff**, then team A locked
**neg**, ==so you can see which side a team does better on==. Pick **Aff**,
**Neg** or **Both**, and **Prelim** (one judge), **Elim** (3- and 5-judge
panels) or **Both**. With both sides shown, ==it says which side team A should
take on a coin flip==, and by how many points. **Simulate 1,000 rounds per
side** ==plays each side out to show the spread a percentage hides==.

#### What goes into the rating [#team-rankings-what-goes-into-the-rating]

==Each team's rating is a blend of three signals==, set by two sliders:

- **Team**: ==the pairing's own rating==.
- **Individual performance**: ==how each debater rates in their *other*
  entries at the same school==, in any division (other partners, or LD). A
  pair that only does well together, or a debater who carries every partner,
  ==shows up here==. Teams whose debaters have no other entries ==just use the
  team rating==.
- **School performance**: ==the average rating of every entry the school has
  in the division==. This is ==program strength (coaching, shared files) rather
  than the pair itself==.

Under each team the simulator shows all three numbers, the blend, and a
**recommended** mix ==for that team==. ==A team with few rounds of its own leans
more on its school== (15 rounds → 50%, 60 → 20%). A team whose debaters have
other entries ==leans more on those, up to half==. ==Click it to apply it==.

==The side effect has two parts==: the field's aff/neg split for the stage (elims
often lean further than prelims), and each team's own aff-vs-neg record
beyond the field's, ==shrunk toward zero for teams with few rounds==. A team's
elim-only rates come from a handful of rounds, so ==its tilt always comes from
its overall record==.

==The base probability uses the standard Glicko-2 formula==, from the two
ratings and how certain each one is, ==and the side effect is added on top==. ==Panel odds assume each ballot is independent at that probability==.

#### Your team, pre-filled [#team-rankings-your-team-pre-filled]

**Settings → General → My team** ==sets your role== (debater, coach, or assistant/judge),
your partner, and ==the ranked team you represent in each division==. It's saved
in this browser and, when you're signed in, to your account. ==The simulator starts from it==.

It appears in two places:

- **Practice round**: ==side A starts as your team for the
  division==, and both sides can be changed.
- **Every team profile**: if you have a team set for that
  division, **Simulate vs. any ranked team** ==pits your team (side A) against
  the profile's team==. On your own profile, or with no team set, ==the profile's
  team is locked in as side A== and any other ranked team can be its opponent.

### Good to know [#team-rankings-good-to-know]

The **All seasons** high school credit only knows TOC champions, runners-up
and top speakers and NDCA champions and top speakers, so other high school
results (elims, NDCA runners-up, bids) don't count: the NDCA's history page
lists no runners-up or elim results for most years. Two debaters with the same
full name would share high school results.

The **Top 10** chart ranks a season by four NDTs of points, so ==a school's
line lags a season or two behind a breakthrough or a slump==, and every rank
past 24th is drawn on the bottom line.

The **All seasons** NDT ranking ==starts first-round bids and the Copeland Award in
1973==, when they began, so earlier teams can only score elims and speakers. A
pair is matched by last names at one school, so ==two partnerships there with
the same last names are merged==, and a speaker award goes to a team only when
the speaker's full name matches one listed team that year (about 9% of awards
count for the school only). The NDT history stops at the 2025 NDT, so the
2025-26 season (the 2026 NDT) isn't counted yet, and the season under way
counts only through its live rankings. A current team listed by last names only
is matched to a debater from the same school's last five NDTs with that last
name, so a newcomer who shares one could be matched to the wrong person.

The Schools tab can be ==up to a day behind== the division tables for the
current season: it shows the copy saved by the last daily rebuild, while a
division table loads the live ratings.

The matchup simulator's individual rating ==finds a debater's other entries by
surname and school==, so ==two debaters with the same surname at one school are
merged==, and ==someone who changed schools isn't linked==. ==The rankings carry no
speaker points==, so individual skill is only inferred from other pairings.
The partner name in **My team** ==is informational and doesn't change the
model==.

A ranked team's handle uses last names only, so ==two partnerships at one
school with the same last names share a page==, and an old initials link that
fits several pairs at one school ==shows them all on one page== without
switching the address.

An old school-plus-initials link ==matches by school and initials==, so two
pairs from one school with the same initials in different seasons ==show on
one page== there (the short link keeps them apart). Two partnerships at one
school with the same last names would share a short link. debatedata's
tables only go back to 2009-10 and list the top 100, so an older or lower
team shows only its NDT or CEDA results, and a team in none of the sources
has no page. Its Videos section ==searches by initials== and so can show
another pair's rounds.

A profile's Videos section ==only finds a round whose team label or title spells
the school the way the rankings do==, or by one of the short forms above. ==A
round recorded under an unlisted abbreviation is missed==, and a school whose
short name another school shares (two *Memorial* high schools) ==can show the
other's rounds==.

Followed teams' results and recaps ==come from the round video library==, so ==a
tournament's record counts only the rounds someone recorded==, and ==a round is
dated by its upload, not the day it was debated==. The rankings keep no
history, so ==a recap's rating change is an estimate== from each decided round's
result and both teams' current ratings. ==Nothing is emailed==; the recap
only appears in the News Stream.

==Ratings cover only the tournaments listed above the table==; ==a team that hasn't
attended one of them is not ranked==. ==The list updates when the rankings are
recomputed, not live==.
