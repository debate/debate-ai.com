**Abstract.** In competitive debate, ==critiques of systemic mindsets are misapplied to preclude debating a supposed outcome. This paper corrects how these quotes should have been applied== from the start. Critics quote philosophical positions in false juxtaposition to create a false dichotomy between supporting a supposed outcome and a scholarship credibility reduction. ==A critique== that identifies broader oppressive structures ==cannot demand that the affirmative ‘rethink first’ unless it offers a feasible alternative== that readers facing the harm could actually use. First, ==all quoted evidence== — including critical theory — ==functions as a lens, not a gateway==; each debater decides what to take from it. Second, a 'the system is doomed' claim does not warrant rejecting concrete action; ==risk of systemic failure is not a certainty of it==. Finally, ==critical reflection and policy testing proceed together==, because acting on a proposal is how its assumptions get verified. ---

## Outline: Sections & Paragraph Extensions

**==This paper argues that critical theory is misused in competitive debate when critiques act as gateways==** that stop judges from evaluating the affirmative. Part I sets a concerned-reader standard for judging. Part II catalogs the logic fallacies behind critique frameworks. Part III applies those fallacies to the major critiques: anti-system, capitalism, security, death rhetoric, Heidegger, biopolitics, discourse, Nietzsche, and settler colonialism. **Part IV proposes ==pluralism, the permutation and agent-specific alternatives==**, and a supplementary addendum parodies critique logic through hypotheticals. **Each section page opens with a summary of its paragraphs**, to be used as extensions of those answers.
