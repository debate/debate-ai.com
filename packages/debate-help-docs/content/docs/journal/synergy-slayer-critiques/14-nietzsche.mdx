1. **Extinction outweighs:** Connolly shows **survival is the precondition for any overcoming**, White shows no life can be judged worthless before it ends, and Szacki shows value to life is no policy standard, because government cannot impose one view of the good life without treating citizens unequally.
2. **Suffering has only extrinsic value:** Hales reads Nietzsche as a consequentialist who **puts life at the center**, and Leiter shows a morality that reduces suffering can still exempt those who choose it. Hatab and Camus show that **rejecting plan as life-denying is itself ressentiment** that sacrifices living people for a utopia where people reevaluate their values according to the critique.
3. **Perspectivism favors the permutation:** Hatab shows evaluations of life are **local pleas rather than refutations**, Magnus shows leftovers of an old tradition can be reused for self-overcoming, and Dillon and Campbell show **affirming life requires active resistance**, not quietism.
4. **Critique is not relativism:** Engle shows Nietzsche was no nihilist and that **relativism leads to fascism and market rule**, White and Nietzsche himself show ethics becomes more urgent without God, May shows **political action celebrates life**, and Hatab (2002) shows creativity needs institutions.
5. **Withdrawal is not neutral:** Klar, Wade and Carreira show that **modest reform makes many people happier**, that cooperation evolved rather than being invented by the weak, and that **life keeps improving**, so the negative’s pessimism fails.

1. ==Preventing extinction outweighs as a precondition for every form of overcoming==. Connolly (1991) argues nuclear weapons change the terms of Nietzsche's project: "==the failure to 'preserve man' could also extinguish the human basis for the struggle Nietzsche named 'overman.' Preservation and overcoming are now drawn closer together==.” Nietzsche never weighed his critique against a species-ending war, and ==no one can affirm suffering after they are dead==. Nor can any life be judged worthless in advance, since "==no final evaluation of a life can be made until, at least, the life is over==” (White 1990). Extinction makes that premature judgment for everyone. ==Value to life also gives policymakers no workable standard==. Szacki (1995) argues that resolving the dispute among conceptions of life "==is attainable only by a totalitarian enslavement of society==," so "the government does not treat them as equals if it prefers one conception to another." ==Extinction ends every citizen's ability to decide what makes life worth living==; the alternative imposes one answer on all of them. “==Nietzsche also indicates that overall evaluations of life cannot be given any veridical status==, since they stem from perspectival interests \[…\] An inability to affirm everyday conditions may be no less problematical than traditional refusals to affirm existential finitude," and Nietzsche "may have lost sight of his own warnings against teleological metanarratives," given that "after all is said and done, ==we should let people live the life they want to live==” (Hatab 1995). Smolkin (1989) explains how Camus faulted Nietzsche's super-humanity, like Marx's classless society, while sacrificing those in the present. Camus called instead for rebels to "==work for justice and for a decrease in suffering==," so "each individual has the leisure and freedom to pursue his own search for meaning." ==Preventing death makes self-creation possible for everyone==. Nietzsche's critique of slave morality does not mean preventing suffering is bad. ==Withdrawing from politics because suffering is inevitable is not neutral==. Critics conflate personal happiness with ==political== unhappiness. “These three studies provide some support for the hypothesis that engaging ==in== political activism is associated with higher levels of well-being” (Klar & Kasser 2009). ==Natural selection== creates survival morality: “morality grew out of behavioral rules shaped by evolution, it is for biologists, not philosophers or theologians, to say what these rules are \[…\] Roots of morality can be seen in the social behavior of monkeys \[…\] Social living requires empathy \[…\] that takes the interests of the entire community” (Wade 2007). The alternative's pessimism is also wrong as Pinker (2014) reports that ==armed conflict is declining==, and suffering has decreased from disease to poverty. Centuries of slave morality have not produced life-denial, and artists can still suffer.

2. ==Debate is like a dance that we do to affirm what we value==, but their cynicism would always win as it is self-justifying asceticism. ==A critique that rejects the plan as life-denying finds it wanting, which is judgmental ressentiment== of the research that gives our lives value: “==Might the critics be guilty of a kind of resentment==? \[…\] The only way to salvage Nietzsche philosophically is to privatize his transformational rhetoric. \[…\] ==Political practice, however, should be left free==, and this includes citizens who are not 'Nietzschean' enough, who might be 'resentful ,' or 'bourgeois,' or 'metaphysical.' Otherwise we would seem forced to conclude that such citizens are not practicing politics in the 'right' way. As long as democratic matter—otherwise we are left with a choice between coercive reform and an insincere tolerance of the wrong kind of politics” (Hatab 1995). ==The affirmative does not reject the negative's views; it affirms its own among others==. For Nietzsche, evaluations of life “==are local estimations that serve the interests of a certain perspective but that cannot stand as a global measure to cancel out other estimations==” and thus critique "cannot amount to a project of refutation or erasure, but rather a 'plea by an interested party'" (Hatab 2002). Even perspectives the negative calls life-denying are forms of willing: "all forms of human life seek to will their meaning." ==That makes the permutation the most Nietzschean option==. Any issues remaining are material for self-overcoming, not something to discard: tradition's “==debris need not be rejected, but may rather be used in the reconstruction of the new self==. \[…\] For Nietzsche, certain features of a tradition previously regarded as repressive may in fact be reappropriated” (Magnus 1996). “I see my life as mine to dance, then ==I might well resist those who want to force me to take only the steps they prescribe. \[…\] There is no passivity or quietism entailed in this affirmation. We can act. We do act== \[…\] Nietzsche clearly indicates that ==for life to be affirmed resistance to despotism is vital==” (Dillon and Campbell 1993). Prefer field experts over debate high-speed spin of ancient aphorisms or it hijacks debate into ideological extremism sold via ethics quotes.

3. ==The critique is already incorporated into society as personal self-help, not to block public policy. Critics repugnantly contrive Nietzsche to oppose any policy solving war== and justify atrocity using vague jargon sophistry. Hales (1995) reads Nietzsche as a consequentialist whose quarrel with utilitarianism is about hedonism, not consequences: "While Nietzsche employs the language of consequentialism, he rejects the Benthamite summum bonum as a mistake." He concludes that "==if anything has intrinsic value, then the only thing that does so is life==." Leiter (2004) adds that for Nietzsche "==the value of suffering \[…\] is only extrinsic==," so a morality to avoid extinction and to reduce suffering "could perfectly well allow an exception for those individuals whose own suffering is essential to the realization of central life projects." ==Reducing suffering of those who never chose it takes nothing away from us. Nietzsche's critique is not relativism and gives no license to stop judging harms==. Engle (2007) argues that "==Nietzsche is no nihilist; in fact, he was passionate about truth==," and that relativism "risks degeneration into ==fascism--if truth is unknowable and moral values relative, then only force exists." Dropping moral standards hands value to the market==: it "elevates market values as the only objective scientific value" and reinforces "the hierarchy of the rich as more valuable than the poor." White (1990) shows the death of God makes ethics more urgent: universal permissibility "==makes such reflection the more pressing==." Nietzsche himself agrees that "==many actions called immoral ought to be avoided and resisted== \[…\] but ==for other reasons than hitherto==." May (2005) argues ==debating policy change is itself life-affirming. “There can be no such thing as a sad revolutionary==. To seek to change the world is to offer a new form of life-celebration, \[…\] ==To refuse to experiment is to resign oneself to the intolerable==. \[…\] The only question is whether you will take up the world that you are of, or leave it to others, to those others who would be more than willing to take your world up for you.” ==Nietzsche== supports ==state== action to give space for self-creation: “It is a mistake, however, to read Nietzsche in simple terms as being against institutions and the rule of law on behalf of self-creation. \[…\] Nietzschean creative freedom is selective and most people should be ruled by normative orders, because universal unrestricted freedom would cause havoc \[…\] Accordingly, creativity is a kind of ‘dancing in chains’ \[…\] Creative freedom, then, ==is not an abandonment of constraint, but a disruption of structure that still needs structure== to prepare and execute departures from the norm” (Hatab 2002). Debating how to stop war and persuade the public is the "dancing in chains" that enables space to affirm life.

4. To answer yet another post-truth critique: Deleuze ignores ==concrete institutions of exploitation== and fails public persuasion: “Deleuze offers few resources for thinking the consequences of what happens within the actually existing world \[…\] By posing the question of politics in the starkly dualistic terms of war machine or state \[…\] ==Deleuze's philosophy amounts to little more than utopian distraction==. Although no small number of enthusiasts continue to devote much energy and ingenuity to the task, the truth is that Deleuze's work is essentially indifferent to the politics of this world. A philosophy based on deterritorialisation, dissipation and flight can offer only the most immaterial and evanescent grip on the mechanisms of exploitation. \[…\] In places like Palestine, Haiti and Iraq, ==the agents of imperialism have more to learn from Deleuzian rhizomatics than do their opponents. \[…\] It disables action in favour of contemplation==. \[…\] because what feeling 'preserves is always in a state of detachment in relation to action.’ \[…\] But those of us who still seek to change our world and to empower its inhabitants will need to look for our inspiration elsewhere” (Hallward 2006, 162-164). ==Framing politics as a choice between the state and a radically different world substitutes utopian speculation for practical political strategy==. This orientation privileges contemplation over effective action, weakening opponents of imperialism while leaving imperial powers free to exploit the same decentralized, flexible methods.
