The plan is no intrinsic cost to a Marxist, privileged teams who scout judges turn critique into **ideological bribery**, and Linklater concedes that **emancipation needs a stable international order**, so doing both works better.
3. **Žižek votes affirmative:** Žižek calls for **stronger collective control of markets** and global cooperation for collective survival, and he warns that **fear of co-option drives purity and marginalization**. Debate's first resolution in 1947 already addressed labor's share in managing industry, so we are already living in the alternative, and critics could swap in any other \-ism to avoid specifics.
4. **Conflating "capitalism":** Critics **blur the plan's use of money with personal values**, letting the judge have their cake and eat it too. Edwards notes that critiques confuse PhD candidates, Wilson says only capitalism sustains its own critics, and Marxists do not blame workers. Capitalism cannot simply be "rejected," so **the negative must show a different opportunity cost** or the alternative world defaults to the plan, since two affirmatives destroy debate.
5. **Purity becomes terror:** Turning a visionary goal into a reason not to act now misteaches students. Levitz says dismissing reforms for not abolishing capitalism only signals superiority, and **total rejection invites inaction or symbolic terror**, like Stalin treating citizens as lab rats.
