1. Influential anticapitalist activist Albert (1998) argues we must move beyond awareness of \-isms to reject and instead build hope through reforms: “In the absence of insight into future possibilities, ==growing awareness of the scale of oppressions== and their tenacity ==breeds cynicism and not resistance==. \[…\] Oglesby \[…\] named imperialism, not just bad policy, as the real culprit in Vietnam. \[…\] ==It is as if to celebrate progress, or to even admit progress, is to somehow negate our critiques. \[…\] It won’t do to say that everyone will be better after the revolution. People need better conditions now==, on moral grounds and also to have any faith in future prospects. \[…\] **==Non reformist reforms==,** he said, are gains in the way people live, in laws, in structures, in consciousness, in our own organization, **which ==improve peoples lives but also create a new platform==** ==from which to fight== for still further. \[…\] ==People need to deeply believe that you can fight city hall and== win. \[…\] The left is largely still trying to convince the public that all is not well with society and **that the cause of problems lies in ‘the system’ and its institutions.** Yet, by and large **==the public knows this already==**. \[…\] But to attract and retain people we also have to ask do our projects, movements, and organizations, and ==does our language== and our personal style lend itself to new people, ==welcome them==, respect them, comfort them, fulfill ==them—or does it shock them==, denigrate them, literally **block them from coming toward us** or sticking with us?” That is this author’s favorite quote, and political synergy forbids fracturing ==support for policies based on whose \-ism is truer: “A better strategy is to promote agonistic \[…\] **coalitions on specific political issues**. \[…\] The specter haunting the Left today is an unattractive set of internal struggles for hegemony== in which each party calls the other names that **give ammunition to the cultural Right**. \[…\] **Synergy on the Left**, then, may reside in \[…\] ==letting go of the fantasy of defining a hegemonic position== to which all true leftists must subscribe. ==The need today is==, rather, ==construction of a series of== **==alliances across multiple lines of difference== in assumption,** priority, ethical source, and modes of politics. As we establish relations of agonistic respect across differences, we can also hope to strengthen our hand in the academy and improve communications with sympathetic parties” (Connolly 1999).

2. Even if mindsets are opposing in theory, ==in practice that means synergizing a greater whole== then if those parts stood alone to unite society better by adding a third element to resolve the tension of that exclusivity, which is the starting point for their movement. ==Pluralist society allows defending many justifications for advocacies, even if self-contradictory: "**Antiwar generals**, corporation executives fostering workers' control and top politicians promoting local self-reliance play a useful role== in efforts for social change, especially to the extent that they work with social movements" (Martin 1990). “==With very rare exceptions, the right things are done for the wrong reasons==. It is futile to demand that" our leaders or activists "do the right thing for the right reason"; instead, "the organizer should know and accept that the right reason is only introduced as a moral rationalization after the right end has been achieved" and in order to persuade state opposition one "==should search for and use the wrong reasons to achieve the right goals==" (Alinsky 1971, 66). “Collaboration not only allows policy actors to achieve more than they would if they were acting alone; it also gives them more credibility in the eyes of their allies, their opponents, the public” (Wagner et al. 2023). Legislation passes when a party builds coalitions across mindsets using two-faced contradictions (Malhotra 2015). The case is trying to convince one-at-a-time that society should change in some way, but that same literature field often advocates changing personal values. We retain the ability to support other solutions to other root causes, without extending those at the end for the ballot.

3. The permutation is **prior consultation with critique authors on a commission in Congress to ensure we persuade the public via their mindset to support the plan**, as Mintz-Woo (2026) explains: “In a commission context, a group of experts is convened by the government to individually and jointly evaluate some portfolio of policies \[…\] **There are many instances of philosophers** reflecting on their service on such commissions. \[…\] The points raised here apply to other contexts where philosophers engage with policy. \[…\] It is pragmatically useful in a commission context to be able to **draw on diverse values, so advocating a policy option with high convergence** potentially allows more people to support it. \[…\] The goal is not to find out what that particular expert has found or believes but to understand what the biodiversity community believes about circumstances like this one. \[…\] An egalitarian can check a sufficientarian's reasoning along the lines of ‘Yes, **supposing that** \[…\]**ism is true, it does (or does not) follow that this given policy option is justifiable.’ This is possible even if the egalitarian does not accept the sufficientarian** pattern of justice**.** \[…\] A policy option that is subject to convergent evaluation can be accepted as more legitimate given that **it can be supported from a variety of philosophical positions.**” This game-changing permutation as one discussion brings in contrasting mindsets so they can synergize and teach each other when to apply theories to build support for a specific plan. This is precisely why some formats like the American Debate Association allow critiques of values to be read, but they still have to uniquely answer if “this given policy option is justifiable,” not just imagine mindsets shifts (Zompetti & Lain 2005). Negatives must read counterplans that overcome constructed threats, not just to project everyone in society rejects it. For example, Israel could agree that all buildings should have bomb shelters and also that discourse on terrorism should not construct threats. Bringing in contrasting views is the very point of synergy. The permutation asks if a reader who thinks as the critique could still support the plan, viewed from society’s interest? If so, the critique expands readers, and fails to negate. ==Think of the case as an article for== concerned readers ==within the critique's journal==. The test is simply this: starting from that movement, would we still be able to have the affirmative's proposal as one of a hundred issues in applying theory? ==The affirmative is already one compartmentalized debate== within the broader starting point of the debate movement that assumes many philosophies with research sharing. ==We can only find use in a== theory if relevant in a context.

4. ==The very purpose of debate topics has been to regulate society’s systemic flaws==. Rejecting debate to use as a starting point examination of ontology, biopolitics, security, or capitalism is like coming to a global forum on how nations can get Iran to denuclearize, and then advocating ==the Iranian state denuclearize, or== that ==society should reject militarism to win every debate. Negative fiat is limited to what is persuasive to the intended audience==, and forecloses that **which would always defeat the discussion,** under the Bengtsson (2024) standard. Appeals to “the case issues can always be better if we rejected the flaws of capitalist society” or to “rethink healthcare so that biopolitical control doesn’t go wrong” ==would always win and thus preclude the discussion actually needed== to overcome public opposition. ==The permutation justifies the plan as a concrete proposal== that ==the critique's movement could adopt==, and as material to refine the policies any post-transformation order would still have to decide. ==Even if our authors use problematic discourse== or appeal for help from inherently flawed systems, ==that is only because that is all that currently exists==. They are not assuming immediate implementation, but just generally advocate for the policy's intrinsic merits in any society's presumed mindset. ==Rejecting the system does not mean rejecting any action advocated to the system==, rather it means to reject the status quo system by researching detailed solutions to a broader goal. ==Critics cannot tell us to rethink if there's any better solutions in their literature; it was their research burden==. Despite the critic's vagueness, ==the alternative is== equivalent to the fiated plan as an ==a advocacy of== a supposed vision of change: usually scholars calling on the public and leaders dealing with this issue to base decisions on an alternative mindset. ==Their critique is already being read and applied in society==: state leaders, scholars, media analysts, and diplomats collaborate to check assumptions of other mindsets. For example on security rhetoric, ==scholars would not want to be used to imply that case impacts are accounted for== as constructed threats so as to ignore statistics on escalating casualties in a region. ==Leaders conducting accurate threat assessment would look to detailed facts== and already know from history clichés like to avoid demonizing another as an securitized enemy.

One can appeal to those who think immigration can help or hurt jobs, support or oppose free trade, or those who use race, class, or gender as starting points. ==One can agree on capitalism’s flaws== and inequalities, ==but still find some value in innovation==. So ==pretending to put first the debate over if capitalism has been used more for good or for bad is a meaningless oversimplification==, and can lead to repugnant comparisons like if free trade innovation outweighs advertising and alienation. Either way, readers would not generalize a specific policy discussion through that lens any more than driving a car through the lens of liberal vs conservative to ignore the specifics in front. We must presume the reader would look to the specifics in a situation, because there will inevitably be at least a few concerned with that harm; those are the only intended readers. ==Reading authors saying that democracy or growth can be bad does not dissuade support of the plan==. The state can use the plan as a global model for democracy, while recognizing it as bad in some places to not use it there. Even if economy shifts to minimal sustainability instead of growth, ==that mindset would still value most plans as necessary short-term== and to avoid war risk, as Linklater (1990) explains. “==Turning” an impact value of concern does not work in real-world persuasion== like debate misteaches: a few quotes counter to mainstream values means readers would see society has many views and apply those to exceptions. ==Even Marxists would understand using appeals to capitalist gain== to gain support for a sensible policy ==to save lives now from poverty and war. “Ethicists and political philosophers vote no more often than other professors==. \[…\] A certain amount of philosophical moral reflection proves beneficial while too much proves harmful” (Schwitzgebel & Rust 2010). This statistic compels us to presume ==critical theorists would support a plan for survival== and would not want to be blamed for their theories being used to delay an practical response to a crisis by advocating for Nirvana. Negatives try to manipulate via a decision-frame that if a reader agrees with their quotes criticizing our society and the state, that implies they’d reject discussing listening to what the government should do. These critics are being contrived as if preclusive of discussing what society should support. ==The permutation cannot “co-opt” and simply shows readers would see the causes as unrelated==.
