1. **Feasible comparison:** Feaver says ignoring policy implications is intellectual laziness, so keeping one's hands clean is lazy too. The best negative strategy is a specific counterplan or solvency turn with a mindset net benefit. Theory is dangerous when it shields from evidence and serves an end-times vision.
2. **Alternatives need agents:** Rorty shows that alternatives without methods fall back on familiar elite models, and that the abstract best should not be the enemy of the better, so critiques must name a framework and an agent. "Learn deeper issues" can justify anything, and a friend facing a draft or crisis-hotline decision would not be swayed by more theory.
3. **Power requires a referent:** Smith, Kahneman and Ó Tuathail show that policymakers rarely change their implicit theories. Bronner, Boggs and Lobel show that rejection without an institutional referent leaves the condemned mindsets in place, hands the space to reactionary elites and lets fear of co-option co-opt activists, so the permutation speaks the language of power.

1. ==Returning debate to feasible comparison restores its purpose== in organizing citations into topic outlines for effective public persuasion backed by research papers. ==It is lazy to tell others to rethink how to stop war==, while keeping one's hand clean of any problematic system, as Feaver (2001, 178) states: “Ignoring ==the== policy implications of theory ==is== often ==a== sign of intellectual laziness. \[…\] Often, when the policy implications are considered, flaws \[…\] are uncovered. Thus, focusing attention on policy implications should lead to better theorizing.” Negatives must have a harm-specific ==counterplan== or alternative approach to address the harm. ==Specific solvency turns are better than far-out net benefits== and may carry a mindset value net benefit—such as reforming schools in a denormalized or anti-capitalist way. Alternative mindsets to solve a harm can be used when the case is not conceded, but answered with how the plan could make a harm worse. ==Critiques are like counterplans to consult Žižek== and others to let the Holy Ž handle it, ==which forces us to debate for the authors==, who would have to agree given consensus on case specifics. ==Modern critique logic-flow can lead to mental abuse== teaching to reduce people to categories by essentializing them to hold them accountable for associations, such as with Western values. ==Theory becomes dangerous as it immunizes against contrary evidence==, ignores the specific concerns of others as “false consciousness,” and treats violence as acceptable in servitude to an eschatology, like aNirvana after the Final Solution to reject all “corrupt” people, to avoid debates to sustain capitalism until its downfall. People base decisions on ethics in a situation, but ==one cannot speculate which path is ethical== to an absolute ==given== the ==unforeseen==.

2. To be a complete argument in their opening speech, ==critique== teams ==should specify the== decision frame for analysis ==and the agent== that should adopt their values: “==Ideas of== political emancipation ==will== necessarily remain abstract without an institutional referent. \[…\] Without a willingness to envision ideas in action, political theory loses its ability to fashion meaningful distinctions between institutions or to formulate ==the== complex struggles facing the exploited" (Bronner 1999, 303). “Emancipatory ideas ==are== hardly likely to convince their target audience (whoever they might be) that progressive change is not only desirable but also plausible and achievable, and therefore worth the effort or risk of trying to secure. Thus, for both epistemological and purely instrumental reasons, concrete utopias must be based on practices that have some basis in preexisting behavior” (Wyn Jones 1999, 77). “We ==should not let the abstractly described best be the enemy of the better==. We should not let speculation about a totally changed system, and a totally different way of thinking about human life and human affairs, replace step-by-step reform” (Rorty 1998, 105). One can infer that critical theorists imply advocating that the general public and leaders facing this issue should base their approach on critique’s rethinking. ==The critic's best answer is that it is helpful to depart== also ==knowing deeper issues==. Some justify adding any philosophy topic as it expands education, but ==such ad-hoc self-justifying logic can apply to anything==, like saying we must first learn all sciences and majors. But ==we learn to deeply scrutinize neither issue==, when instead of depth, we radicalize opposition to common sense policy in ways their authors would cringe at. ==Critics cannot make up framework tricks== teaching the judge to think instead via the perspective of another debate on a related subject. ==This teaches manipulating discussions into political filibustering. Critical theorists would encourage readers to reconceptualize mainstream articles but still support the overall thesis==. Debaters would not be able to dissuade a friend reading an article considering a plan to join the war draft or a plan to suicide answered by a suicide hotline that there is more valuable theory to read, and if everyone thought like that problems would be solved. ==Skepticizing a case author's mindset is not enough to ignore a risk==. There's ==no reason to use a ballot to avoid supporting a plan to survive==, then in-round discussion is better to enrich mindsets. ==Their own authors would reject applying their theories as an excuse== based on a made up rule by early debaters to let the judge vote on just that if they endorse it as more meaningful activism. ==Evaluating a supposed outcome allows weighing only feasible opportunity costs== and intrinsic harm in a decision paradigm. ==Their authors would want all sides to discuss theory then also to resolve the case issue==, so their theory seems relevant if we enter that field. ==Negatives dodge answering the plan with a framework of philosophic competition==. But ==forcing us to defend the opposite of their ideas is ad-hoc self-justifying== and any other social change would work likewise. It justifies turning any real world debate into optimism vs. pessimism or quantum vs known physics. This fails to teach to negate from a thesis, and teaches to change the topic and to argue learning about it outweighs. ==This is a sales tactic to turn people into cynics about social change== and appeal to what personal ethic they’d rather be seen as projecting. ==We can always learn philosophy outside==, there is no reason to reject learning about the case and its intrinsic opportunity costs. That’s the only way to unlock clash and motivate deep research in any field, which later transfers to both policy and philosophy education. ==Critics cannot pick an assumption in isolation that does not dissuade from the overall thesis==: that the judge should decide if the intended readers would still support the thesis.

3. ==Rejection without an institutional== agent ==leaves in place the mindsets it condemns==. If the critic advocates changing how people think, then ==the permutation makes== it ==more likely to succeed== by speaking the language of power to persuade via specific approaches and by refining a proposal that accounts for concerns via crowdsourced outlines. ==Calls to rethink fall on deaf ears when policymakers "are not listening for new ideas==, but instead are looking for those approaches that support their preconceptions. \[…\] Empirical evidence suggests that once in government, ==policy-makers do not change their implicit theories== about the world" (Smith 1997, 512). “While theoretical debates at academic conferences are important to academics, the discourse and concerns of foreign-policy decisionmakers are quite different, so different that they constitute a distinctive problemsolving, theory-averse, policy-making subculture. ==There is a danger that academics assume that the discourses they engage are more significant in the practice of foreign policy==” (Ó Tuathail 1996). Telling people to reject mainstream political mindsets leaves those mindsets in place by choking the space for comparing detailed solutions in context: "==The false sense of empowerment== that comes with such mesmerizing impulses ==is accompanied by a loss of public engagement \[…\] Urgent problems== that are destroying the fabric of American society \[…\] cannot be understood outside the larger social and global context \[…\] The fate of the world hangs in the balance. \[…\] The space ==abdicated by a broad citizenry==, well-informed and ready to participate at many levels, ==can in fact be filled by authoritarian and reactionary elites==” (Boggs 1997). ==Fear of cooption by the system is== a totalizing representation that “has effects that ironically mirror those of cooptation itself \[…\] Opting out of ==the legal arena becomes self-defeating== as it discounts \[…\] the blurring of boundaries between private and public spheres. \[…\] The division of society into different spheres in fact have worked to subvert rather than support the progressive agenda” (Lobel 2007). Reject thinking of there such a thing as “the system” to reject and look to intersectional specifics to avoid justifying authoritarian elites.
