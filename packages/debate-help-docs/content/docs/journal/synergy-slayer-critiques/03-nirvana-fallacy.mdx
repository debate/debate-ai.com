1. **Comparative burden:** Hazlett's Nirvana fallacy and Wyn Jones require comparing proposals to feasible alternatives rather than idealized worlds, so the negative must prove its skepticism discredits the advocacy's facts and that its author endorses categorical rejection. Tetlock shows that policy tradeoffs turn on contested values, and critique scholarship grows coalitions only by persuading of overlooked solutions.
2. **Fiat is a should-value:** Fiat is a should-value, not roleplay, so wishing for a world without capitalism is not an opportunity cost of the plan, just as vegans would not oppose food aid. Feaver supports reading critiques as case takeouts, mindsets change one reader at a time, critiques have no in-round impact, and activism that replaces comparison amounts to bribing the judge.
3. **Burden to answer harms:** A critique that sets aside conceded impacts owes the people facing those harms an explanation. Speice and Lyle note that critiques get lower burdens despite research tradeoffs, so the negative must show a feasible opportunity cost. Telling poor communities to overthrow capitalism first is insulting, "Nazis planned too" would defeat any plan, giving credit to whoever reads first is arbitrary, and mooting case research devalues debate.
4. **Anti-fiat distrust and time skew:** Critics exploit distrust of bureaucrats through truisms like "fiat is not real," the same pattern Arendt saw in revolutionaries. The affirmative need only show that the alternative has not made the plan undesirable to the critique's own followers, which offsets the negative's side bias and time skew, since otherwise the critique wins whether the pro or the con side wins.
5. **No global mindset fiat:** Debaters turn quotes into false oppositions, but changing a mindset is not fiat, because theorists write abstractly for niche audiences and expect competing mindsets to persist. Ethical questions belong in topic papers, like a House committee's question areas, debate stops being switch-side if anti-capitalists never argue the other side, and treating a neg ballot as "rejecting capitalism" is like handing out a coupon for Che Guevara t-shirts.
