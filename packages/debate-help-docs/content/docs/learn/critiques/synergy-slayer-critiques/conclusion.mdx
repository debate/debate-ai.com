Critics commit the Nirvana and causal reductionism fallacies by comparing real proposals to idealized mindsets for dismantling the broader system, instead of feasible alternatives. Judges find it easy to vote for making the world more ethical while trading off with persuasive research to those suffering. It is undebatable that mindsets could be more perfect, but either mindset compares feasible solutions. Their quotes are credibility indictments, but treating them as preclusive gateways violates equal epistemic standing and twists hasty generalizations to excuse inaction. Letting one claim preclude all else teaches prejudice, justifying extremist mindsets that risk collective survival. The permutation uses the plan as common ground a comparison can run across, rather than a stake to be defended on any single value system's terms. On a final note, Some may object that a former competitor is a biased author, but practitioners are the ones who see how critical theory is applied in rounds, a gap the critique literature itself rarely assesses. Critique journals don’t assume the hypocrisy of how teams apply them in rounds to preclude policy analysis. Why don't we email your theorists to ask if they’d rather categorically reject debating mainstream publications which fail to name-drop them, or if they’d sit on the committee to guide solving that harm?
