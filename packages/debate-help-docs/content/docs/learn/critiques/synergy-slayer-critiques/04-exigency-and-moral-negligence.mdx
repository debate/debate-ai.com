1. **Burden to answer harms:** A critique that sets aside conceded impacts owes the people facing those harms an explanation. Speice and Lyle note that critiques get lower burdens despite research tradeoffs, so the negative must show a feasible opportunity cost. Telling poor communities to overthrow capitalism first is insulting, "Nazis planned too" would defeat any plan, giving credit to whoever reads first is arbitrary, and mooting case research devalues debate.
2. **Anti-fiat distrust and time skew:** Critics exploit distrust of bureaucrats through truisms like "fiat is not real," the same pattern Arendt saw in revolutionaries. The affirmative need only show that the alternative has not made the plan undesirable to the critique's own followers, which offsets the negative's side bias and time skew, since otherwise the critique wins whether the pro or the con side wins.

1. ==A critique that asks the judge to set aside conceded case impacts owes an explanation== why those facing the harms should not weigh them. ==Using a critical framework lets one self-assure as more ethically pure, while choking the policy research that finds real-world solutions==: "There are many ==debaters== who ==have gained employment== with a variety of private, governmental, and international policy institutions ==due in large part to their research skills== \[…\] Delving into one area of research will trade-off with another. \[…\] ==Critiques==, for some reason, ==are given a lower burden of proof== by many, and therefore are seen as having 'the maximum competitive benefit with the least effort.'" (Speice & Lyle, 2003). ==It was the negative's burden to show a feasible opportunity cost—one== that the people dealing with the situation could practically use to improve it. ==Telling poor minorities not to focus on their community's issues== because doing so leaves in place broader oppressive structures, and that they must first overthrow global capitalism and fix all its attendant problems, ==is not a serious counter-proposal but an insult that ignores suffering. Judge paradigms have shifted== gradually, ==like a frog slowly boiled, toward accepting generalized truisms that displace research== on improving U.S. society. It is hard to argue against what is wrong with teaching us to be more ethical, other than the ==critic debaters commoditize that as a cover then look for any petty objection== so as ==to avoid comparative research. That literature field is too small to support a year of debates== so it cannot be that teams win framework that it is educational to learn discourse to win on that alone. ==Any plan can be defeated by saying that Nazis also used calculative planning== mindsets, so vote through the lens of rejecting historical unethicality, as that will result in a better lesson to walk away with to do plans like the case. ==People inescapably think of how to make surroundings useful==, but ==only in debate would one learn the paralysis of critiquing that== by saying “using people as a resource is the calculative mindset the Nazis used.” ==Why should the team that read the quote first get credit? We already knew these assumptions, but their framework kills in-round value impacts for both issues. Articles about broader harms do not== persuade or ==dissuade a general reader from agreeing with a case's== advocated ==thesis== that the public reader should support a policy. ==They're using the illusion of teaching us something deeper to block research== on issues while forcing us to now defend generalization fallacies. ==Critics would always win that learning one truism will make us more ethical== when we become policymakers, so they capture impacts better either if the plan or a better solution passes. ==We should not have to defend the educational value of policy research== in comparison ==against== personal impact of ==any related interesting ethical topic because that is a false dichotomy== and all the authors would support learning both. ==They give up the opportunity to ask a detailed topic question== in that literature instead of contriving so that anyone can find isolated islands of literature. ==Mooting case research devalues part of our lives' purpose==, with less people passionate about a topic.

2. ==Another tactic is to tap into distrust for bureaucrats== while promising overthrow to a whole new system. ==Critics bait judges to amorally ignore cases using common truisms== like “our theory solves the root cause,” “policymakers should learn ethics first” and “fiat is not real.” ==Overpowering critics== to reject a team's speech ==incentivizes finding obscure discursive flaws== to avoid discussing social change, ==contrary to a critical theorist's intent==. Arendt (1970) famously wrote, =="The most radical revolutionary will become a conservative the day after the revolution." Debaters have fallen for this fallacy too long. The critique is a gateway argument== claiming that debating minor issues ignores a flawed assumption that outweighs and precedes everything else we could learn in this round. ==The affirmative need only to show that the alternative has not established why the== advocated ==proposal is undesirable to critique followers==, who would not want to be blamed for delaying the best plan for survival in an emergency. ==This is necessary to offset the negative's side bias== and time skew in the middle-game, where the negative extends the least-answered of many off-case positions, allowing esoteric criticisms to escape truth-testing if restated in simple terms. ==The judge may vote negative upon showing sufficient dissuasive costs== of supporting the advocated thesis outweigh its intrinsic benefits. ==The critic frames the debate as whose mindset the judge should project onto society== as the only impact, miseducating in how current issues are evaluated with distrust of government. ==The critic twists debate into a best-speech== and affirms a new thesis. ==The alternative mindset would either agree with the plan or find== flaws and ==better solutions==, either way ==the critique wins if either the pro- or con- side wins. Critics would always win that mindsets of the status quo could be better==. In the end, ==all speeches are just sentences==, so we should not draw two circles of sentences two pick from as if people who think by the critique sentences preclude agreement that the case sentences support the best plan for survival. ==It distorts real world comparison== by acting as if projecting a mindset is an opportunity of a proposal or by comparing impacts on a specific issue vs a general value.
