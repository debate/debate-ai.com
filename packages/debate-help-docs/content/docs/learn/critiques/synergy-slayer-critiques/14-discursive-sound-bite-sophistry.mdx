1. **Discourse can displace urgency:** Kidner (2000), Krishna (1993), and Carlin and Levasseur (2001) argue discourse-centered focus can distance people from urgency, violence, and practical problem-solving, so a discursive flaw is not automatically an impact and the critic must prove the wording makes it worse.
2. **One flaw does not erase the case:** One discursive flaw cannot decide a round because meaningful debate requires multiple warranted claims; Eisenberg’s (2009) “minor amendments” contract-law analogy shows that small wording defects do not void an agreement, rephrasing-wins turns debate into a best-speech contest, and nitpicking alienates debaters and drives researchers away.
3. **Negative burden and broader persuasion:** Critiques are difficult to research in-round, so the neg bears the burden; Berkun (2005) warns rhetorical tricks substitute form for substance, no author proves one statement precludes a whole case, discourse authors usually seek improvement rather than rejection, and a plan supportable under both mindsets reaches more people.
4. **Perspective Synergy counter-critique:** Perspective Synergy can be read as a counter-critique card, calling critiques “Ks” is like second-hand smoke, De Vogli finds unfairness harms health, calling it a “Q” would clarify that changing the topic requires consent, and the Star Wars point is that jargon should not become used like “the force” that converts a policy round into metaphysics.

1. ==The harm is to assume one can simply cherry-pick a problematic discursive mindset and win== just on that. ==That has never been== and never will be ==how real-world debate works==. One generally needs dozens of claims extended to scrutinize assumptions. ==Many policies may have an unethical tinge, and that is weighed== in relation. Their authors only want to be used not in isolation but as part of case debate to distrust authors using the label “terrorist.” We should emulate the "minor amendments" clause under contract law: ==discursive mistakes that are not central to the substance may not affect enforceability== (Eisenberg, 2009). ==If the negative can win just by== advocating ==rephrasing discourse, then debate will become a best-speech contest==, and the negative can win by translating to promote multi-culture. =="Study of discourse enables one to stand aside from issues and avoid any commitment== to a cause or ideal, simply presenting all sides of a debate and pointing out the discursive strategies involved. "Their preoccupation with representation, sign systems, and with the signifier over the signified, leaves one with little sense of the annihilation visited upon the people and land of Iraq. And, as the Vietnam War proved and Schwarzkopf well realized, ==without that physicalistic sense of violence, war can be more effectively sold== to a jingoistic public." (Krishna, 1993, p. 399). Kurtz & Oscarson (2003) show rejecting discourse worsens its violent power: “To think of words as having an ‘open’ future is to recognize that their authority lies less in their historical than in their present uses; it is to acknowledge that people can revise the meaning of words [...] For when we choose not to use offensive words under any circumstance, we preserve their existing meanings as well as their power to injure.” Boggs holds that vital issues “cannot be understood outside the larger social and global context,” so ==their critical authors would want discourse examined alongside policy analysis==, such as calls to distrust securitized impacts in tabloid news. ==It== falsely conflates to project rejecting a flawed mindset as affecting broader change, when it ==forecloses space for how to persuade change==.
