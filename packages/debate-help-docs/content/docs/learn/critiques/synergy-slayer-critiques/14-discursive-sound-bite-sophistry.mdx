1. **One flaw does not erase the case:** Roskoski and Peabody show that voting against offensive language pushes attitudes elsewhere rather than changing them, so nitpicking polarizes and hands politics to the far right. One discursive flaw cannot decide a round, much as Eisenberg's "minor amendments" principle keeps contracts enforceable, and Kidner and Krishna show that focusing on discourse can drain urgency and hide real violence.
2. **Avoiding words preserves their power:** Kurtz and Oscarson show that avoiding offensive words keeps their power to injure, Carlin and Levasseur show that cynical rhetoric ends policy debate, and Boggs wants discourse examined alongside policy. Isolated word critiques therefore teach petty offense-taking, drive researchers away and act as selectively enforced viewpoint policing.
3. **Negative burden:** Because critiques cannot be researched in-round, the negative must prove its indictment applies to this advocacy. Berkun shows how rhetorical tricks defend the ridiculous, no author says one claim precludes a whole case, discourse authors seek improvement rather than rejection, and a plan that works under both mindsets reaches more readers, whereas inflating any phrase into grounds to ignore everything breeds exclusion.
4. **Perspective Synergy counter-critique:** Perspective Synergy works as a counter-critique that endorses both the case and the critique while ruling out "Special K" tactics, which act on students' thinking like second-hand smoke (De Vogli). Veracini's case for lowercase key terms supports dropping the capital K, and changing the topic to a team's discourse should need both teams' consent, like any change to the resolution.

Kurtz & Oscarson (2003) show rejecting discourse worsens its violent power: “To think of words as having an ‘open’ future is to recognize that their authority lies less in their historical than in their present uses; it is to acknowledge that people can revise the meaning of words [...] For when we choose not to use offensive words under any circumstance, we preserve their existing meanings as well as their power to injure.” Debate is still a game, and ==it skews the game if critiques are twisted to not answer feasible plans: "rather than discuss the actual costs or benefits== of Dole's tax plan, ==citizens instead focused upon the rhetorical viability== [...] There is no point discussing the issue of tax cuts if tax cuts will never take place. Essentially, when citizens offer cynical statements during public policy discussions, they effectively bring an end to the public policy debate." (Carlin & Levasseur, 2001). Boggs holds that vital issues “cannot be understood outside the larger social and global context,” so ==their critical authors would want discourse examined alongside policy analysis==, such as calls to distrust securitized impacts in tabloid news.
