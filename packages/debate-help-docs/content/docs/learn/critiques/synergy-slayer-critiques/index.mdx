**Abstract.** In competitive debate formats, critiques of systemic mindsets are misapplied to preclude debating a supposed outcome. This paper corrects how these quotes should have been applied from the start. Critics quote philosophical positions in false juxtaposition to create a false dichotomy between supporting a supposed outcome and a scholarship credibility reduction. A critique that identifies broader oppressive structures cannot demand that the affirmative ‘rethink first’ unless it offers a feasible alternative that readers facing the harm could actually use. First, all quoted evidence — including critical theory — functions as a lens, not a gateway; each debater decides what to take from it. Second, a generalized 'the system is doomed' claim does not warrant rejecting concrete action; risk of systemic failure is not a certainty of it. Finally, critical reflection and policy testing proceed together, because acting on a proposal is how its assumptions get verified.

## Outline: Sections & Paragraph Extensions

This paper presents a case against the misuse of critical theory in competitive debate. It begins with a reasonable-reader standard for judging debates, then argues that critiques lack special epistemic priority, often rely on unattainable alternatives, and substitute rhetorical devices—such as fiat projection, root-cause claims, and discursive nitpicking—for substantive engagement. The latter sections propose pluralism and the permutation as tests of whether a critique truly excludes the affirmative plan, and conclude that purity politics and activist framing can undermine persuasion. The outline maps the paper’s five parts; readers can use it to locate the fuller analysis, and summary extensions for each section.
