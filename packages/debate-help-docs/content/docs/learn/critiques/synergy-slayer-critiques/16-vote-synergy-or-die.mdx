1. Albert argues that ==the left must move beyond criticizing oppression to build hope== through immediate reforms, credible alternatives, and stop blocking the affirmative from joining the movement. “In the absence of insight into future possibilities, ==growing awareness of the scale of oppressions== and their tenacity ==breeds cynicism and not resistance== […] Oglesby […] railed at liberals and named imperialism, not just bad policy, as the real culprit in Vietnam […] ==It is as if to celebrate progress, or to even admit progress, is to somehow negate our critiques==. […] ==It won’t do to say that everyone will be better after the revolution. People need better conditions now==, on moral grounds and also to have any faith in future prospects. […] ==Non reformist reforms==, he said, are gains in the way people live, in laws, in structures, in consciousness, in our own organization, which ==improve peoples lives but also create a new platform from which to fight== for still further […] ==People need to deeply believe that you can fight city hall and win==, and that what you do win won’t be more of the old crap […] \[T\]he left is largely still trying to convince the public that all is not well with society and that the cause of problems lies in ‘the system’ and its institutions. Yet, by and large ==the public knows this already== […] \[A\]lternative institutions […] provide models to instruct our critiques and our strategies, then presumably they have to address racial and sexual and gender and power and also class hierarchies […] But to attract and retain people we also have to ask do our projects, movements, and organizations, and ==does our language== and our personal style lend itself to new people, ==welcome them==, respect them, comfort them, fulfill ==them—or does it shock them==, denigrate them, literally block them from coming toward us or sticking with us?” (Albert, 1998).

2. ==We should never fracture support for sensible policies based on whose \-ism is truer: "A better strategy is to promote agonistic== \[…\] ==selective coalitions on specific political issues==. \[…\] ==The specter haunting the Left today is an unattractive set of internal struggles for hegemony== in which each party calls the other names that give ammunition to the cultural Right. \[…\] \[S\]ynergy on the Left, then, may reside in […] ==letting go of the fantasy of defining a hegemonic position== to which all true leftists must subscribe. ==The need today is==, rather, ==construction of a series of alliances across multiple lines of difference== in assumption, priority, ethical source, and modes of politics. As we establish relations of agonistic respect across differences, we can also hope to strengthen our hand in the academy and improve communications with sympathetic parties in the media, churches, street movements, and labor groups." (Connolly, 1999). Even if mindsets are mutually-exclusive in theory, ==in practice that means creating a "greater whole than the sum of its parts"== to unite society better by adding a third element to resolve the tension of that exclusivity, which is the starting point for their movement. ==Pluralist society allows defending many justifications for advocacies, even if self-contradictory: "Antiwar generals, corporation executives fostering workers' control and top politicians promoting local self-reliance play a useful role== in efforts for social change, especially to the extent that they work with social movements and refuse to play all the 'rules of the game'" (Martin, 1990). =="With very rare exceptions, the right things are done for the wrong reasons==. It is futile to demand that" our leaders or activists "do the right thing for the right reason"; instead, "the organizer should know and accept that the right reason is only introduced as a moral rationalization after the right end has been achieved" and in order to persuade state opposition one =="should search for and use the wrong reasons to achieve the right goals"== (Alinsky, 1971, p. 66). Bisk (2002) warns “if humanity is to survive and have a meaningful existence, then the intellectual project of the twenty-first century must be to move from Postmodernism to Neo-Modernism […] ==to once again instill human society with purpose==, rejuvenating human culture around a coherent, yet pluralistic, framework of ideals.”. The case is trying to convince one-at-a-time that society should change in some way, but that same literature field often advocates ethical movements, business, and international engagement. ==The affirmative retains the ability to support the others== in a general sense by advocating their solution unrelated to the plan, without claiming benefits this round. ==The permutation does not “co-opt”== using the movement ==when it simply shows the public can see the causes as unrelated==.

3. ==Their critique is already being read and applied in society==: state leaders, scholars, media analysts, and diplomats collaborate to check assumptions of other mindsets. For example on security rhetoric, ==scholars would not want to be used to imply that case impacts are accounted for== as constructed threats so as to ignore statistics on escalating casualties in a region. ==Leaders conducting accurate threat assessment would look to detailed facts== and already know from history the cliche to avoid demonizing another as an apocalyptic enemy. ==Legislation passes when a party builds coalitions across mindsets== using two-faced contradictions (Malhotra, 2015). One can appeal to those who think immigration can help or hurt jobs, support or oppose free trade, or those who use race, class, or gender as starting points. ==One can agree on capitalism’s flaws== and inequalities, ==but still find some value in innovation==. So ==pretending to put first the debate over if capitalism has been used more for good or for bad is a meaningless oversimplification==, and it forces one to look to the specifics in a situation. ==Reading authors saying that democracy or economic growth can be bad does not dissuade support of the plan==. The state can use the plan as a global model for democracy, while recognizing it as bad in some places to not use it there. Even if economic planning shifts to minimal sustainability instead of industrial growth, ==that mindset would still value most plans as necessary short-term== and to avoid war and safety risk. ==“Turning” an impact value of concern does not work in real-world persuasion== like debate misteaches: simply introducing a few quotes of those counter to mainstream values just means leaders would see society has many views. ==Even Marxists would understand using appeals to capitalist gain== to gain support for a sensible policy ==to save lives now from poverty and war==.

4. The permutation asks one question: ==could a reader who fully accepts the critique still support the plan? If so, the critique is a lens rather than a reason to vote negative. Think of the case as an article for a concerned reader within the critique's journal==. The test is simply this: starting from that movement, would we still be able to have the affirmative's proposal as one of a hundred issues in applying theory? ==The affirmative is already one compartmentalized debate== within the broader starting point of the debate movement that assumes many philosophies with research sharing. ==The permutation justifies the plan as a concrete proposal the critique's movement could itself adopt== — and as material to refine the policies any post-transformation order would still have to decide. ==Even if our authors use problematic discourse== or appeal for help from inherently flawed systems, ==that is only because that is all that currently exists==. They are not assuming immediate implementation, but just generally advocate for the policy's intrinsic merits in any society's presumed mindset. ==Rejecting the system does not mean rejecting any action advocated to the system==, rather it means to reject the status quo system by researching detailed solutions to a broader goal. ==Critics cannot tell us to rethink if there's any better solutions in their literature; it was their research burden==. Despite the critic's vagueness, ==the alternative is a fiat-based advocacy of an imagined action by an unnamed agent==: usually scholars calling on the public and leaders dealing with this issue to base decisions on an alternative mindset. The question is whether advocating the plan requires abandoning that mindset, or whether one can justify it as appealing under both. ==The permutation resolves the difference to a synergy== where the House committee includes the critique authors to ensure officials and the news explain facts to the public via the right mindset. For example, ==Israel's government could agree that all buildings should have bomb shelters and that public discourse on terrorism should not use constructed threats==. The permutation as one discussion on a news broadcast brings in contrasting mindsets so they can synergize and teach each other when to apply the theory in the specific situation. ==We can only find use in a critical theory if balanced with facts== in a situation's perspective. ==The very purpose of debate topics has been to regulate society’s systemic flaws==. Rejecting debate to use as a starting point examination of ontology, biopolitics, security, and capitalism is like coming to a U.S. & E.U. forum on how nations can get Iran to denuclearize, and then ==fiating the Iranian state denuclearize to win every debate. The scope of negative fiat is limited to what is persuasive to the intended audience==, and forecloses that which would always defeat the discussion. The appeal to “the case issues can always be betters if we rejected the flaws of capitalist society” or “rethink healthcare so biopolitical control doesn’t go wrong” ==would always win and thus preclude the discussion actually needed== to know how to overcome common opposition.
