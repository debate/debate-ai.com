1. **Case comparison already tests assumptions:** The claim that debate needs critiques to test assumptions is backwards because ordinary comparison through cases, disadvantages, counterplans, and solvency already does that work; Foucault (1972, 1988) supports comparison as interrogation, proper critique use is researched counterplans and indicts, and origin of the “K” does not permit a free-floating alternative.
2. **Eve-and-the-apple warrant:** The critic resembles the serpent promising Eve that judges’ “eyes shall be opened” to a higher but unknowable truth, asking them to abandon a concrete harm-avoiding plan for speculative enlightenment; simulated-universe and dreaming digressions reveal the problem, because once common evidence is abandoned, critics can always assert a deeper inaccessible reality.
3. **Logic of evil:** Critiques often conceal a logic of evil by cherry-picking quotations, demanding rejection of the old system, and imposing a good-versus-evil binary that cannot be weighed against practical action; this makes “the cynic always right,” so we should set a precedent against sophistry.
4. **Personal and historical offense:** The author’s experience losing to claims that life has “no value under capitalism,” family history under communism, and a bitter personal attack by a named former opponent demonstrate why totalizing anti-capitalist rhetoric is not merely academic; Bodnick (2023) says critiques radicalize students, the Goebbels comparison illustrates how repeated absolutism normalizes extremity, and Berman (2020) warns anti-system rhetoric can echo Nazi patterns
