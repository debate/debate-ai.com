# Outline: Parts, Sections & Paragraph Extensions

Roadmap: Part I outlines the decision-making standard for debate; Part II addresses constraints on negative fiat and opportunity costs; Part III examines defending general process, state action, and consultation; Part IV analyzes activism, persuasion, identity, and role models.

1. **The Standard for Debate Decision-Making**  
   1. **Intrinsic opportunity cost and the concerned reader:** Evidence only means what it meant to the reader it was written for. The ballot asks if that reader still backs the plan. Congress considers policies serially, so tradeoffs are not intrinsic. Debaters cannot price intervening actors. Only values, feasibility, and solvency turns are reasons to reject.
   2. **Town hall of researched solutions:** Better fiat: the winning outline reaches media, lobbyists, and committees. Tournaments become open-source conferences on a live problem. Quals, underlining, and full text make rounds peer review. The shared purpose rewards finding game-changing articles, not hiding from testing.
   3. **Ethical grounds for projecting a choice:** Projecting an advocacy is a commitment, not wizardry. Sartre: choosing for oneself chooses for all. Kant adds the stricter test of universalizable maxims. Policy implications expose flaws in theory. Enough eyeballs make bugs shallow. Students should advocate as authors, not strawperson institutions.
   4. **Failure of scenario forecasting:** Tetlock shows expert forecasts fail because politics is value-driven. Assigned risk is invented, not predicted. Fiat is not a movie you pick by preference. A reader sold on clean water answers a shortage claim by phasing in, not by abandoning the should. Permutations test value compatibility first.
2. **Constraints on Negative Fiat and Opportunity Costs**  
   1. **The Batterman standard for fiat:** Fiat has two prongs from Solt. The actor must be one the public could support. The proposal must appear in the topic literature against the aff's harm. Fail either and it is a non sequitur, answered by further action the decider controls. The fifty-state counterplan fails on prong two. The aff accepts the same limit on advantages.
   2. **Alternative causes justify mitigation:** Gostin and Wetter say ethical policymaking confronts tradeoffs, not that it abandons policies. Fossil fuel job loss gets retraining. Trade strain gets reallocation. The plan survives; supplements absorb the harm. No author assumes instant passage. Politics is one tradeoff among hundreds.
   3. **Infinite speculative risk blocks action:** If any hypothetical counts, nothing passes and research dies. Intrinsic alternatives actually move deciders. Advisors could have offered Truman oil strikes, serving his aim of showing will-to-use. Extrinsic pleas hardened him. Gvosdev's Bosnia shows objection with no alternative risking mass death. Presumption favors the plan.
   4. **Extinction turns are not intrinsic:** The permutation tests whether both values can be held. Voluntary extinction self-refutes: the movement dies with its advocates. Conceding extinction is good is reverse causality that persuades no one. Judges should infer author intent, not count contrived quotes. No one can be certain a small nuclear war stays small.
   5. **Practice challenge, 2022 NDT final:** Contrived tradeoffs produce warming good, Canada stagnates, Quebec secedes, WWIII. Matthews says a river dispute triggers the same chain. He advocates closer US–Canada ties. Agreeing with him does not preclude the plan. Readers presume other economic options. Ask authors what detail wins their support.
3. **Opportunities Given Up Defending the General Process**  
   1. **Defending the process, not an instant:** The aff defends what follows "Resolved." Teams cannot redefine it into tests of immediacy or certainty. Support is gathered now, when the judge is resolved. Supporting in principle does not forfeit soliciting support elsewhere. Delegation, NATO, or revolution just relocates the debate. Mooting into another literature base ends in-depth comparison.
   2. **Public resolution outweighs symbolic consultation:** Readers endorse the plan on the merits and treat NATO approval as a desirable source of legitimacy and burden-sharing—not a binding veto; even if NATO rejects, policymakers still face pressure to address the harm through a revised coalition, contingency, or framing.
   3. **Uniform state action is federal action:** Shapiro shows compacts get upgraded to federal law and need congressional consent. Manne shows big states compel nationwide compliance. The Commerce Clause makes federal response a responsibility. Divergent caselaw and budgets guarantee patchwork. Uniformity cannot be “fiated” any more than universal kindness. Territories concede federal action.
   4. **Permutation and inevitable coordination:** The state counterplan opens the floodgates on nearly every plan. The position is self-contradictory: the agenda is too full, yet its own logic delegates the issues politics depends on. Readers holding both value sets just ask leaders to schedule around the cost. The permutation preserves federal agencies. Apparent one-sidedness only reflects that fifty states have never acted in unison.
   5. **Distortion of the literature base:** Galloway shows the counterplan thrives by distorting the literature answering it. Nuclear, transportation, and federal lands policy all got “Lopez'd” back. Arbitrary reassignment collapses federalism into a free-for-all. Modeling needs stability. No author writes against scenarios nobody advocates. Domestic topics get choked off under contrived comparisons.
4. **Activism Forecloses Persuasion**  
   1. **The enemy of progress:** Outrage-driven rejection and cancel culture suppress honest dialogue, mask the critic’s own biases, and give up the evidence-tested engagement that actually changes unexamined prejudice.
   2. **Debate is already shifting (Fig. 18):** A resolution requiring activist plans, limited to intrinsic opportunity costs, would test activism on its merits, while moralized name-calling feeds right-wing backlash (Connolly, 1999; Žižek, 2024) and ignores that no one can step outside society to “reject” it.
   3. **There is great harm:** Labeling dislike as racism enforces forced politeness and turns the debate community into an echo chamber that drives out contrarians, as the forum reaction to Ryan Wash’s 2013 win showed.
   4. **Racism vs. achievement disparity:** Treating disparities as proof of racism promotes collective guilt and essentialism, and overusing the “racist” label dilutes it and produces a ballot that changes nothing.
   5. **Why the first of each demographic (Fig. 19):** Students should take inspiration from leaders’ character and strategy rather than matching appearance, since affirmative action and identity matching can reinforce perceptions of lower capability (Leslie, 2014).
   6. **Reading “white people”:** Group labels entrench stigmas like “acting white” (Arora, 2022) and depress aspiration (Hughes, 2024), while the Norfolk Prison debaters show that mastering rigorous persuasion empowers more than symbolic rejection.
   7. **Anti-racist authors would want (Fig. 20):** Minority leaders should learn persuasion through research and red-teaming, because treating every disparity as racism creates a “racism treadmill” that hides real progress (Hughes, 2018, 2020).
   8. **McWhorter (Fig. 21):** Portraying racism or capitalism as totalizing diminishes agency and breeds cynicism, so movements need concrete reforms and credible alternatives that welcome newcomers (McWhorter, 2020; Gibson-Graham, 1996; Albert, 1998).
   9. **We all face intersectional oppression:** Clash and comparison are the point of debate, and reading anti-systemic arguments in rounds disproves the claim that debate is a closed system, so “vote to reject oppression” ballots only trade off with education.
   10. **Blacks can choose their topics:** Mikolajcik (2018) argues forensics stays structured by whiteness, but the author counters that competition should be played for its own sake (Villa, 1996) rather than decided by judges’ activism.
   11. **We are not pretending to roleplay:** Debate already shapes peers and public opinion, so fiat becomes real through coalition-building and shared humor, as Richard Pryor’s comedy brought audiences to confront stereotypes together.
   12. **This critique aims to disrupt:** Liberal fear of criticism suppresses honest engagement and locks in prejudice, and African Americans are urged to lead informed solidarity with African communities.
   13. **Pessimist or optimist:** Evidence-based debate is more accessible and less traumatizing for many marginalized participants (Huber, 2016), and local symbolic wins can shift burdens onto others (Katz, 2000), so debaters should pair awareness with solution comparison and broad strategic skill.
