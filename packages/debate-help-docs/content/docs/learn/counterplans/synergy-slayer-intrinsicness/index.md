**Abstract.** This paper advances an intrinsic opportunity-cost framework of the concerned reader. Evidence should be read in its original persuasive context: whether a reasonable reader would conclude that society should adopt the plan, not whether a judge can imagine every downstream consequence of immediate implementation. The affirmative must establish value, feasibility, and issue-specific reasons to support the plan. The negative should win only by proving an intrinsic, evidenced tradeoff: a feasible alternative or harm the plan necessarily sacrifices within the same controversy. Remote hypotheticals, speculative scenario chains, and arbitrary institutional shifts do not disprove the plan because policymakers can mitigate secondary harms through sequencing, complementary policies, and later action. State counterplans assume action the literature doesn’t support, and their logic leads back to federal coordination. The same standard applies to activist performances that ask judges to reject opponents as complicit in systemic racism: a ballot that merely affirms the truism that racism is wrong offers no plan, no evidenced comparison, and no change beyond the round. Such strategies replace research with outrage, essentialize participants by group identity, and foreclose the dialogue and coalition-building that reducing prejudice actually requires. Judges should prioritize the values and comparative claims in the relevant policy literature, preserving debate’s purpose of rigorous, topic-specific research and public persuasion.
