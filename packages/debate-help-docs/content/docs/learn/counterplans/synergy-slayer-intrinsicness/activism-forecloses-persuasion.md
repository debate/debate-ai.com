# Activism Forecloses Persuasion

*Figure 17 \- Activism forecloses persuasion by elevating ideological purity over dialogue.*

This section argues that activist performance and identity-based critiques in academic debate must be evaluated through the same standards of rigorous research and intrinsic comparison as policy proposals. By prioritizing ideological purity over substantive policy dialogue, activist strategies can disempower debaters, alienate potential allies, and polarize academic discourse. Outrage manipulation and essentialist group framing substitute for evidence-based debate. In response, a research-centered framework values persuasion, intrinsic cost comparison, and coalition-building across differences.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, judges should disregard negative claims that do not establish either intrinsic harms or a feasible opportunity cost grounded in real-world advocacy. Part IV demonstrates that activist claims must meet the same rigorous standards of evidence, warrants, and intrinsic comparison as policy proposals to achieve genuine persuasion. Debate is not a contest between two affirmatives. The negative must prove that the affirmative inherently sacrifices a better, feasible solution that addresses the concerns. Evaluators and participants must rely strictly on the literature base rather than assuming external experts hold exclusive authority. Only debaters are qualified to write how concepts are applied in debate, as no authors assume such contrived use. Debaters may write similar final judgments “slayers” to end repeated generic debates.
