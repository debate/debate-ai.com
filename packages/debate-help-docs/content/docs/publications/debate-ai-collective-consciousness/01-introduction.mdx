==Debate should be a war of warrants where victories are vectorized as weights==, so that ==a crowd-sourced decision-making AI can be publicly stress-tested against competing perspectives.== Critical times call for ==a crowdsourced argument-reasoning dataset== that lets AI models recommend research quotes, evolves chain-of-thought reasoning, unlocks faster reading of long articles, monitors developments through topic-modeled knowledge graphs, and provides a public answer service. ==The central claim is simple: news should be outlined.== Instead of reading the internet as disconnected articles, ==users are able to place claims into a living tree of issues, sub-issues, citations, and impacts.== In this model, ==debate becomes not only a pedagogical activity but also a synergistic structure for social sensemaking.==

==Language models can distill collective thought into a vector space in which each point carries a weighted contribution to decision-making.== Because ==the system exposes which sentences and citations informed an output==, users can inspect whether its reasoning aligns with stated interests. ==Research Agents recommend articles for human researchers working alongside AI to develop a summarized topic outline as a public service==, while monitoring related articles via web searches for keywords associated with that Topic Model. When a user uploads a research paper, ==the system extracts the full text of cited references==, generates topic-model and keyword summaries, monitors the associated literature, and stores highlighted passages. Users could maintain ==Personal Knowledge Bases that record influential sources==, which a future assistant might use to model research interests.

==Debate has traditionally trained users to compare claims, test warrants, and evaluate evidence under adversarial conditions.== Recent work in AI debate and responsible AI in education shows ==growing interest in systems that assist argument generation, evidence review, and ethical reasoning while preserving human oversight== (Wambsganss et al., 2024). This paper extends that trajectory by proposing ==a platform-centered model in which debate workflows become infrastructure for tracking current events==, organizing public research, and aligning AI outputs with transparent evidentiary support. ==Google returns a ranked list of pages, but not a map showing where each article fits within the larger topic structure — it shows documents, not the debate.== A topic-level view allows users to ==see where each article fits into the larger mind map of a topic==, similar to a debate outline's tree headings, ==providing relevant highlights without a narrow ideological bubble mindset.==
