# Papers

==Three papers from the Debate AI Institute, each published as a full web edition with a video overview, a DOI, hover citations and a references list.== The two **Synergy Slayer** papers apply one standard, the *concerned reader*, to the two most common ways negative strategies avoid the plan: critiques that reject it, and counterplans or tradeoffs that are not intrinsic to it. The third paper describes the platform those papers were written on, and argues that debate's structure can scale into a public reasoning system.

## The papers

<Cards>
  <Card
    title="The Synergy Slayer of Critiques in Debate"
    description="Concerned readers consider theory in action to avoid negligent distraction by abstraction. ~37,000 words, 20 sections. Updated October 2026."
    href="/docs/publications/synergy-slayer-critiques"
  >
    Argues that critical theory is misapplied when it is used to preclude debating the plan. Quoted evidence works as a lens, not a gateway; "the system is doomed" is a risk, not a certainty; and a critique cannot demand the affirmative "rethink first" without a feasible alternative. Part I sets the concerned-reader standard, Part II names the logic fallacies (Nirvana fallacy, moral negligence, fiat as solvency), Part III answers the major critiques one by one (capitalism, security, fear of death, Heidegger, biopolitics, Nietzsche, settler colonialism), and Part IV offers the alternative: test theory in action and build a pluralist coalition.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="The Synergy Slayer of Non-Intrinsic Concerns in Debate"
    description="Decision-makers limit consideration to intrinsic opportunity-cost tradeoffs. ~14,000 words, 4 sections. Updated October 2026."
    href="/docs/publications/synergy-slayer-intrinsicness"
  >
    Applies the same standard to counterplans and disadvantages. The negative wins only by proving an intrinsic, evidenced tradeoff that the plan necessarily sacrifices within the same controversy. Remote hypotheticals, speculative scenario chains and arbitrary institutional shifts do not disprove the plan, because policymakers can mitigate secondary harms with sequencing and later action. It shows why the fifty-state counterplan fails the literature test and leads back to federal coordination, and why activist ballots that reject opponents as complicit offer no plan and foreclose persuasion.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="Debate AI Collective Consciousness"
    description="Reimagining the web as an argument mind map is key to crowdsourced AI decision-making. ~6,000 words, 8 sections. Updated September 2026."
    href="/docs/publications/debate-ai-collective-consciousness"
  >
    Introduces Debate-AI.com and its five parts: CARDS, FIAT, LEARN, REASON and STREAM. It proposes the Debate Singularity, a research agenda in which articles, cards and arguments become linked nodes in a shared research tree that supports summarizing, verifying and simulating public decisions. Later sections cover the self-organizing mind map, the word-limit format, rights of consciousness, and DECAP, a model of decision-making by elected community agent personas.
  </Card>
</Cards>

## How they fit together

| Paper | Answers | Core claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Critiques](/docs/publications/synergy-slayer-critiques)** | Kritiks and "reject the system" alternatives | A critique must offer a feasible alternative the affected reader could use |
| **[Non-Intrinsic Concerns](/docs/publications/synergy-slayer-intrinsicness)** | Counterplans, politics and speculative disadvantages | Only intrinsic, evidenced tradeoffs are reasons to reject the plan |
| **[Collective Consciousness](/docs/publications/debate-ai-collective-consciousness)** | Why debate's method matters beyond the round | Crowdsourced, outlined argument trees can support public AI-assisted decisions |
