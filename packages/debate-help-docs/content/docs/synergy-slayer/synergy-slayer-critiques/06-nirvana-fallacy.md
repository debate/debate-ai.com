5. ### **The Nirvana Fallacy and the Feasible-Alternative Requirement**


*Figure 7: Purity politics and hyper-radical demands lead to systemic co-option and lost engagement.*

1. Under opportunity cost theory, the Nirvana logical fallacy states that rational deliberation requires comparing a proposal only against feasible and not "unrealistic, idealized alternatives" in which root causes have been abolished, as that creates a "false dichotomy" blinding us to what can persuade people facing the current harm to action (Hazlett, 2021). This argument logic makes one doubt common sense due to imperfect \-isms, causing you to doubt you can know factual claims exist until you vote negative to do the affirmative through the lens of knowing more about psychoanalysis or ontology. They have the burden to prove their skepticism is sufficient to discredit supporting the facts of the advocacy. Otherwise we mis-educate to distort ethics to prejudice and judge people not based on facts to protect lives but mindsets. The fact that their authors apply criticism to society means they support comparative solutions. We are simply arguing one issue at a time to learn comparative research and proceed from the mindset of the debate community movement which tackles these broader structures with hundreds of cases. Modern debate began with the 1947 national tournament, and annual topics centered around specifics like unions, price controls, and trade, as part of the broader capitalism debate of the era. We have already been living in the alternative. We don't defend just the mindset of just the plan, but comparative research as a means of change on all topics and plans. Critics are in a self-contradictory in-round application projected as "what if every round was like this critic" means one would never learn to research or persuade on details that matter. The negative must show that their author endorses categorical rejection of action, not just scrutiny.

   2. History shows how easily “reject everything not ideologically pure, they misunderstand value to life” justifies war. Critics cannot say that there's only a risk that Marxism can only help enlighten your understanding, because they'd just say Buddhism or any other \-ism next round to avoid the specifics. If we have the opportunity to convince society, classmates, comrades, and institutions with the case then we can also change those mindsets with the critique. We should not teach a false dichotomy of whose call for social change had more impacts as that their authors would instead call on their followers to scrutinize each case quote with specifics informed by the critique to preclude support for the thesis. This is not mere “plan-focus” and allows critique education as is relevant and persuasive without generalizing a prejudice. This is how such critiques should have always been applied, we just confused ourselves by learning to distract on related topics like a “related articles” ad to advocate Nirvana. As Tetlock (2017) explains, political experts can project confidence while still failing to resolve a policy’s real tradeoffs, because those judgments turn on contested values—not merely on technical predictions about what will happen. We can uniquely learn to persuade with detailed case specifics on one topic then cross-apply elsewhere in life but only if we recognize debate means the negative dissuades a thesis via intrinsic costs in that situation. Most students know history or will learn political philosophy elsewhere. Critique scholarship only grows coalitions by persuading interest groups of a specific solution they overlook. Their authors would want cases refuted in theory-aligned ways. 

   3. Fiat is not a live-action roleplaying “Fiat: IRL-MMORPG” video game, but rather these are should-values the reader can conclude upon to see if they would still support the central thesis on an issue. Critical theorists are teaching readers to reconceptualize mainstream discourse, not to reject articles which fail to quote their theory. Negatives cannot ask a real-world reader to ignore their one harm and claim a framework of Marxism vs. capitalism teaches us and solves for the genocidal mentality causing more harm down the road. Reading the critic as case takeouts solves their education better, as Feaver (2001) explains that only refining theory via specifics one can persuade it applies. Critical mindset to scrutinize the case is like rights vs. utilitarianism or “economy is resilient” but one cannot win on that alone. We can only change societal mindsets one reader at a time on a specific concern, so we're just asking the reader to agree that policymakers and society should support a policy and also to improve their mindsets. It is a solipsistic fallacy that the judge or reader gaining this understanding changes the mindsets of relevant actors, whom critics have to persuade in their mindsets. Critiques don't have impacts, those are just reasons to apply the correct theory to improve predictive analysis. Alternatives cannot claim to solve the impacts, as if claiming that rejecting detailed research will lead those dealing with the issue to read ethics and work it all out. There is no in-round impact, since we are just outlining mainstream views and know philosophy as relevant. One can always say it can only help to learn another field of thought. Maybe next they'll claim understanding reality comes first and as a gateway have us do their physics homework. Debaters accepting that critiques always moot discussion gives no purpose to do case research. Critics bribe judges to vote in exchange for activism – more ethical than doing their job of grading a persuasive essay.
