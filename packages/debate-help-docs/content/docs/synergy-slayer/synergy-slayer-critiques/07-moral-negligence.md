6. ### **Reject Mindset Critiques Ignoring Exigency For Moral Negligence**

   1.  A critique that asks the judge to set aside conceded case impacts owes an explanation why those facing the harms should not weigh them. Using a critical framework lets one self-assure as more ethically pure, while choking the policy research that finds real-world solutions: "There are many debaters who have gained employment with a variety of private, governmental, and international policy institutions due in large part to their research skills \[…\] Delving into one area of research will trade-off with another. \[…\] Critiques, for some reason, are given a lower burden of proof by many, and therefore are seen as having 'the maximum competitive benefit with the least effort'" (Speice & Lyle, 2003). It was the negative's burden to show a feasible opportunity cost—one that the people dealing with the situation could practically use to improve it. One never gets that option to tell those suffering to reject considering harms via a more ethical mindset generally, like telling Africans to go vegan and reject colonialist food aid. Telling poor minorities not to focus on their community's issues because doing so leaves in place broader oppressive structures, and that they must first overthrow global capitalism and fix all its attendant problems, is not a serious counter-proposal but an insult that ignores suffering. Debate judge paradigms have been hacked, like a frog slowly boiled, by Soviet propaganda using generalizing truisms to burn the capitalist literature and boycott space for researching how to improve U.S. society.
