9. ### **Selling vs. Debating: Contradicting Values to Support a Solution**

   1. Does it make sense to call it a debate if we ask the judge to impose their subjective opinion of what has more educational impact to be policy bureaucrats or read philosophy? This shifts from debate to comparing sentences in speeches for their impact on personal mindsets and lets judges vicariously project onto students to vote for what path in life to motivate. That is for students to decide and to be true to themselves as we form values via internal reflection, not by scouting out what judges like to hear. The critic came not to debate but to sell something better. Persuasively justifying this sales tactic teaches the finesse of politicians who do not answer the question but shift to ideologize and then reassure us that it will all be handled better. Imagine a critic telling a friend concerned with a real issue that they would see entirely differently and would be better off in the long run if they reflected on deeper systemic injustice. That would not persuade because only in a critique framework can one ignore exigency. This is a way to cajole the judge into thinking their vote can be more meaningful today than a best plan for survival by instead challenging us to defend the side criticized by any ethics journal. In reality, the overpowering of critiques in the debate format incentivizes looking for obscure philosophical rhetoric about society's historical flaws, because those are made to seem ethically superior to evaluating a policy debate. Critical authors may find repugnant to see how their journal articles are used to avoid considering how to help people in mainstream issues. Every round could be like a political activist trying to convince the most cynical philosophy student that demands we first answer for what society has done, as per a hundred schools of thought. Why should debaters rethink mainstream issues through a statistically-niche lens that would often offend mainstream society? The judge can *only* simulate the *intended* mainstream reader, not take the perspective of a cynic who demands that everyone defend all of society's flaws. Even if their obscure theory is correct, few readers would see it as the correct time to apply when reading a news issue. We need to go into society and persuade by common values — not only nuclear risk and ethics – to coalesce literature towards the synergy point of an optimal solution outline. We evolve society *faster* on issues from abortion, to AI, to ending war.
