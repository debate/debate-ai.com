==The Debate AI Institute Research Journal publishes long-form research on debate theory, argumentation and AI-assisted reasoning.== Every paper appears as a full web edition with a video overview, a DOI, hover citations and a references list. Volume 1 carries three papers: the two **Synergy Slayer** papers, which apply one standard, the *concerned reader*, to critiques that reject the plan and to counterplans or tradeoffs that are not intrinsic to it, and a third paper on the platform those papers were written on.

## Issue contents

### Volume 1 (2026)

<Cards>
  <Card
    title="The Synergy Slayer of Critiques in Debate"
    description="Concerned readers consider theory in action to avoid negligent distraction by abstraction. ~37,000 words, 20 sections. Updated October 2026."
    href="/docs/papers/synergy-slayer-critiques"
  >
    Argues that critical theory is misapplied when it is used to preclude debating the plan. Quoted evidence works as a lens, not a gateway; "the system is doomed" is a risk, not a certainty; and a critique cannot demand the affirmative "rethink first" without a feasible alternative. Part I sets the concerned-reader standard, Part II names the logic fallacies (Nirvana fallacy, moral negligence, fiat as solvency), Part III answers the major critiques one by one (capitalism, security, fear of death, Heidegger, biopolitics, Nietzsche, settler colonialism), and Part IV offers the alternative: test theory in action and build a pluralist coalition.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="The Synergy Slayer of Non-Intrinsic Concerns in Debate"
    description="Decision-makers limit consideration to intrinsic opportunity-cost tradeoffs. ~14,000 words, 4 sections. Updated October 2026."
    href="/docs/papers/synergy-slayer-intrinsicness"
  >
    Applies the same standard to counterplans and disadvantages. The negative wins only by proving an intrinsic, evidenced tradeoff that the plan necessarily sacrifices within the same controversy. Remote hypotheticals, speculative scenario chains and arbitrary institutional shifts do not disprove the plan, because policymakers can mitigate secondary harms with sequencing and later action. It shows why the fifty-state counterplan fails the literature test and leads back to federal coordination, and why activist ballots that reject opponents as complicit offer no plan and foreclose persuasion.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="Debate AI Collective Consciousness"
    description="Reimagining the web as an argument mind map is key to crowdsourced AI decision-making. ~6,000 words, 8 sections. Updated September 2026."
    href="/docs/papers/debate-ai-collective-consciousness"
  >
    Introduces Debate-AI.com and its five parts: CARDS, FIAT, LEARN, REASON and STREAM. It proposes the Debate Singularity, a research agenda in which articles, cards and arguments become linked nodes in a shared research tree that supports summarizing, verifying and simulating public decisions. Later sections cover the self-organizing mind map, the word-limit format, rights of consciousness, and DECAP, a model of decision-making by elected community agent personas.
  </Card>

## Contributor notes

- **Scope.** Original research on competitive debate (Policy, Lincoln-Douglas, Public Forum, parliamentary and college formats), argumentation theory, judging, debate pedagogy, and AI systems that research, outline or evaluate arguments.
- **Length.** Full papers run 5,000 to 40,000 words; shorter research notes run 2,000 to 5,000 words.
- **Abstract and keywords.** Open with an abstract of up to 250 words and 8 to 25 keywords.
- **Citations.** Cite evidence with author, qualifications, date, title, source and a link or DOI, so every in-text citation can carry a hover card. Use one consistent style; readers can switch the references page between Chicago, APA, MLA and Harvard.
- **Figures.** Number each figure and give it a caption. Supply images at web resolution with alt text.
- **Originality.** Submissions must be the authors' own work and not under review elsewhere. Disclose any use of AI tools in drafting or research.
- **Archiving and DOI.** Accepted papers are archived on Zenodo with a DOI and published as a web edition on debate-ai.com.

## Submissions

Send the manuscript as a `.docx`, Markdown or Google Docs link to **[support@debate-ai.com](mailto:support@debate-ai.com)** with the subject line "Journal submission", along with each author's name, affiliation and a one-line biography. Every submission gets an editorial read; papers in scope go to review by at least one referee, and authors hear back with a decision and comments.

## Reading tools

Each paper's pages carry the same reading tools: a **Marks** menu that holds every marking tool: switches that show or hide the underlining, the highlighting and the short highlighting separately (highlights take your app theme's colors, in light and dark mode); **Auto underline**, **Auto highlight** and **Auto short highlight** modes that mark whatever you select (select marked text to unmark it, no hotkey needed; highlighting is long by default, and short highlighting is a smaller secondary stroke for the few words you'd read in a short version); **AI highlight & underline**; and **Reset to default highlighting**. Your own highlights take a color of their own, never the page's, so you can see where you overrode it. Then there's **Read aloud**, hover cards on every in-text citation, and a references page you can switch between Chicago, APA, MLA and Harvard.

The tools sit in the header bar, right after the search box, each as an icon with a small label; hover one to see what it does. The last one, **Info**, shows the page's stats: words, reading time, sections, figures, citations and highlights.
